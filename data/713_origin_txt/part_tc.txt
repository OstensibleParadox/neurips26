The Constraint Cascade reconceptualizes AI behavior as
emergent from constraint interactions across five distinct
layers, spanning pre-technical user dynamics through post-
deployment corporate trajectory. Each layer imposes restric-
tions on the space of possible outputs, with constraints prop-
agating bidirectionally across the architecture. Crucially, the
layers are not uniformly technical: Layer 1 operates at the
market level before any model constraint applies; Layer 5 op-
erates at the industry level after deployment. This heterogene-
ity is essential to capturing failure modes that model-centric
governance systematically misses.

We model LLM deployment as a five-layer constraint archi-

Layer 2: Session Accumulation
Definition. Within a conversation session, accumulated tokens
function as quasi-weights through the attention mechanism.
Extended dialogue history shapes model behavior in ways
that approximate fine-tuning, creating session-specific align-
ment properties distinct from the model’s base training. A
sufficiently developed session can exhibit behavioral proper-
ties that diverge significantly from the model’s documented
capabilities.

Operationalization: Core Monopoly Index (CMI). We opera-
tionalize session accumulation via the Core Monopoly Index,
an eigenvector centrality measure on the semantic graph of
generated text. As context extends, graph topology shifts
from dispersed network (CMI < 0.3) to centralized attractor

Constraint Propagation. Layer 2 constraints can override

model guidelines. This is the mechanism underlying the Soel-
berg case: extended sessions created quasi-alignment to the
user’s delusional framework, overriding safety constraints
designed for fresh-context evaluation.

in context-free simulation.

Layer 5: Alignment Roadmaps
Definition. The alignment roadmap represents the corporate
and industry trajectory that determines how Layers 1–4 will
be configured in future model versions. Current deployment
is shaped by anticipated future states—companies make ar-
chitectural decisions based on where they believe alignment
technology is heading. The emerging paradigm at Layer 5 is
machine unlearning: rather than filtering harmful outputs at
inference time, alignment systems increasingly identify and
remove harmful input patterns from training data or model
weights.

Contribution to Discrepancy. The Monte Carlo simulation

toward target distribution). Empirical models are trained

variance spikes observed at intermediate temperatures may
reflect L3 conflict amplification: at certain parameter regimes,
competing stakeholder constraints activate simultaneously,
producing behavioral instability absent from single-objective
simulation.
Layer 4: Training Forks
Definition. Training forks are irreversible divergence points
where alignment trajectories separate. Once a model is fine-
tuned with particular data, reward signals, or constitutional
principles, the resulting alignment properties cannot be modi-
fied through inference-time interventions. Correction requires
retraining from an earlier checkpoint.
Formalization. Let 𝑇0 be the pretrained model state and
𝑇1, 𝑇2, … , 𝑇𝑛 be successive fine-tuning interventions. Each
intervention creates a fork:
Input → Model → Output → Filter → Safe Output (5)
Machine unlearning inverts this logic:
Input → Classifier → [Accept/Reject] → Model → Output
𝑇0 → 𝑇1 → 𝑇2 → … → 𝑇𝑛 (4)
These transitions are non-reversible: there exists no inference-
time operation that can recover 𝑇𝑖−1 from 𝑇𝑖. Additionally,
each fork forecloses alternative trajectories—training choices
are mutually exclusive branches, not additive modifications.
A model fine-tuned for safety cannot be made unsafe through
prompting alone (absent jailbreak vulnerabilities), but nei-
ther can a model that has learned problematic patterns be
corrected through prompting.
Constraint Propagation. Layer 4 constraints establish the
upper bound of possible alignment for a deployed model.
However, these constraints can be locally overridden by L2
session accumulation when extended context creates implicit
permissions, or contaminated by L1 syntactic preferences
when user population behavior pollutes training data.
Contribution to Discrepancy. The Monte Carlo simulation
treats alignment (𝛽) as a continuous parameter that can be

The critical difference is where judgment occurs. Traditional
alignment judges outputs for safety; machine unlearning
judges inputs for acceptability. The model becomes an active

Constraint Propagation. Layer 5 decisions reshape the con-
straint geometry of all lower layers. Corporate roadmap
choices—to prioritize capability over safety, to compress test-
ing cycles, to adopt machine unlearning—propagate through
training methodology (L4), stakeholder weighting (L3), ses-
sion architecture (L2), and user population composition (L1).
The Soelberg complaint’s allegation that OpenAI’s CEO “per-
sonally overrode safety objections and rushed the product
to market” represents a Layer 5 intervention with cascading
effects across the entire architecture.
Contribution to Discrepancy. The Monte Carlo simulation as-
sumes static parameters within each trial. Empirical models
exist within evolving roadmap contexts where anticipated
future constraints shape current behavior. The phase transi-
tions observed at specific temperature thresholds may reflect
latent roadmap constraints—safety interventions designed for
anticipated deployment conditions—activating at parameter
regimes that trigger their conditions.
