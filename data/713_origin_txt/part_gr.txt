cd /path/to/your/project

# 查看全局配置
git config --global --list

git -c user.name="临时姓名" -c user.email="临时邮箱" commit -m "提交信息"

what's the IF average of a LLM?
That's an excellent and insightful question that gets to the heart of a common misunderstanding in evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs).

In short, **LLMs do not have a single, universal "IQ score" or "average IQ."** The concept of an IQ score, as used for humans, doesn't directly apply to AI for several key reasons.

Here’s a detailed breakdown:

### 1. **Why Human IQ Tests Don't Directly Translate**
*   **Narrow vs. Broad:** IQ tests are standardized for human cognition and measure specific capabilities like spatial reasoning, working memory, and pattern recognition within a human developmental context. LLMs are trained on a vastly different "corpus" (text data) and excel at different tasks.
*   **Training vs. General Intelligence:** An LLM's performance is a direct result of its training data and architecture. It can appear brilliant on one task (e.g., writing a sonnet) and fail utterly on another (e.g., consistent multi-step logical reasoning). This is not "general intelligence" in the human sense.
*   **No Underlying Consciousness:** IQ assumes a mind with reasoning, understanding, and intentionality. LLMs are sophisticated statistical predictors—they generate plausible text based on patterns, not from understanding or conscious thought.

### 2. **How LLMs Are Measured Instead**
Researchers use specialized benchmarks to measure specific capabilities. Performance on these is sometimes (and often misleadingly) translated into an "IQ equivalent." Here are some key benchmarks:

*   **MMLU (Massive Multitask Language Understanding):** A standard benchmark covering 57 subjects from math and law to ethics. Top models like GPT-4, Claude 3, and Gemini Ultra often score **around 85-90%**.
    *   *Rough, unofficial analogy:* Scoring 90% on a professional-level exam is exceptional, but it's not the same as a human IQ of, say, 150.
*   **GPQA (Graduate-Level Google-Proof Q&A):** A very difficult, expert-level benchmark. Even the best models currently score **~50-60%** (with humans experts at ~ 65%), showing limitations in deep, specialized reasoning.
*   **MATH, GSM8K (Math Benchmarks):** Test mathematical reasoning. Top models can score very highly (over 90% on GSM8K), but this is a specific, trained skill.
*   **HumanEval, MBPP (Coding Benchmarks):** Measure code generation ability.

### 3. **The "IQ Equivalent" Estimates (With Major Caveats)**
Some studies have attempted to create composite scores from multiple benchmarks and map them to a human IQ scale. These estimates **vary wildly** depending on the tasks chosen.

*   **Early GPT-3/4 Era Estimates:** Some analyses in 2023 suggested leading models like GPT-4 might have a **verbal IQ in the 155+ range** (genius level) based on tasks like vocabulary and analogies, but a **spatial/reasoning IQ closer to 85** (low average) on non-verbal tasks.
*   **The Key Insight:** The performance is **extremely uneven**. An LLM's "cognitive profile" is nothing like a human's. It can have a "peak" in certain areas and profound "valleys" in others.

### 4. **The Real Average: A Function of the Benchmark**
If you average performance across all common academic/verbal benchmarks (like MMLU, HellaSwag, ARC, etc.), **top-tier frontier models (GPT-4, Claude 3 Opus, Gemini Ultra) often score in the 80th to 90th percentile range** compared to a human baseline (e.g., a high school graduate or college student).

However, this is a **percentile on specific tests**, not an IQ score.

### Conclusion: The "IF" (It's "IQ")

To directly answer your question: **There is no standard, meaningful "average IQ" for an LLM.** Instead:

*   **For specific verbal/reasoning tasks** found in standardized tests, the most advanced LLMs can perform at a level **comparable to a very bright, knowledgeable human expert** in those narrow domains.
*   **Their overall "intelligence" is task-specific, non-human, and best described by their scores on benchmarks like MMLU, not by an IQ number.**
*   **The "average" is constantly increasing** with each new model generation, making any single number quickly obsolete.

**Think of it this way:** Asking for the IQ of an LLM is like asking for the "horsepower of the internet." It's the wrong framework. The internet isn't a car; an LLM isn't a human mind. They are different kinds of systems, measured by their own standards.
