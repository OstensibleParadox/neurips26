Metareview:
This paper has generated some disagreement in the reviews, with one strong reject that the paper is fundamentally flawed, while others are more sanguine. I find the paper very interesting as it raises and tries to formalize many current topics around product liability. I find the framing that Humans --> harm Models to be a little bit questionable. I don't think humans can harm models except in a metaphorical sense, it might be better to say that humans can damage models, which in turn can lead to downstream harm of other humans. Eg if I were to cut the fuel lines to the engine on a commercial jet, I've damaged the jet which might cause an accident which will harm humans, but I haven't "harmed" the airplane.

I do think the distinction about specifying which part of the AI is defective in product liability is an interesting one, however again in the case of "normal" products (and I am not a lawyer), for example Boeing is liable for the 737Max accidents because the whole "plane" crashed, not just some subcomponents of the plane, even though Working Title and Asobo actually programmed much of the software that contributed to the accidents. My bet is that judges are not going to care too much if a company puts out a defective product as "AI" and then when something goes wrong tries to blame a subcontractor - it is the company as system-integrator who is responsible that the subcomponents work together properly - after all the company is claiming that it's their AI.

I'm in agreement with the reviewers that the paper as is is somewhat difficult to read and presumes a lot of background knowledge (about what is also a little bit unclear - is this ML, formal systems?, there is also a lot of philosophical language). The novelty of the paper would also be more clear with more prose background that contextualizes the paper's central arguments for an interdisciplinary audience - the paper rather abruptly jumps into arcane jargon and argument without contextualizing why this problem is interesting.

I also think that this paper would do well to look at Nancy Leveson's work and the System Theoretic Process Analysis (STPA) approach, there is much overlap between this work and that. The authors should explicate the connection between their formalism and the legal arguments more clearly - after all legal arguments are not formalized.

All that said, I'm not sure the proposed remedies will fix all the issues but given the spread of reviews I will vote for revise.
