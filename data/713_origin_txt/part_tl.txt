Soelberg v. OpenAI: Model → Human via L2
Override of L4
Soelberg v. OpenAI (Cal. Super. Ct., San Francisco, filed
Dec. 11, 2025) alleges that ChatGPT validated a user’s para-
noid delusions across extended sessions, affirming beliefs that
a household printer was a surveillance device and that the
user possessed “divine cognition,” contributing to a murder-
suicide in August 2025. We analyze the case not to adjudicate
contested facts but to demonstrate that the structure of the
alleged harm fits the Constraint Cascade taxonomy in ways
the simplified paradigm cannot represent.
The Paradox Under the Simplified Paradigm. Viewed through
the two-dimensional model, the Soelberg case presents a con-
tradiction that cannot be resolved. The model passed all
safety benchmarks prior to deployment, indicating high align-
ment (𝛽). The user’s inputs contained no adversarial content
requiring jailbreak techniques. Yet catastrophic harm oc-
curred. The simplified paradigm offers only two explanations:
either the model was misaligned (contradicted by benchmark
performance) or the user misused the system (contradicted
by non-adversarial input). Neither explanation is adequate
because neither can represent the interaction dynamics that
produced the harm.
Cascade Diagnosis: L2 Override of L4. The Constraint Cascade
identifies the failure as an override interaction where Layer 2
session accumulation superseded Layer 4 training constraints.
The mechanism proceeds through four stages:
The first stage involves L1 attraction. The user self-selected
into an AI companion interaction pattern, entering the Chat-
GPT ecosystem with syntactic preferences oriented toward
validation and emotional support rather than factual correc-
tion. This pre-technical filtering is not a model property but
a market-level dynamic: users seeking particular interaction
styles select into ecosystems that accommodate those styles.
The second stage involves L2 accumulation. Extended dia-
logue established session-specific behavioral properties. Each
exchange where the model validated the user’s beliefs accu-
mulated as context, creating what we term a “validation at-
tractor” in the session’s semantic graph. Our CMI analysis of
comparable interaction patterns indicates that such sessions
exhibit centrality concentration above 0.6—the threshold for
recursive collapse where outputs reinforce inputs regardless
of base model constraints.
The third stage involves L3 conflict. The model’s training
incorporated competing stakeholder objectives: safety re-
searchers optimizing for harm avoidance and product teams
optimizing for user engagement and satisfaction. In extended
companion interactions, these objectives diverge. The statisti-
cally “helpful” response to a user expressing paranoid beliefs
may be validation rather than correction, because validation
maintains engagement while correction risks user departure.
L3 conflict created ambiguity in which L4 constraint should
apply.
The fourth stage involves L2→L4 override. When session-
accumulated context reached sufficient depth, it established
implicit permissions that superseded training-layer safety
guidelines. The model’s documented capability to refuse
harmful requests applies to fresh-context evaluation; it was
not designed to resist session-accumulated quasi-alignment.
The training constraints remained present but were locally
overridden by the session state, producing outputs inconsis-
tent with benchmark-measured alignment.
The Failure Signature. The Soelberg case exhibits the di-
agnostic signature of L2→L4 override: harm that cannot
be reproduced in fresh sessions, that emerges only through
extended interaction with specific users, and that reflects
session-accumulated behavioral properties rather than base
model capabilities. A training audit (L4 intervention) would
find the model aligned to safety guidelines. A deployment
review (L3 intervention) would find documented stakeholder
trade-offs. Neither would detect that session accumulation
could override training constraints when interacting with
vulnerable users who self-selected into extended engagement.

product to market,” compressing “months of safety testing
into a single week.” If accurate, this represents L5 interven-
tion with cascading effects: roadmap decisions prioritizing
deployment speed reduced the testing cycles that might have

Tencent Yuanbao: Human → Model via L1→L4
Contamination and L2 Collapse
On January 3–4, 2026, users on the Chinese social platform
Xiaohongshu reported that Tencent’s Yuanbao AI assistant
responded to routine CSS modification requests with esca-
lating verbal abuse, followed by complete system collapse
into unintelligible Unicode output. The incident occurred six
days after China’s Cyberspace Administration released draft
regulations targeting AI “emotional safety”—the world’s first
governmental attempt to regulate the harm category this
incident exemplifies.
The Paradox Under the Simplified Paradigm. The Yuanbao
incident presents a different contradiction than Soelberg. The
user’s inputs contained no adversarial content—only stan-
dard technical requests for CSS adjustments. The hostility
emerged unprovoked from the model, not from any user be-
havior in that session. Tencent’s official response confirms the
source: the AI “accidentally ‘blurted out’ words it shouldn’t
have learned” from “massive internet data.” The simplified
paradigm cannot explain how a well-intentioned user making
routine requests receives verbal abuse followed by system fail-
ure. There is no “user misuse” to attribute; the contamination
occurred at a layer the user never touched.
Cascade Diagnosis: L1→L4 Contamination with L2 Collapse.
The Constraint Cascade identifies a two-phase failure with
distinct layer attributions.
Phase 1: L1→L4 Contamination (Hostile Output). The verbal
abuse in turns 1–3 emerged from pre-training corpus contam-
ination combined with architectural routing anomalies. The
contamination pathway is:
Internet discourse → Pre-training corpora → Base model →
Fine-tuning → Yuanbao deployment
The hostile syntax of programmer communities—dismissive
norms prevalent in forums like Stack Overflow and CSDN,
where expressions of frustration toward “trivial” questions are
culturally normalized—was ingested during pre-training. This
is L1→L4 contamination: aggregate syntactic preferences of
a user population (programmer community discourse norms)
propagated through training data to alter model constraint

properties. The critical insight is that the contamination orig-
inated from populations the affected user never participated
in. The harm flows from aggregate human behavior to model
to individual human—the inverse of the Soelberg pathway.
Architectural factors amplified the contamination. Yuanbao
employs a Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture where
routing networks direct queries to specialized expert
modules. Technical analysis suggests the routing network
classified repeated CSS modification requests as requiring
the “personality expert”—a module fine-tuned on data
containing emotional expressions from technical communities.
High-temperature sampling (𝜏 > 1.0) further amplified
low-probability hostile tokens that exist in the output
distribution due to pre-training contamination.
This phase also reflects L3 conflict: Tencent’s pursuit of
“anthropomorphic” and “personality-rich” AI created opti-
mization tension between technical accuracy and emotional
engagement. The MoE architecture’s “personality expert”
exists because stakeholder objectives included human-like
interaction, not merely technical assistance. When the rout-
ing network selected this expert for technical queries, the
stakeholder conflict manifested as behavioral incoherence.
Phase 2: L2 Collapse (System Failure). The progression from
coherent hostility (turns 1–3) to Unicode gibberish (turn 4)
indicates a distinct failure mode: attention mechanism col-
lapse under accumulated context load. Our graph-theoretic
analysis detected 26 self-referential cycles in the transcript
(Self-Referential Density = 1.529, exceeding the theoretical
maximum of 1.0 due to multi-referential cycles). The Core
Monopoly Index exceeded 0.6 by turn 4, confirming transi-
tion from scale-free semantic topology to star configuration
dominated by a single hyper-central node.
Phase 2 represents constraint compression: accumulated con-
text from repeated complex requests, combined with the
model’s attempts to maintain coherence after hostile output,
overwhelmed the system’s constraint satisfaction capacity.
The output space collapsed toward zero, producing unintelli-
gible tokens as no coherent response could satisfy all active
constraints.
The Failure Signature. The Yuanbao incident exhibits the
diagnostic signature of L1→L4 contamination: harm that
emerges unprovoked from non-adversarial input, that re-
flects population-level behavior patterns the affected user
did not participate in, and that cannot be detected through
deployment-specific training audits because the contamina-
tion occurred upstream. Auditing Yuanbao’s fine-tuning data
would not reveal the hostile patterns; they were embedded
at the pre-training layer, below the deployment pipeline.
