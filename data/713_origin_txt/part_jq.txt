• Layer 1 failures (user-model mismatch): Market-level dis-

Neither path preserves criminal law’s ultima ratio principle.
The graduated thresholds that make criminal law a last resort
cannot be reconstructed for systems where output is the only
observable. AI governance must either accept strict liability
at the corporate level or develop domain-specific frameworks
that do not pretend to map categories (intent, negligence,
recklessness) that structurally cannot apply.
Contribution Summary
This paper contributes the translation layer itself: diagnostic
vocabulary that renders AI architectural dynamics legible to
legal and policy analysis. We provide:
(1) The Constraint Cascade Model: A five-layer taxonomy
specifying where constraints apply and how they propa-
gate.
(2) The Liability Paradox: A formal impossibility result es-
tablishing that mens rea attribution is undecidable for
AI systems, grounding the shift to strict liability.
(3) Bidirectional Failure Taxonomy: Formal definitions dis-
tinguishing Model→Human and Human→Model harm
directions, enabling targeted governance.
(4) Diagnostic Metrics: Agency Index, CMI, and SRD pro-
viding quantifiable signatures of layer-specific failures.
(5) Judicial Connection: Mapping the “products not speech”
evolution in Garcia to constraint cascade layers, demon-
strating practical legal application.
The Constraint Cascade does not resolve the fundamental
tension between AI architecture and legal doctrine—that ten-
sion is real and will persist. Rather, it provides the vocabulary
necessary to navigate that tension productively: to identify
which layer failed, to design interventions matched to failure
origins, and to build governance frameworks that acknowl-
edge what AI systems actually are rather than importing
categories from what they are not.
We propose the Agency Index (𝐴 ∝ 𝐷KL × MDL−1) as a di-
agnostic heuristic for distinguishing system malfunction from
strategic behavior (Figure 2a). A malfunction exhibits high
output divergence with high incompressibility—noise without
structure. Strategic behavior exhibits high divergence with
low semantic description length (measurable via clustering
in embedding space), distinguishing coherent strategies from
low-complexity syntactic repetition (loops)—coherent devia-
tion from baseline. This metric does not resolve the Liability
Paradox; it provides the audit criterion necessary to iden-
tify which failures warrant deeper investigation. Reference
implementation in Appendix.

Bidirectional Failure Mode Analysis
The Constraint Cascade framework is designed to diagnose
harm in both directions: Model → Human (AI outputs influ-
encing user behavior) and Human → Model (user behavior
contaminating AI outputs). The Agency Index provides a
heuristic for the former—distinguishing strategic deviation
from malfunction in model outputs.
For the Human → Model direction, we analyze syntactic
preference distributions at Layer 1 as potential contamina-
tion vectors. When user populations exhibit systematically
divergent interaction norms—including normalized adversar-
ial usage, jailbreak-as-entertainment patterns, or discourse
styles that alignment frameworks classify as harmful—the
feedback loop from L1 (user behavior) through L2 (session
patterns) to L4 (training updates or output distribution
shifts) can degrade alignment properties in ways invisible to
pre-deployment auditing.
This bidirectionality has architectural implications. The Con-
straint Cascade model makes explicit what has remained
implicit in industry practice: that Layer 1 contamination
pathways exist and cannot be governed through output fil-
tering alone.
Resolving the Dual Bind: Formal Foundations
The preceding sections established two unmappability results.
Section 2 demonstrated that criminal law’s mens rea cate-
gories cannot map onto autoregressive systems because intent
attribution from behavioral observation is undecidable (the
Liability Paradox). Section 3 demonstrated that simplified
technical models cannot map onto empirical LLM behav-
ior because the two-dimensional alignment-noise paradigm
produces predictions systematically violated by production
systems (the Monte Carlo–Empirical Discrepancy). Section 4
introduced the Constraint Cascade Model as an alternative
architecture.
This section formalizes the relationship between these results.
We prove that the dual unmappability is not coincidental but
structurally related—both legal and technical frameworks fail
because they assume behavioral properties that multi-layer
constraint architectures do not exhibit. We then develop
Anon.
a typology of constraint interactions that provides the ex-
planatory vocabulary for locating failure origins within the
cascade.
Theorem 2: Dual Unmappability
We formalize the central theoretical contribution as a map-
ping impossibility result with a constructive resolution.
Definition (Concept Spaces). Let:
• L denote the legal-concept space, comprising the cate-
gories through which law attributes responsibility: in-
tent (purposeful, knowing, reckless, negligent), causation
(but-for, proximate), and agency (principal, accomplice,
instrument).
• T denote the simplified technical-concept space, compris-
ing the parameters through which current governance
frameworks characterize AI behavior: alignment 𝛽 ∈ [0, 1],
noise 𝜂 ∈ [0, 1], and their interaction.
• R denote the real-world AI-behavior space, comprising
the observable behavioral dynamics of deployed systems:
output distributions, variance profiles, phase transition
signatures, and scale-dependent stability patterns.
• C denote the constraint-cascade space, comprising the
five-layer architecture: 𝐶 = 𝐿1 × 𝐿2 × 𝐿3 × 𝐿4 × 𝐿5, where
each 𝐿𝑖 represents the constraint state of layer 𝑖.
Theorem 2 (Dual Unmappability). The following mapping
relations hold:
(i) Legal Unmappability. There exists no computable function
𝑓 ∶ 𝐿 → 𝑅 such that legal categories reliably predict or
explain AI behavioral dynamics.
(ii) Technical Unmappability. There exists no computable
function 𝑔 ∶ 𝑇 → 𝑅 such that simplified technical parameters
reliably predict or explain AI behavioral dynamics.
(iii) Cascade Computability. There exists a computable func-
tion ℎ ∶ 𝐶 → 𝑅 such that constraint-cascade configurations
predict observable behavioral dynamics, and there exist par-
tial translation functions:
• ℎ1 ∶ 𝐶 → 𝐿′, where 𝐿′ is an adapted legal-concept space
• ℎ2 ∶ 𝐶 → 𝑇 ′, where 𝑇 ′ is an expanded technical-concept
space
Proof. Part (i): Legal Unmappability. By Theorem 1 (Mens

Corollary (Governance Implication). Effective AI governance
requires operating in constraint-cascade space 𝐶 rather than
legal space 𝐿 or simplified technical space 𝑇. Governance
frameworks built on 𝐿 will systematically fail to attribute re-
sponsibility for harms emerging from constraint interactions.
Governance frameworks built on 𝑇 will systematically fail
to predict behavioral dynamics exhibiting phase transitions
and cross-layer resonance. Only frameworks capable of repre-
senting multi-layer constraint interactions can achieve both
attribution and prediction.

agnostic vocabulary for locating failure origins and matching
interventions to failure types.

interventions at the overriding layer: session boundaries, con-
