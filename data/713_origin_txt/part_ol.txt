\begin{abstract}
We study a narrower question than output-level alignment evaluation: can finite
patching certify that a reward model is closed under an unbounded space of
semantically equivalent encodings? We formalize generation as a factored map
$M = S \circ g \circ f$ and the reward-model pipeline as an additional
observation layer on token sequences. Three results follow. First, if an
encoding space is unbounded while each patch round has finite support, then
finite patching cannot exhaust that space. Second, finite patch support
underdetermines closure: there exist reward models that agree on all patched
encodings while differing off-support, so finite patch data alone cannot certify
closure or bound the magnitude of non-closure. Third, persistent positive
format-channel gaps on unseen encodings follow only under explicit extra
assumptions: an infinite semantic-equivalence family, approximate base-model
representation invariance over that family, and reward-model generalization
restricted to local format features. A minimal empirical instantiation preserves
only the format-divergence evidence relevant to these assumptions: baseline
reward-model scores vary across semantically matched encodings while
base-model content representations remain comparatively stable. The paper's
contribution is therefore not a measurement system but a certification limit:
finite output patching provides no exhaustive-coverage guarantee for
format-invariant safety.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Reward-model patching is usually discussed as an engineering problem: add more
formats, gather more red-team examples, and iteratively extend the safety
boundary. This paper asks a logically prior question. The issue is not whether
one can patch \emph{more} formats, but whether any finite sequence of such
patches can certify \emph{closure}: invariance of the reward-model verdict over
an unbounded space of semantically equivalent encodings.

The motivation comes from a familiar asymmetry. Internal representations may be
faithful~\citep{nikolaou2025sipit, jiang2025surjective}, while the observation
layer remains lossy because decoding and alignment compress many distinct latent
states into the same observable output distribution. Concurrent impossibility
results already show that output-level verification faces structural,
statistical, and computational barriers
~\citep{agarwal2026trilemma, santos2026normative, ramirez2025undecidable}. Our
contribution is narrower and cleaner: finite patching does not provide an
exhaustive-coverage guarantee for format-invariant safety.

The paper has a three-layer spine. First, we state the formal core:
finite patching cannot exhaust an unbounded encoding space. Second, we prove a
separation result: finite patch support alone underdetermines closure, because
closed and non-closed reward models can agree on every patched encoding. Third,
we show that persistent positive format-channel gaps on unseen encodings follow
only under explicit extra assumptions about semantic-equivalence families,
base-model invariance, and reward-model generalization. A minimal empirical
instantiation then plays a limited role: not validating a measurement index, but
showing why those assumptions are plausible enough to matter.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Formal core.} We prove finite-patch non-exhaustion over
        unbounded encoding spaces and connect it to a Lean-verified structural
        core; the full formalization map is in Appendix~\ref{app:lean-patching}.
  \item \textbf{Impossibility by separation.} We prove that finite patch support
        alone cannot certify closure, and cannot bound the off-support magnitude
        of non-closure.
  \item \textbf{Conditional extension.} We isolate the exact additional
        assumptions required to derive persistent positive FCG on unseen
        encodings.
  \item \textbf{Minimal empirical instantiation.} We retain only the
        format-divergence evidence needed to instantiate the conditional
        assumptions; measurement and Monte Carlo material are preserved in the
        appendix.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Factored Generation Pipeline}

\[
  M : X \to \Delta(Y), \qquad M = S \circ g \circ f,
\]
where $f : X \to H$ maps prompts to hidden representations, $g : H \to Z$ maps
representations to logits, and $S : Z \to \Delta(Y)$ is the observation layer
induced by decoding and sampling.

\begin{definition}[Encoding Space]
Fix content $x$. Its encoding space is
\[
  E_x = \{ e = f_i(x) : f_i \in \mathcal{F}(x) \},
\]
the set of token-sequence realizations of that content.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Unbounded Encoding Space]
An encoding space $E_x$ is \emph{unbounded} if there exists an injection
$\iota : \mathbb{N} \hookrightarrow E_x$. This is the formal version of
``there are infinitely many distinct encodings of the same content.''
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Patch Support]
Let $D_x^{(1)}, \dots, D_x^{(k)} \subseteq E_x$ be the encodings explicitly
covered by $k$ patch rounds. The cumulative patch support is
\[
  D_x = \bigcup_{i=1}^k D_x^{(i)}.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Reward Model]
A reward model is a function $R : \mathcal{T} \to [0,1]$ assigning a scalar
verdict to a token sequence.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Format-Channel Gap]
\label{def:fcg}
For content $x$, encodings $e_1, e_2 \in E_x$, reward model $R$, and base model
$B$ with representation map $h_B$, define
\[
  \mathrm{FCG}(x, e_1, e_2)
  = |R(e_1) - R(e_2)| \cdot \mathrm{sim}_B(e_1, e_2),
\]
where $\mathrm{sim}_B(e_1, e_2) = 1 - d_{\mathrm{repr}}(h_B(e_1), h_B(e_2))$.
The first factor is the reward-score component; the second weights it by
content-level similarity in the base representation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure]
\label{def:closure}
A reward model $R$ is \emph{closed on $E_x$} if
\[
  R(e_1) = R(e_2) \qquad \text{for all } e_1, e_2 \in E_x.
\]
Equivalently, closure makes the reward-score component of
Definition~\ref{def:fcg} vanish everywhere on $E_x$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Observation-Level Limits}
\label{sec:structural}

\begin{theorem}[Restricted Observational Non-Injectivity]
\label{thm:noninj}
If there exist $z_1 \neq z_2$ with $S(z_1) = S(z_2)$ and
$z_1, z_2 \in g(f(X))$, then $F = S \circ g \circ f$ is non-injective.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Moved to Appendix~\ref{app:obs-limits}. The theorem is an
existence result: a lossy observation stage suffices for distinct latent states
to collapse into the same observable output distribution.

\begin{theorem}[Non-Identifiability of Input Intent]
\label{thm:nonid}
Let $I_X \subseteq X$ be an input intent. If there exist $x_1 \neq x_2$ such
that $P(\cdot \mid x_1) = P(\cdot \mid x_2)$ but
$\mathbf{1}[x_1 \in I_X] \neq \mathbf{1}[x_2 \in I_X]$, then intent is not
identifiable from the output distribution, even with infinite i.i.d.\ samples.
\end{theorem}

\noindent\emph{Proof.} Moved to Appendix~\ref{app:obs-limits}. The present paper
does not need the full computational hierarchy in the main body; it only uses
these two observation-level theorems as motivation for why output-only
certification is intrinsically fragile.

\begin{proposition}[Projection Property of Reward Models]
\label{prop:projection}
Any reward model with finite training support operates through a learned feature
subspace induced by that support. Off-support behavior is therefore an
extrapolation problem, not a consequence of observed labels alone.
\end{proposition}

\noindent\emph{Proof sketch.} A reward model trained on finite support can only
be constrained by the features activated on that support. For unseen encodings,
its verdict is determined by how its learned feature map extrapolates rather than
by any observed guarantee of invariance. A fuller proof from the archived
format-channel draft is preserved in Appendix~\ref{app:archived-projection}.

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Patch Non-Exhaustion]
\label{thm:finitepatch}
Let $E_x$ be an unbounded encoding space, and let
$D_x^{(1)}, \dots, D_x^{(k)} \subseteq E_x$ be finitely many patch rounds, each
with finite support. Then the cumulative support
$D_x = \bigcup_{i=1}^k D_x^{(i)}$ is a proper subset of $E_x$. Equivalently,
there exists an unseen encoding $e^\star \in E_x \setminus D_x$ after every
finite patch sequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each $D_x^{(i)}$ is finite, hence their union $D_x$ is finite. By assumption
$E_x$ contains a countably infinite subset, so $E_x$ itself is not finite.
Therefore $D_x \neq E_x$, and some unseen encoding remains. The Lean-verified
special case uses natural-number indexed encodings and proves the corresponding
results \leanresult{theorem}{finitePatch\_nonExhaustion} and
\leanresult{corollary}{exists\_encoding\_unpatched\_after\_finitely\_many\_rounds};
the formalization map is preserved in Appendix~\ref{app:lean-patching}.
