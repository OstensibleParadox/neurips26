> “既然内部没有真正的意图状态，那么**安全评估者唯一能观测到的就是输出行为**。因此，评估者只能退而求其次，试图通过外部的**语义不变性测试**来间接认证模型是否对齐。但我们会证明，即便这种退而求其次的外部认证，也面临结构性不可能。”

具体衔接段落的建议写法（可直接放入论文）：

```latex
\subsection{From Internal Impossibility to External Certification Limits}
\label{sec:transition}

Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_dissimilarity} establishes that autoregressive language models lack the causal architecture required to instantiate an Endogenous Intentional State. Consequently, any attribution of subjective intent, *mens rea*, or emotional valence to the model's internal processes is a category error---a projection of folk psychology onto a deterministic input-output map.

This internal impossibility shifts the burden of safety assurance entirely to the \emph{external} observable layer. Since one cannot inspect a non-existent intentional state, evaluators and auditors are constrained to examine only the output distribution $P(Y \mid X)$ and its sensitivity to semantically equivalent input variations. The de facto standard for such external assurance is \emph{finite patching}: iteratively expanding the set of covered input encodings to certify that the reward model's verdict remains invariant across semantically equivalent expressions of the same content.

The remainder of this paper addresses the following question: \emph{even if we abandon the search for internal intent and rely solely on external behavioral certification, can finite patching provide an exhaustive guarantee of semantic invariance?}

We answer in the negative. The external route inherits the same fundamental limitation: the observation layer $S$ is non-injective (Theorem~\ref{thm:noninj}), and the space of semantically equivalent encodings is unbounded. In the following sections, we prove that finite patching cannot exhaust this space (Theorem~\ref{thm:finitepatch}), and that closure of the reward model over unseen encodings is underdetermined by any finite patch support (Proposition~\ref{prop:underdetermined}). Together, these results demonstrate that external behavioral certification is structurally incomplete, regardless of the absence of internal intentional states.
```

### 第四部分：外部路径 —— 语义不变性认证的极限
- **Theorem 2 (Finite-Patch Non-Exhaustion)**：任何有限轮次补丁都无法覆盖无界编码空间。
- **Proposition 3 (Underdetermination of Closure)**：有限补丁数据无法区分闭包与非闭包奖励模型，也无法约束非闭包的幅度。
- **Proposition 4 (Conditional Positive FCG)**：只有在额外强假设下，才能导出未见编码上的正格式通道间隙。
- **实证最小实例化**：展示在真实模型中，奖励模型分数随编码变化而基模型表示相对稳定，说明这些假设在实际系统中具有合理性（从而外部认证漏洞真实存在）。

### 第五部分：综合讨论与不可能性闭环
- 将**内部本体论否定**与**外部认识论/认证论极限**合并为一张“不可能三角”图（可选）。
- 结论：AI 安全认证不能依赖“意图归因”（因结构不存在），也不能依赖“有限补丁认证”（因覆盖不可能穷尽）。真正的安全保证需要完全不同的架构范式（如 Active Inference 智能体）或根本不同的监管策略。

2. **防止“稻草人”指责**：衔接段明确指出，正因为内部没有意图，评估者**被迫**依赖外部测试。这赋予外部路径分析以实践必要性——它不是在攻击一个无人使用的脆弱方法，而是在解构当前安全实践的**唯一可行选项**。

\begin{abstract}
Claims that current language models exhibit human-like intent, emotion, or
related psychological states require a prior formal question: what would make
such attribution warranted in the first place? We give a two-pronged negative
answer for current autoregressive architectures under an output-only evaluation
regime. First, using an Active Inference comparison object, we define an
endogenous intentional state (EIS) as a Markov-blanketed latent variable with
autonomous degrees of freedom that remain causally upstream of policy formation,
and we show that autoregressive cores do not instantiate such states; a
conditional extension covers wrapper systems whose full operative state is
screenable from the external interaction trace. Second, once evaluation is
restricted to external behavior alone, verdict invariance across semantically
equivalent encodings becomes a necessary surrogate condition, yet finite
patching cannot exhaust unbounded encoding spaces and finite patch data cannot
certify closure on them. The resulting claim is deliberately narrow: for current
autoregressive language models and output-only certification procedures,
intentional-state attribution is not formally warranted by either internal
architecture or finite behavioral evidence.
\end{abstract}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Large language models are routinely described as if they possessed intentions,
emotions, or even legally resonant mental states. Those descriptions may be
useful shorthand in informal discourse, but a technical paper should first ask
what would have to be true for such attribution to be formally warranted.

This paper studies that question along two routes. The first is internal:
does the architecture itself contain a causal variable that can bear the load of
intentional-state attribution? The second is external: if the architecture does
not expose such a variable, can a black-box evaluator recover a sufficiently
strong behavioral surrogate from output evidence alone? These questions are
logically distinct, and both matter. Internal representations can be rich and
highly informative without thereby constituting autonomous intentional
states~\citep{nikolaou2025sipit, jiang2025surjective}. Conversely, a purely
behavioral certification story must survive the fact that output distributions
are mediated by decoding, alignment, and surface-form variation.

The manuscript's organizing claim is intentionally narrow. We do not argue that
all possible machine architectures must fail to support intentional states, nor
that behavioral safety testing is pointless. We argue instead that for current
autoregressive language models, and for evaluation procedures that lack access
to a genuine internal intentional variable, neither route suffices to warrant
human-style intentional-state attribution.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Structural prerequisite.} We define an endogenous intentional
        state (EIS) in Active Inference terms as a latent variable with
        autonomous degrees of freedom and policy relevance.
  \item \textbf{Internal impossibility.} We prove that autoregressive cores do
        not instantiate EIS, because their operative state is fully determined
        by the current context trace.
  \item \textbf{Scope-controlled extension.} We extend the internal result to a
        broader but still limited class of wrapper systems whose full operative
        state remains screenable from the external interaction trace.
  \item \textbf{External impossibility.} We show that once evaluation falls back
        to an output-only regime, verdict invariance over semantically
        equivalent encodings becomes a necessary surrogate target, yet finite
        patching cannot certify that target on an unbounded encoding space.
  \item \textbf{Synthesis.} We combine the internal and external results into a
        single claim about the current evidential limits of intentional-state
        attribution for autoregressive language models.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Preliminaries}
\label{sec:background}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Factored Generation Pipeline}

\[
  M : X \to \Delta(Y), \qquad M = S \circ g \circ f,
\]
where $f : X \to H$ maps prompts and prior context to hidden representations,
$g : H \to Z$ maps representations to logits, and $S : Z \to \Delta(Y)$ is the
observation layer induced by decoding and sampling.

For the internal question, the relevant issue is not whether the representation
space $H$ is rich, but whether the model contains an endogenous state variable
with causal autonomy relative to the current observation stream.

\subsection{Active Inference and Endogenous Intentional States}

Active Inference models an agent as maintaining latent beliefs behind a Markov
blanket that separates internal states from the external environment
\citep{friston2010free}. On that description, action is not merely an emitted
response; it is selected relative to internal latent variables that both persist
and update under endogenous dynamics.

\begin{definition}[Endogenous Intentional State]
\label{def:eis}
Let $I_t$ be a latent variable in an agent model with current observation
$X_t$ and action policy $\pi_t$. We call $I_t$ an \emph{endogenous intentional
state} if both of the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Causal autonomy.} $I_t$ retains degrees of freedom not screened
        off by the immediate observation, so that $H(I_t \mid X_t) > 0$.
  \item \textbf{Policy relevance.} $I_t$ is causally upstream of policy
        formation, in the sense that $\pi_t$ depends on $I_t$ as part of the
        system's internal action-selection dynamics rather than merely as a
        deterministic recoding of $X_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}
The first clause is the load-bearing distinction for the present paper. Memory
mechanisms such as KV caches or scratchpads can preserve information across
steps, but persistence alone is not enough. The question is whether the system
contains a latent variable with autonomous degrees of freedom relative to the
current externally supplied trace.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Output-Only Evaluation Regime}

\begin{definition}[Output-Only Evaluation Regime]
\label{def:output-only}
An evaluator is in an \emph{output-only evaluation regime} when it can vary
inputs and observe outputs or downstream verdicts, but cannot inspect or
intervene on any putative internal intentional variable.
