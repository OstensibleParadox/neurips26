Definition. Compression occurs when constraints from mul-

Diagnostic Signature. Compression failures produce unex-

individually satisfiable.
Governance Response. Compression failures require archi-
tectural interventions: constraint prioritization hierarchies,
graceful degradation protocols, and transparency about which
constraints are blocking output.

Diagnostic Metrics: Operationalizing the
Constraint Cascade
The Constraint Cascade Model provides architectural vo-
cabulary for locating failure origins across five layers. How-
ever, diagnostic vocabulary without measurement capacity re-
mains analytically inert. This section develops three metrics—
Agency Index (A), Core Monopoly Index (CMI), and Self-
Referential Density (SRD)—as the operational instruments
required to detect constraint interactions and identify layer-
specific failures in production systems.
These metrics are not proposed as regulatory standards but
as diagnostic tools enabling the translation from “unmap-
pable” behavioral dynamics to “locatable” failure origins.
Following Jacobs and Wallach’s framework for measurement
validity in algorithmic fairness contexts [? ], we evaluate each
metric against construct validity requirements, acknowledg-
ing that introducing quantitative measures into governance
contexts carries significant normative weight. The metrics
must capture what they claim to capture before they can
inform intervention design.
Theoretical Framework: Construct Validity for
Governance Metrics
Jacobs and Wallach [? ] established that fairness metrics in
machine learning must satisfy validity requirements equiva-
lent to measures in social sciences. Measurement is not merely
technical operationalization but involves normative choices
about which aspects of complex constructs to capture and
which to elide. We adopt their seven-component framework
to evaluate each metric:
Face Validity: Do measurements appear plausible on their sur-
face? Content Validity: Does the operationalization capture
all relevant aspects of the construct? Convergent Validity: Do
measurements correlate with other measures of the same con-
struct? Discriminant Validity: Are measurements uncorrelated
with measures of different constructs? Predictive Validity: Do
measurements predict relevant observable properties? Hy-
pothesis Validity: Can substantively interesting hypotheses
emerge from measurements? Consequential Validity: What
are the societal impacts of using these measurements?

Each metric is evaluated against these components, with
explicit acknowledgment where validation is complete, partial,
