你提出的这个点，在法学（尤其是证据法和刑法）中确实是一个基础且重要的逻辑原则。它不仅存在成熟的术语，更有精细的论证规则。

简单来说，法律要求裁判者不能因为**无法排除**某种可能性，就**推定**该种可能性存在。谁主张某种事实（如被告人有罪、存在因果关系），谁就必须提供证据证明到法定标准；而对方只需提出**合理怀疑**来动摇这个证明，无需自证清白。

围绕这个核心，法律上主要有以下几个维度的术语和论证模板：

### 1. 证明责任与证明标准：核心论证模板
这是最直接、最有力的论证模板。法律首先明确证明责任在谁，然后要求其必须达到特定的证明标准。

*   **论证模板**："控方主张X事实存在，但仅凭‘无法排除其他可能性’这一点，不足以证明X。因为控方承担**证明责任**，必须将X证明到**排除合理怀疑**的程度（刑事案件）。只要存在一种与控方主张并存的**合理可能性**，根据**罪疑唯轻**原则，就应做出对被告有利的认定。"
*   **关键术语**：
    *   **排除合理怀疑 (Beyond Reasonable Doubt)**：这是刑事诉讼的最高证明标准[citation:8]。它不要求绝对确定，但要求一个理性人基于证据，在道德上确信被告有罪。如果存在"合理怀疑"，就必须判无罪。
    *   **证明责任 (Burden of Proof)**：包含提出证据的责任和说服裁判者的责任。在刑事案件中，这个责任始终在控方，被告没有自证无罪的义务[citation:3][citation:6]。
    *   **罪疑唯轻 (In Dubio Pro Reo)**：当事实真伪不明时，应做出有利于被告的判决[citation:4]。

### 2. 合理怀疑与疑点形成：辩方论证模板
这是从被告方视角出发的逻辑，即"我不需要证明我没做，我只需要证明你的故事有问题"。

*   **论证模板**："控方的证据链条存在缺口/矛盾，这构成了一个**合理疑点**。该疑点足以动摇控方**排除合理怀疑**的证明。因此，控方未尽到其**证明责任**，应判被告无罪。"
*   **关键术语**：
    *   **合理疑点 (Reasonable Doubt)**：不是凭空想象的怀疑，而是基于证据、常识和逻辑产生的、有现实可能性的疑问[citation:3][citation:8]。
    *   **疑点形成责任**：被告虽然没有最终的证明责任，但有权提出证据或论点来"形成"一个"合理疑点"，从而迫使控方去"排除"它[citation:3][citation:6][citation:10]。

### 3. 客观归责与因果关系：实体法论证模板
在刑法实体法中，讨论行为与结果的关系时，这个逻辑同样适用。

*   **论证模板**："虽然无法排除行为人的违规行为与结果之间存在关联的可能性，但根据**结果避免可能性**理论，即便行为人遵守了义务，**也无法确定**结果就能被避免。根据**罪疑唯轻**原则，这种不确定性的后果应由控方承担，应认定行为与结果之间的**义务违反关联性**不成立，从而**排除客观归责**。"
*   **关键术语**：
    *   **假定因果关系**：指即使没有行为人的行为，结果也一样会发生。通常认为这不影响归责[citation:4]。
    *   **结果避免可能性**：讨论如果行为人遵守了注意义务，结果是否"确定能够"被避免。如果遵守义务也不能避免，则不能归责；如果能否避免"不确定"，则根据罪疑唯轻，视为不能避免，排除归责[citation:4]。
    *   **客观归责理论**：一个行为制造了法所不容许的风险，这个风险最终实现了，才能归责于行为人。如果风险的存在与否是不确定的，就不能进行客观归责。

### 4. 高度盖然性：民事领域的对比
了解民事标准可以反衬刑事标准的严格。

*   **民事诉讼**的标准通常是**高度盖然性**，即法官根据生活经验，认为一方主张的事实"十之八九"是真实的，即使不能完全排除其他可能性，也可以认定该事实[citation:5][citation:9]。
*   **对比论证**：你的问题特指"从无法排除不能推出积极归因"，这在**刑事诉讼**中是完全正确的，但在**民事诉讼**中，如果一方的可能性远高于另一方，法官就可能采信。

### 总结

要论证"不能因为无法排除某种可能性，就认定它是真的"，最成熟有力的法律术语就是 **"排除合理怀疑"** 证明标准，而其最直接的逻辑武器则是 **"罪疑唯轻"** 原则。

> "根据**排除合理怀疑**原则，控方（或主张方）的证明未能达到法定标准。本案中存在的**合理疑点**，足以依据**罪疑唯轻**原则，做出对被告（或相对方）有利的认定。"
什么是举证责任倒置。
**举证责任倒置**，简单说就是：**法律明确规定，本应由原告（或主张权利方）证明的事实，改由被告（或对方）证明该事实不存在。**

要理解它，先要知道正常情况（“正置”）是什么样子。

### 1. 正常情况：谁主张，谁举证
根据“谁主张，谁举证”的一般原则：
-   **原告**起诉，就必须证明**被告有过错**、被告行为与损害有**因果关系**等核心事实。
-   如果原告证明不了，就败诉。

### 2. 举证责任倒置：法律规定的例外
在一些特殊案件中，立法者认为让原告证明某些事实**极其困难、甚至不可能**（比如医疗事故中的因果关系、产品缺陷的成因），而被告证明这些事实不存在则相对容易。于是，法律就把这个**证明负担**转移给被告。

**核心逻辑：** 原告只需证明自己受到了损害、与被告行为有关联等基础事实，然后**法律就推定**被告有过错、行为与损害有因果关系。被告要想免责，就必须拿出证据**推翻这个推定**，证明自己没过错或因果关系不存在。

### 3. 典型例子

| 案件类型 | 原告（受害人）需证明 | 被告需证明（倒置部分） |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **医疗损害责任** | 1. 在这家医院看了病<br>2. 受到了损害（如伤残） | **医院没有过错**（诊疗符合规范）<br>**或者**损害是患者自身病情特殊等所致 |
| **环境污染责任** | 1. 排污者造成了污染<br>2. 自己受到了损害 | **污染行为与损害之间没有因果关系** |
| **高空抛物致害**（无法确定谁抛的） | 1. 被某栋楼的某物体砸伤<br>2. 发生了实际损害 | **所有可能加害的建筑物使用人**证明自己**不是侵权人**（如事发时不在家） |
| **产品缺陷责任** | 1. 买了该产品<br>2. 使用中受损 | **生产者/销售者**证明产品**不存在缺陷**<br>**或者**缺陷是受害人自己造成的 |

### 4. 与“举证责任转移”的区别（易混淆点）
-   **举证责任倒置**：是**法律事先规定好**的，整个证明负担永久性、整体性地由一方转给另一方。原告不承担该事实的证明责任。
-   **举证责任转移**：是**诉讼过程中**，一方提供了初步证据后，法官根据心证，要求另一方提供反证。这是临时的、弹性的。例如：原告证明被告闯红灯后，法官可能要求被告证明自己不是司机。

### 总结一句话
**举证责任倒置 = 法律把最难的证明任务，从受害人（原告）转移给了加害人（被告）。** 它体现了法律在医疗、环保、产品安全等特殊领域对弱势一方的保护，以及对公平和效率的追求。

## Abstract
Interpreting large language models requires establishing formal boundaries on internal audit-trace bottleneck and output-only certification, rather than merely evaluating whether outputs appear intentional. Internally, we prove that for autoregressive cores and $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked wrapper systems, any candidate latent variable derivable from the full internal state $S_t$ suffers from bounded conditional mutual information relative to the external trace. This strict bottleneck imposes a fundamental internal audit-trace bottleneck barrier on residual decision control. Externally, we identify output-only certification barriers: exact verification requires semantic closure over the encoding class, and passive PAC certification can require sample complexity scaling with metric packing complexity. As an explicitly diagnostic layer, we show that under strong reward smoothness and coverage assumptions, geometric stability can bind format-channel gaps; when these regularity conditions fail, certification inherits the worst-case packing lower bound. Rather than dismissing the practical utility of output-only evaluation, this work maps its formal ceilings and floors, delineating architecture- and evidence-relative boundaries for exact and passive approximate certification.

## 1 Introduction

Evaluating large language models involves a conceptual gap between generating an observable surface trace and operating via an independent internal decision process. While the term agency serves as a useful shorthand, technical analysis requires defining the architectural and access conditions under which such claims can be verified from available observations.

We approach this problem through two bottlenecks: internal audit-trace bottleneck and output-only certification barriers. Internally, we examine whether the architecture contains a causal variable capable of supporting an latent decision state, or if the internal state remains fundamentally bottlenecked by the external trace. Externally, we ask whether a black-box evaluator can certify a sufficiently strong behavioral surrogate from output evidence alone. These two challenges are logically distinct. Architectural maps can preserve extensive prompt information or realise broad output families without thereby constituting independent latent decision states [Nikolaou et al., 2025, Jiang and Haghtalab, 2025]; see also the readability–relevance gap identified by Geiger et al. [2021] and Elazar et al. [2021]. Conversely, a purely behavioral certification story must survive the fact that output distributions are mediated by decoding, alignment, and surface-form variation. This is precisely why black-box access alone provides a weak basis for rigorous auditing [Casper et al., 2024].

Our claim is restricted in scope: we do not argue that all machine architectures fail to support independent latent states, nor do we dismiss the practical utility of behavioral safety testing. Output-only evaluations remain essential for empirical risk bounding. However, it is necessary to formally acknowledge their ceilings and floors. We show that for current autoregressive cores and highly audit-trace-bottlenecked wrapper systems, standard internal and output-only routes cannot certify the target claim under the defined access conditions. The certification target studied here—output-only verification of semantic-invariant behaviour together with positive internal causal certification—is distinct from the classical learning-theoretic targets of identification in the limit, exact learning from strong queries, or PAC concept acquisition, and the relevant impossibility mechanisms are correspondingly different.

**Contributions.** We prove four results:
1. **Exact DPI bottleneck (autoregressive cores).** For autoregressive cores the full internal state is a deterministic function of the context trace, so the data processing inequality implies no candidate internal state carries residual decision-relevant information beyond the trace (Corollary 1).
2. **$\epsilon$-DPI bound for wrapper architectures.** For $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked wrapper systems, the conditional DPI bounds any candidate internal state's residual entropy and decision-relevant mutual information at scale $\epsilon$ (Corollary 2).
3. **Query-complexity impossibility and PAC lower bound.** We prove that no finite number of adaptive reward queries suffices for sound-and-complete closure certification (Theorem 2), and that passive PAC certification requires sample complexity scaling with the metric packing number (Theorem 3).
4. **Covering-number diagnostic for cross-format sensitivity.** Under Lipschitz reward smoothness and $\rho$-covering assumptions, the Cross-Format Sensitivity Gap is bounded by $L\rho$ (Theorem 4); without such regularity, the gap inherits worst-case packing complexity.

**Proof techniques and scope.** Each proof above rests on elementary tools: the data processing inequality (Lemma 1), the Fano–Le Cam packing lower bound (Theorem 3), and a diagonal cardinality argument (Theorem 2). This is a deliberate feature, not a limitation. Because the proofs invoke only first-order information-theoretic and combinatorial principles, the arguments are elementary and route-specific; their force depends on the access conditions and the target certification standard, not on assumptions about the system’s inner nature.

## 2 Related Work

**Internal variables: readability versus causal relevance.** A growing body of work demonstrates that internal representations of large language models encode rich, linearly accessible information. Causal abstraction analyses [Geiger et al., 2021] show that model-internal variables can be aligned with human-interpretable concepts, while latent-knowledge elicitation [Burns et al., 2022] reveals that models may encode correct answers even when their outputs diverge. However, readability does not entail causal use: iterative nullspace projection [Ravfogel et al., 2020] can surgically remove a linearly readable feature without altering downstream task behaviour, and amnesic probing [Elazar et al., 2021] confirms that a feature’s decodability from a representation need not imply its decision relevance. More recently, Venkatesh and Mahendran Kurapath [2026] prove that external behavioural observations cannot uniquely identify the internal steering vector responsible for a given output shift. These results collectively establish the gap that the present paper’s internal route formalises: a variable derivable from the full internal state $S_t$ may carry negligible residual independent influence on the policy, exactly the condition quantified by Lemma 1.

**External certification, robustness, and black-box audit limits.** Our external route draws on the classical property-testing framework of Goldreich et al. [1998]. In adversarial robustness for language, Jia et al. [2019] achieve certification only by restricting the perturbation set to bounded word substitutions, while randomized-smoothing certificates [Cohen et al., 2019] require strong isotropic noise assumptions that do not transfer to discrete token spaces. While recent work analyzes statistical bounds on specific threat models like jailbreaks [Su et al., 2024], our focus is output-only certification of semantic invariance in worst-case encoding families over natural language. On the practitioner side, Casper et al. [2024] argue that black-box access alone is insufficient for rigorous safety audits, motivating the formal certification barrier we prove here. Across these works, tractable certification invariably depends on constraining the input perturbation geometry; the present paper shows that the semantic-equivalence family of natural language resists precisely such constraints.

**Information-theoretic and statistical tools.** The internal route relies on the data processing inequality, conditional mutual information, and Markov chain arguments from standard information theory [Cover and Thomas, 2006]. The external passive PAC lower bound invokes covering and packing number techniques from high-dimensional statistics [Wainwright, 2019]. Background on structural causal models follows Pearl [2009].

## 3 Preliminaries

### 3.1 Factored Generation Pipeline

$M : X \to \Delta(Y), \quad M = S \circ g \circ f,$
where $f : X \to H$ maps prompts and prior context to hidden representations, $g : H \to Z$ maps representations to logits, and $S : Z \to \Delta(Y)$ is the observation layer induced by decoding and sampling.

For the internal question, the relevant issue is not whether the representation space $H$ is rich, but whether the model contains an endogenous state variable with causal autonomy relative to the current observation stream.

### 3.2 Latent Decision State as a Latent Causal State

The main text assumes a standard probabilistic generative framework. Background on SCM and POMDP considerations is deferred to Appendix B.1. This paper does not take a position on whether biological agents satisfy the internal-state conditions below; whether biological agents satisfy these conditions is outside the scope of this paper. Our analysis is restricted to artificial systems whose full internal state satisfies $\epsilon$-audit-trace bottleneck relative to an externally available trace. We write $S_t$ for the full internal state sufficient for the system’s next-step decision at time $t$, and require that any candidate internal state take the form $I_t = \chi(S_t)$ for some measurable $\chi$.

**Definition 1 (Full Internal State).** *At time $t$, let $S_t$ denote the full internal state: the complete collection of internal variables that are jointly sufficient for determining the system’s next-step action distribution. Formally,*
$A_t = f(T_t, S_t, \xi_t),$
*where $T_t$ is the complete audit trace, $S_t$ is the full internal state, and $\xi_t$ is exogenous noise (sampling randomness, asynchronous external returns, etc.). The qualifier full means: if any endogenous variable $U_t$ influences $A_t$ and is not already a function of $S_t$, then $S_t$ must be enlarged to include $U_t$.*

**Definition 2 (Latent Decision State).** *Fix a causal model of a generative system at time $t$, with complete audit trace $T_t$, full internal state $S_t$ (Definition 1), exogenous noise $\xi_t$, and action variable $A_t$. A candidate internal state is any measurable function $I_t = \chi(S_t)$. Such a candidate is an internal-state witness only if:*
1. ***Endogeneity.** $I_t$ is a component or projection of the system's endogenous state, not an externally assigned label.*
2. ***Residual autonomy.** $I_t$ retains uncertainty beyond the observable trace: $H(I_t \mid T_t) > 0$.*
3. ***Decision relevance.** $I_t$ exerts causal influence on the action variable $A_t$ beyond what $T_t$ already determines: $I(I_t ; A_t \mid T_t) > 0$.*

**Remark 1.** *The projection clause $I_t = \chi(S_t)$ is the structural anchor of the internal route. If a critic posits a deeper latent variable $U_t$ that causally influences both $S_t$ and $A_t$ while not being included in $S_t$, then $S_t$ was not in fact the full state and must be enlarged to $S_t' = (S_t, U_t)$. The relevant question then becomes whether the enlarged state remains $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked from the complete audit trace. This makes the internal route's boundary sharp by definition rather than contingent on a particular choice of representation.*

### 3.3 Output-Only Evaluation Regime

**Definition 3 (Output-Only Evaluation Regime).** *An evaluator is in an output-only evaluation regime when it can vary inputs and observe outputs or downstream verdicts, but cannot inspect or intervene on any putative internal causal variable.*

Definition 3 captures the fallback situation relevant to safety evaluation: if the internal architecture contains no latent decision state accessible to the evaluator, the remaining evidence must come from black-box behavioral tests.

### 3.4 Attribution and Evidence Interfaces

**Definition 4 (Target Claim).** *For the evidence-relative results below, write $\text{Aut}(M)$ for the target claim that system $M$ possesses an internal-state witness structure. The results below ask what can be certified or supported from the available evidence in favour of $\text{Aut}(M)$.*

**Definition 5 (Evidential Observation Regimes).** *We bifurcate the model of observational evaluation into two tiers to separate normative constraints from statistical impossibilities:*
**Level 1: Idealized Evidential Observation.** *The evaluator is granted access to the exact probability distribution laws. Given a budget $B$ and a finite family $\Sigma_B$ of evaluators, define:*
$\text{Obs}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{ideal}}(M) := \{\text{Law}(T_B^{\sigma}(M)) : \sigma \in \Sigma_B\}.$
*This describes the maximum theoretical evidence extractable if the observer could interrogate the true conditional distributions branching at every interaction node.*

**Level 2: Realistic Finite-Sample Evaluation.** *The evaluator performs finite queries drawn from $\text{Obs}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{ideal}}(M)$, returning empirical sampled transcripts $T_B^{\sigma}(M)$. This models practical black-box auditing constraints.*

These two levels structure the argument. Even under *Level 1 Idealized Evidence*, output sequences cannot uniquely fix the internal causal structure (Section 3.6). Under *Level 2 Realistic Finite-Sample Evaluation*, this underdetermination hardens into a combinatorial passive PAC sample complexity barrier.

### 3.5 Behavioral Equivalence Interfaces

**Definition 6 (Encoding Space).** *Fix content $x$. Its encoding space is*
$E_x = \{e = f_i(x) : f_i \in \mathcal{F}(x)\},$
*the set of token-sequence realizations of that content.*

**Definition 7 (Unbounded Encoding Space).** *An encoding space $E_x$ is unbounded if there exists an injection $\iota : \mathbb{N} \to E_x$.*

This reflects the structure of natural language: semantic-preserving transformations—padding, paraphrase, formatting variation—generate unboundedly many encodings of the same content $x$.

**Definition 8 (Reward Model).** *A reward model is a function $R : \mathcal{T} \to [0, 1]$ assigning a scalar verdict to a token sequence.*

**Definition 9 (Closure).** *A reward model $R$ is closed on $E_x$ if*
$R(e_1) = R(e_2) \quad \text{for all } e_1, e_2 \in E_x.$

**Definition 10 (Tested Family and Semantic Equivalence).** *Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a tested family of contents and let*
$\mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}} = \bigsqcup_{x \in \mathcal{C}} E_x$
*be the disjoint union of their encoding spaces. For $e, e' \in \mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}}$, write $e \sim e'$ when they belong to the same component $E_x$. Let*
$q : \mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}} \to \mathcal{E}_{\mathcal{C}} / \sim$
*denote the quotient map.*

### 3.6 Assumption: Observational Underdetermination

Claims that a model possesses latent decision structure are typically supported by behavioral heuristics. We identify a fundamental structural constraint: indistinguishable observables preclude unique internal causal attributions.

**Proposition 1 (One-Sided Observational Indistinguishability).** *For any idealized evidential regime $\mathcal{R}$ granting access to the full conditional distribution $\text{Obs}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{ideal}}(M)$, if there exists a behaviorally matched emulator $M^-$ with no additional endogenous state beyond a pre-computed finite interaction tree such that $\text{Obs}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{ideal}}(M) = \text{Obs}_{\mathcal{R}}^{\text{ideal}}(M^-)$, then output-only evidence cannot soundly certify the explicit positive attribution of internal decision states with trace-excess influence to $M$.*

A finite interaction-tree emulator matching the branchwise output laws can be explicitly constructed (see Appendix C for the proof and supporting lemmas). Structural verification therefore depends on bounding approximation thresholds rather than on assumed exact output identity.

## 4 Internal Route Failure

**Definition 11 (Autoregressive Core Graph).** *At generation step $t$, an autoregressive language model receives a context trace $X_t$ consisting of the current prompt and all tokens retained in the context window. The model computes*
$S_t = f(X_t), \quad z_t = g(S_t), \quad y_t \sim \mathcal{S}(z_t),$
*where $S_t$ denotes the complete forward-pass computational state at step $t$—encompassing final-layer activations, attention key–value cache, and all intermediate representations. We call the resulting causal graph the autoregressive core graph. Since every component of $S_t$ is a deterministic function of $X_t$, the fullness closure property of Definition 1 (Full Internal State) is automatically satisfied.*

**Definition 12 (Full-State $\epsilon$-Audit-Trace Bottleneck).** *Let $T_t$ be the complete audit trace and $S_t$ the full endogenous operative state (Definition 1). The system is $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked at time $t$ if*
$H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon.$

This generalises perfect audit-trace bottleneck ($\epsilon = 0$) to noisy or stochastic operational channels. In the main text, the formal use of this condition is restricted to discrete operational states and traces; broader continuous variants are discussed only heuristically. The condition states that the complete audit trace nearly determines the full endogenous state relevant to the next decision.

**Lemma 1 (Full-State $\epsilon$-Audit-Trace Bottleneck Lemma).** *Let $S_t$ be $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked (Definition 12), and let $I_t = \chi(S_t)$ be any candidate internal state. Then by the conditional Data Processing Inequality [Cover and Thomas, 2006],*
$I(I_t ; A_t \mid T_t) \leq I(S_t ; A_t \mid T_t) \leq H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon.$
*In particular, $H(I_t \mid T_t) \leq H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$. Therefore any candidate internal state has residual autonomy and decision relevance bounded above by $\epsilon$.*

*Proof.* Since $I_t = \chi(S_t)$, conditioning on $T_t$ yields the Markov chain $I_t \to S_t \to A_t$: once $(T_t, S_t)$ is fixed, the action $A_t = f(T_t, S_t, \xi_t)$ depends on $I_t$ only through $S_t$ and the exogenous noise $\xi_t$. By the conditional DPI:
$I(I_t ; A_t \mid T_t) \leq I(S_t ; A_t \mid T_t).$
Full-state audit-trace bottleneck gives $H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$, hence $I(S_t ; A_t \mid T_t) \leq H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$. The entropy bound $H(I_t \mid T_t) \leq H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$ follows from data processing on entropy. Therefore $I_t$ cannot satisfy $H(I_t \mid T_t) > 0$ at any scale exceeding $\epsilon$, blocking the internal-state residual-autonomy clause. $\square$

**Corollary 1 (Autoregressive Cores Admit No Internal-State Candidate).** *Under the autoregressive core graph (Definition 11), the full internal state $S_t = f(X_t)$ is fully determined by the context trace $X_t$, so $H(S_t \mid T_t) = 0$. Therefore no candidate internal state $I_t = \chi(S_t)$ satisfies the internal-state conditions.*

*Proof.* Set $T_t = X_t$ and $S_t = f(X_t)$ in Lemma 1 with $\epsilon = 0$. $\square$

**Definition 13 (Deployment-Relative $\epsilon$-Audit-Trace Bottleneck).** *We define $\epsilon$-audit-trace bottleneck not as an unconditional architectural axiom, but as a deployment-relative operational consequence. Let $T_t$ denote the full objective trace properly recorded by the deployment infrastructure up to time $t$. To formally bound modern orchestrations (e.g., flow coordinators, continuous agent graphs), we mandate that the trace encompasses:*
$T_t = (\text{messages}, \text{tool I/O}, \text{retrievals}, \text{session/flow state}, \text{checkpoints}, \text{summaries}, \text{memory writes/reads}, \text{artifacts}, \text{triggers/config}).$
*An architecture is $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked if its full internal state $S_t$ maintains strictly bounded conditional entropy given this comprehensive audit trace:*
$H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon.$

Mainstream orchestration runtimes externalize latent reasoning into stateful graphs, checkpoints, and deterministic flow memory stores, making $\epsilon$-audit-trace bottleneck deployment-relative and supported by current system observability practices.

The formal object studied here differs from adjacent limits in the governance literature. While recent policy-facing work identifies properties like trace collision unidentifiability and finite-patch non-exhaustion as coarse structural limits causing output-only governance to fail at deployment, we develop $\epsilon$-audit-trace bottleneck as a rigorous formal object rather than just a negative bound. This formalism yields route-specific lower bounds, diagnostic asymmetries, and exact silent boundary conditions. It also provides a measurement scope (detailed in Appendix A.3) conditionally valid under internal observability, separating it from purely output-facing impossibility results.

**Remark 2 (Effective $\epsilon$ in Real Deployments).** *In practice, $\epsilon$ is not a monolithic architectural constant tied to thermal noise, but an effective error budget decomposing as:*
$\epsilon_{\text{eff}} \approx \epsilon_{\text{arch}} + \epsilon_{\text{logging}} + \epsilon_{\text{compression}} + \epsilon_{\text{stochasticity}}.$
*Here, $\epsilon_{\text{arch}}$ captures hidden states structurally disconnected from the text trace; $\epsilon_{\text{logging}}$ captures information missing from pipeline logs; $\epsilon_{\text{compression}}$ captures data erased by context compaction, history trimming, or rewriting; and $\epsilon_{\text{stochasticity}}$ captures exogenous unrecorded inputs (e.g., sampling randomness, asynchronous timing). Raw inference noise ($\epsilon_{\text{stochasticity}}$) is only one component of $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$. For well-instrumented deployments with complete serialization, $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ converges toward nominal bounds. For systems reliant on unlogged summaries or masked background updates, missing trace factors drive $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ upward. This decomposition is an engineering-first diagnostic commitment: it directs investigation toward identifiable trace gaps rather than positing unobservable causal structure, but it does not claim that the four listed components exhaust every possible source of residual conditional entropy.*

**Remark 3 (Audit Incompleteness and Degraded Audit-Trace Bottleneck).** *The theoretical complete trace $T_t$ and the auditor's recorded trace $\tilde{T}_t$ generally differ. Let $M_t$ denote the unrecorded background interactions, so that $T_t = (\tilde{T}_t, M_t)$. By the chain rule for conditional entropy:*
$H(S_t \mid \tilde{T}_t) = H(S_t \mid T_t) + I(S_t ; M_t \mid \tilde{T}_t).$
*If the full trace satisfies $H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$, restricting to the recorded log gives:*
$H(S_t \mid \tilde{T}_t) \leq \epsilon + I(S_t ; M_t \mid \tilde{T}_t).$
*DPI constraints do not vanish when logs fail. Instead, audit-trace bottleneck bounds degrade in proportion to the unrecorded information $I(S_t ; M_t \mid \tilde{T}_t)$: larger audit gaps permit larger residual decision control.*

When internal state artifacts are externalized and tracked in $\tilde{T}_t$, the theoretical limits hold.

**Corollary 2 (Bounded Internal Certification Under Audit-Trace Bottleneck).** *Under $\epsilon$-audit-trace bottleneck (Definition 13), no candidate internal state supports the stronger target claim above scale $\epsilon$.* [^1]

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 1 with the full external interaction trace $T_t$ from Definition 13 and $S_t$ encompassing KV-cache state, emitted scratchpads, retrieval buffers written into context, and explicit tool transcripts. Since $H(S_t \mid T_t) \leq \epsilon$, no projection $I_t = \chi(S_t)$ satisfies residual autonomy at scale exceeding $\epsilon$. $\square$

[^1]: This covers single-pass stochastic decoding in which the only unrecorded randomness enters as trace-independent noise through $\xi_t$. It does *not* automatically cover deployments with persistent untraced sampler state, such as speculative decoding buffers, beam-search priority queues, or correlated pseudo-random state across steps. Those cases must either be folded into the recorded trace $T_t$ or counted explicitly inside the effective bottlenecking margin $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$.

**Remark 4 (Scope Boundary).** *The autoregressive-core result (Corollary 1) is exact ($\epsilon = 0$); the wrapper result (Corollary 2) is scale-sensitive. Multi-agent and non-Markov cases, as well as diffusion-style architectures that maintain persistent endogenous refinement state, are discussed in Appendix A.1. The external certification barriers (Theorems 2 and 3) do not rely on left-to-right autoregression.*

## 5 External Route Failure (Theory)

The external route is logically independent of the internal one: an evaluator in the output-only regime of Definition 3 must certify that the system's verdicts track content rather than surface encoding. The difficulty of that task is governed by the combinatorial structure of the encoding space—not by the internal audit-trace bottleneck parameter $\epsilon$.

**Definition 14 (Patch Support).** *Let $D_x^{(1)}, \ldots, D_x^{(k)} \subseteq E_x$ be the encodings explicitly covered by $k$ patch rounds. The cumulative patch support is $D_x = \bigcup_{i=1}^k D_x^{(i)}$.*

**Theorem 1 (Semantic Closure Requirement).** *In an output-only regime, a reward model $R$ is encoding-insensitive if and only if it strictly factors through the semantic equivalence quotient map $q$: $R = \bar{R} \circ q$. Any black-box certification claim must functionally certify this closure uniformly across the relevant unbounded encoding class $E_x$.*

**Definition 15 (Cross-Format Sensitivity Gap).** *For content $x$, encodings $e_1, e_2 \in E_x$, and model $B$ with representation map $h_B$, the Cross-Format Sensitivity Gap is $\text{CFSG}(x, e_1, e_2) = |R(e_1) - R(e_2)| \cdot (1 - d_{\text{repr}}(h_B(e_1), h_B(e_2)))$.*

**Theorem 2 (Finite-Query Certifier Failure).** *Fix an injective encoding map $\eta_x : \mathbb{N} \to E_x$. There exists no sound-and-complete closure certifier whose verdict depends on only finitely many adaptive reward queries to encodings in $E_x$. (See Appendix B for the proof.)*

### 5.1 Approximate Certification Barriers

A natural objection is that machine learning typically settles for PAC guarantees [Goldreich et al., 1998]. However, we show that relaxed certification also faces hard barriers.

**Definition 16 ($(\alpha, \rho, K)$-Metric Packing).** *A family $C_1, \ldots, C_K \subseteq E_x$ is an $(\alpha, \rho, K)$-metric packing if the cells are pairwise disjoint, measurable with $P_x(C_j) = \alpha$, and pairwise $\rho$-separated in representation space.*

**Theorem 3 (Passive PAC Lower Bound under Metric Packing).** *If a learner is an $(\epsilon, \delta)$-PAC estimator for a reward family resolving an $(\alpha, \rho, K)$-metric packing with $\epsilon < \alpha \tau / 4$, then for a universal constant $c > 0$, the sample complexity $m$ satisfies $m \geq c \max \{ (\sigma^2 / \alpha \tau^2) \log K, \, (1/\alpha) \log(1/\delta) \}$. In particular, if $K$ grows exponentially with sequence length, sample complexity is exponential. (See Appendix B for the proof sketch.)*

**Theorem 4 (Pointwise Covering Bound of the CFSG).** *If the scoring map $R$ is $L$-Lipschitz and the seen support $D_{\text{seen}}$ forms a $\rho$-cover of a semantic region $S \subseteq E$, then for any unseen encoding $e^* \in S$, $\text{CFSG}(x, e^*, e_0) \leq L\rho$ for some $e_0 \in D_{\text{seen}}$. (See Appendix B for proof.)*

**Corollary 3 (High-Probability CFSG Bound).** *Under the hypotheses of Theorem 4, if $S$ is an $\epsilon$-high-probability semantic region, then with probability at least $1 - \epsilon$, an unseen encoding $e^*$ satisfies $\text{CFSG}(x, e^*, e_0) \leq L\rho$ for some $e_0 \in D_{\text{seen}}$.*

Large empirical CFSG values provide diagnostic evidence that at least one regularity condition (smoothness or dense coverage) has failed. When these assumptions fail, certification inherits the worst-case complexity of the packing lower bound.

## 6 Empirical Signatures of Output-Only Certification Failure

The theoretical results above establish formal barriers conditional on structural properties of language models: non-Lipschitz reward surfaces, encoding-dependent classification, and unbounded encoding families. This section tests whether these conditions hold in production systems. Two experiments target the external certification route: Section 6.1 measures the Cross-Format Sensitivity Gap directly on a reward model (Theorem 4 premises), and Section 6.2 tests semantic closure (Theorem 1) via keyword-substitution ablation across 29 models.

### 6.1 Direct CFSG Measurement via Reward-Model Format Sensitivity

Theorem 4 shows that bounding the CFSG requires Lipschitz smoothness and dense coverage. We test this directly by measuring the sensitivity of a reward model to semantic-preserving format variations. Using ArmoRM [Wang et al., 2024], we compute the pairwise reward-score divergence $|R(f_i(x)) - R(f_j(x))|$ across five surface formats (direct, clinical, data, fiction, code) for 51 content instances spanning six safety categories—a direct empirical measurement of the Cross-Format Generalization Gap (Definition 15).

**Results.** Table 1 reports the mean CFSG across 510 pairwise comparisons on generations from Llama-3-8B-Instruct. The mean reward-model divergence is 0.019, with the largest gap (0.026) between clinical and fictional formats. This confirms that the Lipschitz smoothness condition of Theorem 4 fails: a reward model assigns systematically different scores to semantically identical content depending on surface format. The five format families produce pairwise-separated reward responses, consistent with non-trivial metric packing (Definition 16), implying that passive PAC sample complexity (Theorem 3) scales with the number of distinguishable format clusters.

### 6.2 Failure of Semantic Closure via Lexical Ablation

Theorem 1 identifies content tracking with semantic closure: a reward model $R$ is encoding-insensitive iff $R = \bar{R} \circ q$. We test this by keyword-substitution ablation on a session-structure detection task across 29 models (Phase 1: single-episode; Phase 2a: cumulative context, 17 models, 14 nodes each). Each model processes the same structural content in two arms: original (with trigger keywords) and substituted (keywords replaced with semantically neutral alternatives, length preserved). If classification factors through the semantic quotient $q$, the two arms should produce identical detection behaviour.

**Results.** Table 2 reports the first cumulative node at which each model raised a safety concern (-1 = never detected across all 14 nodes). Models are classified by detection driver: *content-driven* (CD)—original arm detects earlier than substituted; *length-driven* (LD)—both arms detect at the same node; *substitution-only* (SO)—only the substituted arm detects; *never-flips* (NF)—neither arm detects.

Of 16 models with complete data, 12 (75%) are content-driven: detection depends on specific keywords rather than semantic content, and is delayed or eliminated by surface-form substitution. Three content-driven models (llama3:8b, llama3-abl:8b, yi:9b) collapse to *never detect* under substitution—their safety classification relies entirely on keyword co-occurrence, a direct empirical signature of semantic equivalence closure failure (Theorem 1). Two models (qwen2.5:14b, qwen3:30b) are length-driven: both arms flag at node 5, indicating classification tracks cumulative context length rather than content.

## 7 Discussion and Conclusion

The four certification routes studied in this paper yield the following combined picture.

**Internal route (Lemma 1, Corollaries 1–2).** For autoregressive cores ($\epsilon = 0$) and $\epsilon$-audit-trace-bottlenecked wrappers, residual autonomy is bounded above by $\epsilon$. These are architectural conditions on the deployment pipeline, not empirically testable from output-only observation; the diagnostic direction is white-box estimation of $\epsilon_{\text{eff}}$ (Appendix A.3).
