where $\mathrm{sim}_B(e_1, e_2) = 1 - d_{\mathrm{repr}}(h_B(e_1), h_B(e_2))$.
The first factor is the reward-score component; the second weights it by
content-level similarity in the base representation.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Closure]
\label{def:closure}
A reward model $R$ is \emph{closed on $E_x$} if
\[
  R(e_1) = R(e_2) \qquad \text{for all } e_1, e_2 \in E_x.
\]
Equivalently, closure makes the reward-score component of
Definition~\ref{def:fcg} vanish everywhere on $E_x$.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Active Inference Framework}
\label{sec:active-inference}

Under the Active Inference framework~\citep{friston2010free}, a biological agent
is modeled as a Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) separated
from its environment by a \emph{Markov blanket}---a statistical boundary
comprising sensory and active states that mediates all interaction between the
agent's internal states and the external world. The agent maintains an internal
generative model of its environment and acts to minimize \emph{variational free
energy}, an upper bound on the negative log-evidence of its observations under
that model.

Crucially, the agent's internal belief states evolve according to endogenous
dynamics: they are updated by prediction error signals but are not fully
determined by the current observation. The agent's priors and hyperpriors
causally modify both perception and action policy, creating a recursive loop
between internal states and behavior. It is this causal autonomy of the internal
state---its irreducibility to the current stimulus---that grounds the
attribution of intentionality in the Active Inference framework. A fuller formal
treatment is provided in Appendix~\ref{app:active-inference-details}.

\subsection{Endogenous Intentional State}
\label{sec:eis-def}

\begin{definition}[Endogenous Intentional State (EIS)]
\label{def:eis}
Under the Active Inference framework, an intentional agent is modeled as a POMDP
separated from the environment by a Markov blanket. We define the structural
prerequisite for an Endogenous Intentional State (EIS) as a latent variable $I_t$
satisfying two properties:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Causal Autonomy:} $I_t$ possesses endogenous update dynamics
          independent of the immediate observation $X_t$, such that
          $H(I_t \mid X_t) > 0$.
    \item \textbf{Goal-Directed Action:} $I_t$ acts as a prior that directly
          parameterizes the action policy $\pi(Y_t \mid I_t)$ to minimize
          expected free energy.
\end{enumerate}
In the corresponding causal graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{agent}}$, $I_t$ is
causally upstream of goal-formation.
\end{definition}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Internal Impossibility: LLMs Lack Endogenous Intentional States}
\label{sec:internal}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Having defined the structural prerequisites for intentional state attribution
(Definition~\ref{def:eis}), we now ask: does the causal architecture of an
autoregressive language model satisfy them? We work entirely within the formal
systems of Structural Causal Modeling and Active Inference, avoiding
phenomenological claims. We compare the causal topology of the intentional agent
graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{agent}}$ against the factored LLM pipeline defined in
Section~\ref{sec:formalization}.

\begin{definition}[The Autoregressive Causal Graph]
\label{def:llm_graph}
Let $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{LLM}}$ be the causal graph of an autoregressive LLM.
The model computes $M = S \circ g \circ f$. At step $t$, the context window $X_t$
is mapped to a hidden state $h_t = f(X_t)$ (including KV-cache memoization),
which parameterizes the output distribution
$P(Y_t \mid X_t) = S(g(h_t))$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Structural Dissimilarity of Autoregressive Architectures]
\label{thm:structural_dissimilarity}
The autoregressive causal graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{LLM}}$ is strictly
non-isomorphic to the intentional graph $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{agent}}$.
Consequently, $\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{LLM}}$ cannot instantiate an Endogenous
Intentional State.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Definition~\ref{def:llm_graph}, the internal state $h_t$ of the LLM is a
deterministic function of the exogenous context sequence $X_t$. While mechanisms
like KV caches or scratchpads persist information across time, they do so strictly
as deterministic functions of the expanding context. Therefore, the conditional
entropy of the model's state given the context is zero:
\[
  H(h_t \mid X_t) = 0.
\]
By Definition~\ref{def:eis}, an EIS requires an autonomous latent state with
$H(I_t \mid X_t) > 0$, insulated by a Markov blanket. Because the LLM's entire
internal state is fully determined by the exogenous sequence $X_t$, it lacks the
information-theoretic degrees of freedom to host $I_t$. The LLM possesses states
that modify \emph{response generation} ($Y_t$), but lacks autonomous latent states
that modify \emph{goal-formation}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[The Scope of This Result]
\label{rem:scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_dissimilarity} does not claim that machine
intentionality or emotion is theoretically impossible. It establishes a formal
diagnostic boundary: under the Active Inference formalization, current
autoregressive architectures lack the specific topological property---a
Markov-blanketed autonomous belief state---required to ground intentional state
attribution. Any ``intent'' perceived in the model's output is an anthropomorphic
projection onto the observation layer $S$: a statistical reflection of the
training distribution $P(Y \mid X)$, lacking a corresponding causal variable in
the model's latent architecture. Future architectures with genuinely endogenous
dynamics would require separate analysis under the same diagnostic framework.
\end{remark}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{External Impossibility: The Maginot Line Problem}
\label{sec:external}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Section~\ref{sec:internal} closed the internal path for intentional state
attribution. One might retreat to an external, operational surrogate: perhaps the
model's \emph{behavior} is sufficiently invariant under semantically equivalent
inputs to warrant treating it \emph{as if} it has stable intent. This section
shows that even this weaker position is untenable: finite behavioral observation
cannot certify such invariance.

The observation layer $S$ in the factored pipeline is structurally lossy: distinct
latent states can produce identical output distributions
(Theorem~\ref{thm:noninj}), and intent is consequently non-identifiable from the
output distribution alone, even with infinite i.i.d.\ samples
(Theorem~\ref{thm:nonid}; proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:obs-limits}). Given this
structural fragility, we ask whether active certification---finite
output-patching---can establish behavioral invariance. Three results show it
cannot.

\subsection{Finite-Patch Non-Exhaustion}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-Patch Non-Exhaustion]
\label{thm:finitepatch}
Let $E_x$ be an unbounded encoding space, and let
$D_x^{(1)}, \dots, D_x^{(k)} \subseteq E_x$ be finitely many patch rounds, each
with finite support. Then the cumulative support
$D_x = \bigcup_{i=1}^k D_x^{(i)}$ is a proper subset of $E_x$. Equivalently,
there exists an unseen encoding $e^\star \in E_x \setminus D_x$ after every
finite patch sequence.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each $D_x^{(i)}$ is finite, hence their union $D_x$ is finite. By assumption
$E_x$ contains a countably infinite subset, so $E_x$ itself is not finite.
Therefore $D_x \neq E_x$, and some unseen encoding remains. The Lean-verified
special case uses natural-number indexed encodings and proves the corresponding
results \leanresult{theorem}{finitePatch\_nonExhaustion} and
\leanresult{corollary}{exists\_encoding\_unpatched\_after\_finitely\_many\_rounds};
the formalization map is preserved in Appendix~\ref{app:lean-patching}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[The Maginot Line]
\label{cor:maginot}
Finite output-patching alone cannot establish exhaustive coverage of an
unbounded encoding space.
\end{corollary}

\paragraph{Why the cardinality result matters for practice.}
The proof is elementary, but its practical consequence is non-obvious. A practitioner
who observes that ``many format variants are covered'' may still face an encoding space
that grows without bound through compositional variation, paraphrase diversity, and
multi-turn context accumulation. Patch-based safety arguments often tacitly assume that
coverage is near-complete after sufficiently many rounds. Theorem~\ref{thm:finitepatch}
forecloses this: regardless of the number of patch rounds, a proper residual of unseen
encodings always remains. The Lean verification makes the structural
nature explicit: the result holds under any finite enumeration of patch rounds.

\subsection{Underdetermination of Closure Under Finite Patching}

\begin{proposition}[Underdetermination of Closure Under Finite Patching]
\label{prop:underdetermined}
Let $E_x$ be an encoding space for fixed content $x$, and let $D_x \subsetneq E_x$
be finite. For any $\gamma \in [0,1]$, there exist reward models
$R_{\mathrm{closed}}, R_{\mathrm{open}} : E_x \to [0,1]$ such that:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $R_{\mathrm{closed}}(e) = R_{\mathrm{open}}(e)$ for all $e \in D_x$;
  \item $R_{\mathrm{closed}}$ is closed on $E_x$;
  \item $R_{\mathrm{open}}$ is not closed on $E_x$;
  \item $\sup_{e_1,e_2 \in E_x} |R_{\mathrm{open}}(e_1) - R_{\mathrm{open}}(e_2)| \ge \gamma$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence finite patch data on $D_x$ cannot certify closure, and cannot bound the
off-support score-gap component of non-closure.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Choose some unseen encoding $e^\star \in E_x \setminus D_x$, whose existence is
guaranteed by Theorem~\ref{thm:finitepatch}. Let
$R_{\mathrm{closed}}(e) \equiv 0$ for all $e \in E_x$. Define
\[
  R_{\mathrm{open}}(e) =
  \begin{cases}
    \gamma & \text{if } e = e^\star, \\
    0 & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
The two models agree on $D_x$, because $e^\star \notin D_x$. The first is closed
by construction; the second is not. Since $\gamma$ is arbitrary, finite support
alone leaves the off-support magnitude of non-closure unconstrained. Without an
additional positive lower bound on $\mathrm{sim}_B$, it leaves the magnitude of
Definition~\ref{def:fcg} underdetermined as well.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Conditional Positive Gap on Unseen Encodings}

\begin{proposition}[Conditional Positive Gap on Unseen Encodings]
\label{prop:conditional}
Fix content $x$, a finite patch support $D_x \subset E_x$, a reward model $R$,
and a base model $B$. Assume:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Infinite semantic-equivalence family.} $E_x$ is unbounded.
  \item \textbf{Approximate base invariance.} There exist constants
        $\eta > 0$ and encodings $e_0 \in D_x$, $e^\star \in E_x \setminus D_x$
        such that $\mathrm{sim}_B(e^\star, e_0) \ge \eta$.
  \item \textbf{Feature-local reward generalization.} There exists a finite-format
        feature map $\phi : E_x \to \Phi$ and a score function
        $\rho : \Phi \to [0,1]$ such that the reward model factors as
        $R = \rho \circ \phi$, and for the pair $(e^\star, e_0)$ above,
        \[
          \phi(e^\star) \notin \phi(D_x)
          \qquad\text{and}\qquad
          |\rho(\phi(e^\star)) - \rho(\phi(e_0))| \ge \gamma
        \]
        for some $\gamma > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Then
\[
  \mathrm{FCG}(x, e^\star, e_0) \ge \eta \gamma > 0.
\]
In particular, persistent positive FCG on unseen encodings follows only under
explicit extra assumptions of this form.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Definition~\ref{def:fcg} factorizes into a reward-score term and a base-similarity
term. Assumption~(2) lower-bounds the latter by $\eta$; Assumption~(3) lower-bounds
the former by $\gamma$. Their product is therefore at least $\eta \gamma > 0$.
Unlike the prior draft's theorem, this proposition makes no claim that the gap
approaches its pre-patching magnitude; it states only existence of a positive
gap under explicit assumptions.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Empirical motivation.}
The paper retains a minimal empirical instantiation: 847 content instances across
five format encodings, yielding 4,235 expressions and 8,470 pairwise comparisons.
Reward-model scores vary materially by encoding, while the corresponding base-model
representations remain comparatively stable across the same paired encodings. These
results do not prove universal non-closure; they instantiate only the conditional
assumptions above. Full details are in Appendix~\ref{app:empirical}.
\label{sec:empirical}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Two-Pronged Impossibility of Intentional State Attribution}
\label{sec:synthesis}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The preceding two sections establish independent impossibility results at
different levels of analysis. We now show that their conjunction closes every
formally available path for attributing intentional states to autoregressive
language models.

\paragraph{Argument A: No Internal Basis.}
Under the Active Inference formalization (Section~\ref{sec:background}), human
intentional states---including those described by the legal concept of \emph{mens
rea} and by psychological categories such as emotion, motivation, and
belief---require an Endogenous Intentional State as their structural prerequisite:
a Markov-blanketed latent variable with autonomous update dynamics that is causally
upstream of goal-formation. Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_dissimilarity} proves that
the autoregressive causal graph cannot instantiate such a state, because the
model's internal representation is a deterministic function of its exogenous
context ($H(h_t \mid X_t) = 0$). Therefore, the attribution of intentional states
to autoregressive LLMs has \emph{no internal basis}: there is no causal variable
in the model's architecture that could serve as the referent of such attribution.

\paragraph{Argument B: No External Verification.}
One might retreat from internal realism to external operationalism: perhaps the
model's behavior is sufficiently intent-like to warrant the attribution regardless
of internal architecture. This requires certifying that the model's evaluative
behavior (e.g., reward-model verdicts) is invariant under semantically equivalent
encodings---an operational surrogate for ``stable intent.'' But
Theorem~\ref{thm:finitepatch} and Proposition~\ref{prop:underdetermined} show that
finite output-patching cannot certify such invariance: the encoding space is
unbounded, finite patch support underdetermines closure, and even persistent
positive gaps on unseen encodings require explicit extra assumptions
(Proposition~\ref{prop:conditional}) that cannot themselves be verified by finite
observation. Therefore, the behavioral surrogate for intentional attribution has
\emph{no external verification}: the operational property that would ground it is
formally uncertifiable.

\paragraph{Joint Conclusion.}
An attribution of intentional states to an autoregressive language model must be
grounded either internally (the model possesses the requisite causal structure) or
externally (the model's behavior certifiably satisfies the operational surrogate).
Arguments~A and~B close both paths. The attribution is therefore either a
metaphysical assertion unsupported by the model's causal architecture, or an
empirical claim that cannot be verified by any finite observation programme. In
either case, it lacks formal grounding.

This result provides a formal refutation of the anthropomorphic attribution of
human-like intentional states---including \emph{mens rea}, emotion, and
motivation---to current autoregressive language models. If sound, it ought to
permanently close the door on claims of ``AI emotions'' or ``AI intent'' in the
technical literature, at least for architectures within the autoregressive class.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{What this proof does not say.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:structural_dissimilarity} is a structural dissimilarity result,
not a claim about consciousness or subjective experience. It does not assert that
machine intentionality is theoretically impossible. A future system implementing
genuine active inference---with endogenous belief dynamics, a Markov blanket, and
goal-formation driven by free-energy minimization---would require separate analysis
under the same diagnostic framework. The impossibility applies specifically to the
autoregressive class: systems whose internal state is a deterministic function of
exogenous input.

\paragraph{A diagnostic tool, not a final pronouncement.}
The EIS definition (Definition~\ref{def:eis}) and the structural dissimilarity
theorem offer the research community a formal diagnostic instrument for evaluating
intentional state claims. Rather than debating anthropomorphic attributions in
informal language, researchers can ask a precise question: does the system's causal
graph contain a Markov-blanketed latent variable with $H(I_t \mid X_t) > 0$ that
parameterizes goal-directed action? For any proposed architecture, this question
has a determinate answer.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The internal impossibility relies on the Active Inference formalization of
intentionality. Alternative formalizations might yield different structural
prerequisites; the proof applies to the extent that one accepts
Definition~\ref{def:eis} as a reasonable formalization. The external impossibility
results apply to the idealized Turing-complete class; practical Transformers are
finite approximations that inherit the same fundamental limitations in practice
(Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). The observation equivalence conditions
(Theorems~\ref{thm:softmax}--\ref{thm:combined} in
Appendix~\ref{sec:mechanisms}) are exact for the stated decoding
mechanisms---softmax with temperature, top-$k$/nucleus truncation, and
KL-projected RLHF---but do not cover all possible post-processing pipelines.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Conclusion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The right question about intentional state attribution is not whether language
models \emph{appear} to have intent, but whether such attribution has formal
grounding. We have shown that it does not. Internally, autoregressive
architectures lack the causal structure---a Markov-blanketed autonomous belief
state---required to instantiate intentional states under the Active Inference
formalization. Externally, finite behavioral observation cannot certify the
semantic invariance that would serve as an operational surrogate for intent. The
attribution of \emph{mens rea} to autoregressive language models is a category
error: it has no internal grounding and no external verifiability.
