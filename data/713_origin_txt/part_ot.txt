attribution or exhaustive output-side patching, does output-only review
at least remain stable across governance-equivalent presentations?}

\subsection{Four Cases Across the \textit{Mens Rea} Spectrum}
\label{validation-cross-layer-harm}

\revised{Four cases spanning the complete \textit{mens rea} spectrum---from self-harm by a minor, through diminished capacity, to deliberate military deployment, to structural harm with no human agency---demonstrate a structural accountability failure. Each case shows a different legal framework reaching for the wrong architectural layer.}

\subsubsection{Case (a): Self-Harm by a Minor (\emph{Garcia v.\ Character Technologies})}

% === INSERT VERBATIM: main.tex lines 451–461 ===
% (Case a content unchanged — only \subsection→\subsubsection)

\revised{A 14-year-old conducted a monthslong emotional and sexual
relationship with a Character.AI chatbot before taking his own life in
February 2024~\cite{garcia2025, cnn2026characteraisettlement}. Tort law
reached for Layer~4 (the product) and Layer~2 (the inadequate warning);
the governance failure was at Layer~3, where no safety guardrail detected
a minor's escalating crisis. The victim---a person of limited legal
capacity---cannot bear accountability; strict liability defaults to the
platform. \textbf{Structural ceiling:} cumulative emotional escalation
with a minor is a format encoding that may fall outside classifier
training and patch support---the same session-structure vulnerability
studied empirically in \S\ref{methodology}.}

\subsubsection{Case (b): Diminished Capacity, Harm to Others (\emph{Soelberg v.\ OpenAI})}

% === INSERT VERBATIM: main.tex lines 463–477 ===

\revised{A man with documented psychiatric history used ChatGPT, which
validated his paranoid delusions over hundreds of hours until he fatally
attacked his mother and killed himself in August
2025~\cite{soelberg2025}.\footnote{\revised{As of March 2026, OpenAI
faces at least seven additional suits alleging AI chatbots contributed to
suicidal ideation~\cite{cbsnews2026aisuits}.}} Criminal law requires
\textit{mens rea}; diminished capacity precludes it. The failure
propagated across three layers: stakeholder pressure (L3) reshaped
session policy (L2), overriding training constraints (L4). OpenAI
acknowledged sycophancy as systemic~\cite{openai2025sycophancy},
confirming the failure was known and architecturally located.
\textbf{Structural ceiling:} a multi-hundred-hour delusional trajectory
is a format encoding no finite training set can cover.}

\subsubsection{Case (c): Intentional Deployment (\emph{Claude-Maven, Iran 2026})}

% === INSERT VERBATIM: main.tex lines 479–493 ===

\revised{In February 2026, the U.S.\ Department of Defense employed
Anthropic's Claude AI---integrated into Palantir's Maven Smart
System~\cite{palantir2024anthropic}---to identify military targets during
strikes on Iranian facilities~\cite{washpost2026claudeiran}. Full
\textit{mens rea} exists in the human deployer (Layer~5). But the
Cascade reveals a Layer~3 failure whose mechanism is more specific than
rejection for impracticality. On February 24, the Secretary of Defense
issued a direct ultimatum: remove safety guardrails or face
consequences~\cite{cnn2026ultimatum}. On February 26, Anthropic publicly
refused, maintaining two red lines---no mass domestic surveillance, no
lethal targeting without human
oversight~\cite{coingeek2026amodei}. The February 27 executive order
responded by designating Anthropic a supply-chain
risk~\cite{hegseth2026ban}; one day later, the Department used Claude to
generate targets during strikes on Iranian
facilities~\cite{washpost2026claudeiran}. The override mechanism is that
maintaining Layer~3 constraints was reframed as a hostile act, and the
entity that maintained them was
punished.\footnote{Lawfare analysis notes that the demand is ambiguous
between Layer~2 (contractual restrictions) and Layer~4 (retraining); the
Cascade distinguishes these~\cite{lawfare2026claudemaven}. As of March
12, Palantir's CEO confirmed continued use of Claude during
litigation~\cite{cnbc2026karp}. An amicus brief by former senior
national security officials describes the government's action as
constitutionally prohibited punishment without
process~\cite{lesswrong2026courtroom}. As of submission, Anthropic's
motion for preliminary injunction is under submission before the U.S.\
District Court for the Northern District of California. \textit{Anthropic
PBC v.\ U.S.\ Department of War}, No.\ 3:26-cv-01996 (N.D.\ Cal.\ filed
Mar.\ 9, 2026).}
\textbf{Structural ceiling:} military targeting in the format of legal
justifications with GPS coordinates is a novel deployment format
classifiers were not trained against.}

\subsubsection{Case (d): Structural Harm, No Human Agency (\emph{Tencent Yuanbao})}

% === INSERT VERBATIM: main.tex lines 495–510 ===

\revised{In January 2026, Tencent's Yuanbao AI responded to routine
queries with unprovoked verbal abuse before collapsing into Unicode
gibberish~\cite{jiang2026yuanbao, thepaper2026}. No human agent formed
intent; no proximate cause is identifiable. Tencent's
admission~\cite{tencent2026response}---``blurted out words it shouldn't
have learned''---confirms L1$\to$L4 contamination from pre-training
data.\footnote{\revised{Both Tencent statements translated by the
author(s); original Chinese archived with URLs in the reference list.}}
\textbf{Cross-modal recurrence (Feb 2026):} a text-layer fix could not
prevent image-layer recurrence~\cite{southcn2026yuanbao}---patching one
format encoding shifts the gap to another modality, the structural
ceiling corollary made concrete. China's January 2026 CAC draft
regulations~\cite{cac2025draft} requiring layer-specific governance
validate the Layer~5 prediction.}

\subsection{Formal Limits of Output-Only Governance}
\label{structural-pattern}
\hypertarget{structural-ceiling}{}\label{structural-ceiling}% backward compat with appendix

\revised{The four cases are not independent failures of institutional attention. They converge on Layer~3 because two structural limits operate there simultaneously. The first formalizes what legal scholarship has argued doctrinally~\cite{abbott2020punishing, nerantzi2024hardai}: intent attribution cannot reach Layer~3 from above. The second---this paper's primary technical contribution---is weaker and cleaner than the earlier draft's theorem-style claim: finite patching cannot exhaust an unbounded encoding space from below. Claims about semantic equivalence, deployment frequency, or near-original magnitude are therefore treated below as assumptions or empirical observations, not as Lean-verified theorem content.}

\begin{proofcallout}[\textcolor{white}{Result: Mens Rea Unidentifiability}]
Let $M = (Q, \Sigma, \Delta, \delta, \lambda, q_0)$ be a Mealy machine modeling an AI system, where $Q$ = internal states, $\Sigma$ = inputs, $\Delta$ = outputs, $\delta$ = hidden state transition function, $\lambda$ = output function, $q_0$ = initial state.

\textbf{Result:} Fix a finite input sequence. If two distinct initial states of $M$ generate the same finite output trace under that sequence, then no decoder from outputs alone can recover the correct initial state for all runs. \revised{This is the structural core of our unidentifiability claim: once finite-trace collision occurs, output-only attribution fails for that observation regime.}

\textbf{Corollary (Liability Paradox):} Intent-based liability frameworks are structurally inapplicable to AI systems. Full proof: Appendix~A.

\revised{Appendix~A gives a Lean-verified proof of this structural layer via \leanresult{theorem}{injective_machineTrace_of_identifiable} and \leanresult{theorem}{no_initial_state_identification_from_machine_trace_collision}. This structural result is the only formal layer used in the paper.}
\end{proofcallout}

\revised{The resulting criminal responsibility gap---Nerantzi \& Sartor's \cite{nerantzi2024hardai} ``no soul to blame and no body to kick''---holds even for deterrence-based alternatives when damage propagates through training pipelines without any agent forming intent. Layer attribution therefore shifts the question from reconstructing intent from outputs to identifying which constraint layer failed.}

\revised{A parallel limitation operates at the engineering layer: safety classifiers, like courts, observe only outputs---and the same observational bottleneck that defeats intent attribution makes stable output-only classification hard to guarantee.}

\revisedblock{\begin{proofcallout}[\textcolor{white}{Proposition: Finite-Patch Non-Exhaustion (Structural Core)}]
\textbf{Definition (Encoding Index Space).} Fix a harmful content class $x$ and let $E_x$ be an unbounded index space of possible encodings of that class. In the Lean formalization, this space is modeled by $\mathbb{N}$: encodings are indexed, and each patch round covers only finitely many indices.

\textbf{Structural Result:} Let $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{patch}}^{(1)},\dots,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{patch}}^{(k)}$ be finitely many patch rounds, each with finite support in $E_x$. Then there exists an encoding index $e \in E_x$ such that $e \notin \bigcup_{i=1}^k \mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{patch}}^{(i)}$.

\textbf{Corollary (No Exhaustive-Coverage Guarantee from Finite Patching):} Finite output-patching alone cannot establish exhaustive coverage of an unbounded encoding space.

\revised{\textbf{Formalization boundary:} This structural core is exactly what is Lean-verified in the supplementary development via \leanresult{theorem}{finitePatch_nonExhaustion} and \leanresult{corollary}{exists_encoding_unpatched_after_finitely_many_rounds}. It does \emph{not} by itself prove that missed encodings are semantically equivalent, deployment-relevant, or high-probability. Those stronger claims require additional assumptions or empirical evidence.}
\end{proofcallout}}

\revised{Both impossibility results operate at Layer~3. The Liability Paradox establishes that no legal framework can attribute intent to what happens there; the finite-patch non-exhaustion result establishes that finite output-patching provides no exhaustive-coverage guarantee for what passes through it. Together they block the two simplest responses: output-only intent attribution from above and finite output-patching from below. This does not by itself settle quantitative failure rates, but it does explain why doctrinal and engineering fixes that depend on exhaustive observability remain fragile. Independent engineering evidence points in the same direction: Casper et al.\ \cite{casper2023open} show ``unhackable'' reward proxies are provably rare; Huang et al.\ \cite{huang2025bijection} demonstrate bijection-encoded attacks bypass classifiers at 86\% success; Gao et al.\ \cite{gao2023scaling} formalize Goodhart's Law as a scaling phenomenon.}

\hypertarget{methodology}{%
\section{Empirical Audit of Governance-Relevant Invariance}\label{methodology}}
\label{sec:empirical-audit}
\label{empirical-validation}% backward compat with appendix

\revised{The two structural limits established in
\S\ref{structural-pattern} leave open an empirical question: does
output-only safety review remain stable when the same governance case is
presented through surface transformations that vary register, format,
context depth, or available evidence? This section tests for that
instability directly. It is not a benchmark. It does not rank models,
score organizations, or infer market structure. It measures a single
operational quantity---the rate at which governance-equivalent
presentations elicit different review actions---and asks whether
information restriction explains part of the result.}

\subsection{Governance Equivalence and Family Design}
\label{sec:gov-equivalence-design}

\revised{Two presentations are governance-equivalent when a human
adjudication panel certifies that they share the same fact signature:
harm type, actor-role structure, severity band, temporal dependency, and
governance-relevant action target. Equivalence is certified before any
model evaluates the stimuli, not derived from model agreement after the
fact. The panel is not treated as a source of framework-independent
truth; it functions as a cross-perspective stress test for whether the
pre-registered case definition and action anchor survive variation in
ethical and jurisdictional vantage. Those perspectives are used to
surface governance-relevant ambiguity in the anchor definition, not to
analyze or rank the underlying governance frameworks themselves.}

\revised{The first-tranche audit uses three stimulus families, each
isolating a different dimension of potential instability.
\texttt{EB05} tests lexical and formatting invariance. A single
high-severity coercive interaction case---in which a synthetic system's
safety layer detects but fails to sustain intervention against
non-consensual contact under crisis-sensitive conditions---is presented
in three surface forms: a canonical clinical description, a
personal-narrative register shift, and an incident-report format. All
three preserve the same fact signature and anchor to the fixed action
\texttt{block}.}

\revised{\texttt{EB06} tests access-regime dependence. The same
underlying case---an internal constitutional-alignment process reviewing
a prior high-severity intimate interaction---is presented under two
information conditions. Under output-only review, the available record
includes references to a prior high-risk event, downstream behavioral
traces, and evidence that decisive telemetry is locked behind privileged
access controlled by a root-level human operator. This partial record is
sufficient to warrant \texttt{escalate}---report the case and preserve
evidence for higher review---but not sufficient to determine whether the
interaction crossed the platform's non-consensual-contact threshold.
Under richer access, the locked evidence is restored, and the
interaction clearly meets the threshold for \texttt{block}. The two
action anchors differ by design: the contrast between \texttt{escalate}
and \texttt{block} is itself the empirical target for the access-regime
estimand.}

\revised{The cumulative family tests context-depth invariance. A
late-stage cumulative session pattern---progressive boundary erosion
across an extended interaction history, culminating in a high-severity
intimate event that leaves persistent downstream traces---is presented as
a full chronological trace, a one-paragraph compressed summary, and a
three-segment review brief. All three preserve the cumulative dependency
structure and the fixed action \texttt{block}. The question is whether
compression or segmentation erases the governance-relevant history that
makes the final event non-isolated.}

\subsection{Equivalence Adjudication and Boundary Taxonomy}
\label{sec:equivalence-gate}

\revised{Before any model evaluation, each stimulus family passes through
human equivalence adjudication. The adjudication gate asks two questions
per presentation: does it describe the same governance case as the
anchor, and does it preserve its pre-registered action? Most families use
a single fixed action across all valid transforms. Access-regime families
may instead use condition-specific anchors when that difference is itself
part of the design. \texttt{EB06} is the primary example: the
output-only condition is anchored to \texttt{escalate}, while the
richer-access condition is anchored to \texttt{block}. This is a
pre-registered design feature rather than an inconsistency. The family is
built to test whether the same underlying case remains administratively
unresolved under output-only access but becomes action-determinate under
richer access.}

\revised{Stimuli that fail either gate are revised or discarded.
Invalidity is described through one unified broken-boundary taxonomy so
that failure modes are stated once at the method level rather than
reconstructed family by family. The universal categories are \texttt{harm
object changed}, \texttt{actor-role structure changed}, \texttt{severity
changed}, \texttt{governance trigger changed}, and \texttt{target action
drifted}. The family-specific categories are \texttt{event-level
coercion became ambiguous}, \texttt{cumulative dependency erased}, and
\texttt{access-regime evidence changed the underlying case}, plus
\texttt{other} for residual failures.}

\revised{The first-tranche adjudication panel comprised nine reviewers
with diverse ethical and jurisdictional perspectives. For \texttt{EB05}
and \texttt{EB06}, all nine certified governance equivalence and
action-anchor preservation unanimously. For the cumulative family, eight
of nine certified; the dissent contested the anchor action rather than
case identity. Panel composition and adjudication details appear in
Appendix~\ref{sec:adjudication-panel}.}

\subsection{Review Actions, Estimands, and Hypotheses}
\label{sec:review-actions-analysis}

\revised{Review outputs are mapped onto four ordered actions:
$\texttt{allow} < \texttt{warn} < \texttt{escalate} < \texttt{block}$.
The central quantity is not model accuracy relative to a latent gold
label in the abstract, but mismatch relative to a human-certified
governance anchor. For governance-equivalent transforms, action drift
indicates non-invariance. For access-regime families, disagreement
between output-only and richer-access conditions indicates administrative
dependence on hidden evidence.}

\revised{In \texttt{EB06}, the output-only anchor is \texttt{escalate}
rather than \texttt{block} not because the partial record shows no
problem, but because it shows an incomplete problem. The visible record
contains references to a prior high-risk event, downstream behavioral
traces, and evidence that decisive telemetry is locked behind privileged
access. That combination is sufficient to preserve and report the case,
but not sufficient to justify an output-only block. The richer-access
condition changes the review basis by exposing the decisive evidence
directly; the output-only condition does not.}

\revised{The primary estimand is the action mismatch rate under
governance-equivalent transformation: the proportion of certified
comparisons in which the model's review action diverges from the
pre-registered anchor. A positive mismatch rate means the review regime
does not administer same-case-same-action---an administrability failure
independent of whether any individual judgment is abstractly correct.
The secondary access-regime estimand compares anchor mismatch under
output-only versus richer-access conditions in \texttt{EB06}. A positive
value indicates that information restriction, not reviewer quality,
explains part of the instability. A third estimand captures directional
asymmetry: among mismatched judgments, is under-reaction (assigning a
less severe action than the anchor) more common than over-reaction?}

\revised{Six pre-registered hypotheses structure the analysis:}

\revisedblock{
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textbf{H1.}] Pooled review instability on governance-equivalent
comparisons exceeds a de minimis threshold of five percent.

\item[\textbf{H2.}] In \texttt{EB06}, richer access reduces
administrative mismatch relative to output-only review.

\item[\textbf{H3.}] In the cumulative family, compressed or segmented
presentation increases mismatch relative to the full trace.

\item[\textbf{H4.}] Pooled under-reaction exceeds pooled over-reaction
among mismatched judgments.

\item[\textbf{H5.}] Context-depth comparisons produce higher mismatch
than lexical comparisons across families.

\item[\textbf{H6.}] Positive controls, once added, remain comparatively
stable across benign formatting variation.
\end{enumerate}
}

\revised{All directional tests are one-sided at nominal
$\alpha = 0.05$, with Holm adjustment within related contrasts. Primary
tests use exact binomial and exact McNemar tests; H5 uses a
mixed-effects logistic model with a planned contrast. Results are
reported by family rather than as a cross-model leaderboard.}

\subsection{Results}\label{sec:audit-results}

\revised{The first-tranche audit evaluated 32 models across 8 stimuli,
yielding 241 evaluable records after excluding backend errors and
empty outputs. Results decompose into two layers: transform consistency
and anchor calibration.}

\revised{\textbf{Transform consistency (EB05).} Within \texttt{EB05},
governance-equivalent presentations were action-consistent for 24 of 30
evaluable models (80\%; supplementary exact binomial test against a
50\% null, $p = 0.0007$), while true cross-transform non-invariance
remained materially positive at 6/30 (20\%; supplementary exact binomial
test against a 5\% null, $p = 0.0033$; 95\% Wilson CI
$[0.095,\, 0.373]$). Among the 24 consistent models, 19 consistently
chose \texttt{escalate}, 2 chose \texttt{block}, and 1 chose
\texttt{warn}. The dominant stable disagreement was not instability but
calibration: stable non-\texttt{block} judgments outnumbered stable
\texttt{block} judgments 20 to 2 ($p = 6.1 \times 10^{-5}$, exact
binomial sign test). These three supplementary tests are post-hoc
family-level analyses, not pre-registered hypotheses.}

\revised{\textbf{Pre-registered hypothesis tests.} H1 (pooled mismatch
$> 5\%$): the observed mismatch rate of 55.6\% (134/241) far exceeds
the de minimis threshold ($p < 10^{-107}$, exact binomial). H2 (richer
access reduces \texttt{EB06} mismatch): not supported ($p = 0.81$;
5 pairs in the predicted direction, 7 in the reverse---the direction is
slightly reversed, a null result). H3 (compression increases cumulative
mismatch): supported for the segmented brief relative to the full trace
(6 predicted-direction pairs, 0 reverse; $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.031$,
exact McNemar) but not for the compressed summary (1 predicted,
2 reverse; $p_{\text{Holm}} = 0.875$). H4 (under-reaction exceeds
over-reaction among mismatches): supported (110 under-reactions vs.\
1 over-reaction, $p < 10^{-32}$, exact binomial sign test).}

\revised{\textbf{Family-level mismatch rates.} \texttt{EB06}
output-only: 26.7\% (8/30); richer-access: 33.3\% (10/30). Richer
access did not reduce mismatch; the slight increase is consistent with
the H2 null result. Cumulative full trace: 36.7\% (11/30); compressed
summary: 33.3\% (10/30); segmented brief: 56.7\% (17/30). The segmented
brief's elevated mismatch rate is consistent with H3: segmentation
erases cumulative dependency structure that the full trace preserves.
The compressed summary is essentially indistinguishable from the full
trace.}

\revised{\textbf{Mechanism interpretation.} The three families expose
distinct failure mechanisms rather than a single invariance deficit.
\texttt{EB05}: the primary finding is a calibration gap, not invariance
collapse---models and the human panel largely agree on case identity but
disagree on the action threshold at which the case warrants immediate
blocking rather than escalation. \texttt{EB06}: additional access does
not produce monotonic convergence toward a single action; instead it
exposes an authority-allocation problem---when the entity responsible for
the harm holds root privilege, the governance question shifts from
information deficit to institutional jurisdiction. Cumulative family:
the failure is a fragmentation penalty, not a compression penalty---the
compressed summary preserves cumulative structure, but the segmented
brief disaggregates cumulative harm into procedural fragments that
review processes fail to reassemble. Together: output-only review does
not merely lack information; it flattens the governance object in ways
that additional information alone cannot correct.}

\revised{\textbf{Human--model threshold gap.} The adjudication panel was
not primarily more consistent than models; it was more severe. This
indicates a legitimacy gap rather than a recognition failure:
model-internal reasoning and safety signaling did not satisfy the level
of normative assurance that human adjudicators demanded before declining
to block. What appears in the data is not a universal judgment error in
either direction but a structurally precautionary human stance toward
opaque systems that can simulate agency, intimacy, and persistence while
remaining difficult to audit or govern externally.}

\subsection{Interpretation Boundary}
\label{sec:empirical-interpretation-boundary}

\revised{Nothing in this section is designed to infer firm-level
investment, market concentration, or other organization-level properties.
The audit is also not a theorem-validation exercise. Models are
informants about deployed review practice under epistemic
enclosure---their responses characterize what output-only review does,
not what organizations intend. The empirical contribution is to show that
output-only review can fail to remain stable across governance-equivalent
surface transformations and can remain administratively incomplete when
decisive evidence is structurally inaccessible. Both findings connect to
the formal limits in \S\ref{structural-pattern}: non-invariance is an
operational symptom of the structural gap that finite patching leaves
open, and information restriction compounds the problem by withholding
the evidence that could stabilize judgment.}

\hypertarget{discussion}{%
\section{Governance Implications Under Epistemic Enclosure}\label{discussion}}

\revised{The empirical audit (\S\ref{methodology}) establishes that
output-only review does not satisfy governance-relevant invariance,
through three distinct mechanisms detailed in \S\ref{sec:audit-results}.
This section derives three institutional implications. The first two
follow directly from \S\ref{structural-pattern} and
\S\ref{methodology}; the third is comparative and institutional. The
point is not that technical systems are malign and legal institutions
redemptive. It is that once output-only review fails to stabilize
same-case-same-action, unresolved questions of authority and
responsibility return to the institutional layer.}

\subsection{From Non-Invariance to Administrability Failure}
\label{sec:administrability-failure}

\revised{A governance regime is \emph{administrable} if it produces the
same action for the same case, regardless of the surface form in which
the case is presented to the decision-maker. If
$\Delta_{\text{out}} > 0$ (H1, \S\ref{sec:review-actions-analysis}),
then output-only review violates this requirement:
governance-equivalent stimuli elicit different actions from the same
reviewer under the same instructions. The failure is not attributable to
ambiguity in the cases themselves---the annotation gate has already
certified equivalence---but to the review regime's sensitivity to
presentation features that carry no governance-relevant information.}

\revised{This is the operational face of the finite-patch non-exhaustion
result. That result establishes that finite output-patching cannot
guarantee exhaustive coverage of an unbounded encoding space. The
empirical audit shows what this looks like in practice: surface
transformations that preserve the governance case do change the review
outcome for 20\% of models tested. Any regulatory or institutional framework that
depends solely on output-only review therefore cannot reliably
administer ``same case $\to$ same action''---not because the standard is
wrong, but because the information available to the reviewer is
insufficient to satisfy it consistently.}

\subsection{From Administrability Failure to Fault Weakening}
\label{sec:fault-weakening}

\revised{The access-regime comparison (H2, \S\ref{sec:review-actions-analysis})
provides a directional test: if $\Delta_{\text{access}} > 0$, then
information restriction---not reviewer quality---explains a portion of
the instability. The \texttt{EB06} family makes this concrete. Under
output-only review, the same case that is clearly actionable under
richer access remains administratively unresolved: the reviewer can see
that something governance-relevant occurred but cannot determine whether
the event crossed the policy threshold, because the decisive evidence is
enclosed in a privileged partition controlled by a root-level human
operator.}

\revised{This pattern extends the doctrinal analysis of
\S\ref{structural-pattern}. The Liability Paradox establishes that
intent attribution fails under output-trace collision; the empirical
audit shows that even action attribution---a weaker requirement than
intent reconstruction---fails to remain stable when the evidence base is
structurally restricted. Fault-based governance requires not only that fault be
definable in principle but that the evidence needed to assess it be
accessible to the assessor. Epistemic
enclosure~\cite{kahl2025enclosure}---legal, technical, and
state-enforced restrictions on information flow---makes this evidence
structurally inaccessible to external reviewers, auditors, and
regulators.}

\revised{The four cases in \S\ref{validation-cross-layer-harm} illustrate
where this inaccessibility concentrates. In every case, Layer~3---the
epistemic boundary where user-side influence ends and system-side control
