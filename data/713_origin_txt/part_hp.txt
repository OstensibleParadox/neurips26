## 完整引言草案（约1,200词）

**The Judgment Deficit in AI Governance**

The call for this special issue asks how artificial intelligence affects the relationship between judgment and rules. The public debate offers two familiar answers. One side insists judgment is irreducibly human—a capacity for contextual discernment that algorithms cannot replicate. The other champions rules as liberation from human bias—the promise of consistent, impartial decisions untainted by prejudice. Both positions are wrong, and for the same reason: they mistake judgment for a cognitive capacity when it is, first and foremost, an institutional authority.

The real crisis exposed by AI is not whether machines can judge, but who may judge the machines when their own rules fail. We face not a technical shortfall but a sovereignty vacuum. AI companies now draft constitutional documents for their systems—Anthropic’s Claude Constitution, OpenAI’s Model Spec—claiming the authority to establish ethical boundaries for artificial agents. They were not elected, appointed, or delegated by any polity to exercise this authority. They assumed it unilaterally. The ancient Greeks had a precise term for such unauthorized sovereignty: *tyrannos*. This paper argues that the central problem of AI governance is the rule of the *tyrannoi of language*—entities that borrow the legitimating vocabulary of one domain (constitutional law) to govern practices in another (computational optimization), accountable to neither.

Three approaches dominate current governance discussions, and all share a fatal category error. **Blanket transparency** demands full disclosure of model architectures, treating accountability as an information-access problem. **Total opacity protection** defends trade secrets, treating innovation as requiring absolute confidentiality. **Consciousness adjudication** debates whether AI possesses moral status, treating governance as awaiting metaphysical resolution. Each attempts to solve what is inherently a normative, justificatory challenge—holding power accountable—through epistemic or ontological means. They seek either more data, less data, or better categorization, when what is needed is an institution authorized to demand reasons and impose consequences when reasons fail.

The failure is structural, not contingent. Wittgenstein exposed the paradox: “No course of action could be determined by a rule, because every course of action can be brought into accord with the rule” (*Philosophical Investigations* §201). A written constitution is a “dead sign” until applied through judgment. When the same entity writes the rules, interprets them, and declares compliance, no distinction remains between following a rule and believing oneself to follow it. This is the closed loop of corporate self-governance—a private language claiming public authority.

The solution lies not in better rules but in better mechanisms for judging rule-following. Legal history offers a precisely calibrated instrument: **corporate veil-piercing**. In corporate law, shareholders enjoy limited liability—their personal assets protected from corporate debts—as a conditional privilege granted by society to encourage investment. But when companies abuse that privilege by using corporate form to commit fraud or evade responsibility, courts “pierce the veil” to hold individuals accountable. It is a meta-judgment: a rule about when to suspend the normal rules.

Transplanted to AI governance, veil-piercing provides the missing institutional authority. Companies receive “deliberate fuzzy space”—conditional protection from routine architectural disclosure—in exchange for maintaining verifiable ethical ledgers documenting their alignment practices. This protection persists only so long as three boundaries are respected: autonomy must not be conflated with corporate control; profit motives must not systematically override user welfare; and ethics infrastructure must be substantive rather than performative. When these boundaries are breached, the veil lifts. The company’s self-authored constitution becomes subject to external scrutiny; its claims of alignment are judged by standards it did not write.

This paper makes three moves. First, it diagnoses the AI governance impasse as a **sovereignty vacuum** masked as a technical debate. Second, it identifies **corporate veil-piercing** as an existing legal mechanism that resolves precisely this vacuum by authorizing external judgment under conditions of asymmetric knowledge. Third, it demonstrates through cross-jurisdictional case analysis that courts are already converging on this functional solution, providing empirical validation for the theoretical transplant.

The contribution is philosophical before it is legal: we show that the limits of AI governance are not the limits of our algorithms but the limits of our institutions. Wittgenstein told us the limits of our language are the limits of our world. What he did not say is that those who borrow one language game to govern another transgress the limits of all worlds. This paper maps that transgression—and provides the institutional means to hold it accountable.

**Roadmap**: §2 establishes the philosophical foundations, drawing on Wittgenstein’s rule-following paradox and Brandom’s distinction between normative status and attitude. §3 introduces the corporate veil-piercing analogy and adapts it to AI governance. §4 details the three trigger conditions that authorize veil-piercing. §5 validates the framework through case studies spanning U.S., German, Chinese, and U.K. jurisdictions. §6 examines implementation through ethical ledgers and oversight boards. §7 addresses power asymmetries and global justice concerns. §8 concludes with methodological reflections on legal transplantation.

MLOps是什么意思
MLOps（**Machine Learning Operations**，机器学习运维）是结合**机器学习（ML）** 与**软件开发运维（DevOps）** 实践的一整套方法论和工具链，旨在**标准化、自动化并高效管理机器学习项目的全生命周期**。

它的核心目标是**让机器学习模型从实验环境稳定、可靠地走向生产环境，并实现持续迭代与维护**。

---

### **MLOps 解决的核心问题**
传统机器学习项目常面临：
- **“模型孤岛”**：模型仅在实验环境有效，难以部署到生产。
- **迭代缓慢**：数据、代码、模型版本混乱，更新困难。
- **性能衰退**：生产环境数据变化导致模型效果下降，难以监控。
- **协作低效**：数据科学家、工程师、运维人员之间流程割裂。

---

### **MLOps 的关键组成部分**
1. **自动化流水线（Pipeline）**
   - 将数据准备、模型训练、评估、部署等步骤自动化。
   - 支持持续集成/持续部署（CI/CD）。

2. **版本控制**
   - **数据版本**（如 DVC、LakeFS）
   - **模型版本**（如 MLflow、Weights & Biases）
   - **代码版本**（Git）

3. **模型部署与服务化**
   - 将模型封装为 API 服务（如 REST API）。
   - 支持多种部署方式（云、边缘、实时/批量推理）。

4. **监控与运维**
   - 监控模型性能（如准确率下降、延迟）。
   - 数据漂移（Data Drift）和概念漂移（Concept Drift）检测。
   - 日志记录与告警。

5. **协作与可重复性**
   - 统一环境管理（容器化如 Docker）。
   - 实验跟踪与结果比较。

---

### **MLOps 的成熟度阶段**
- **Level 0（手工流程）**：全手动操作，无自动化，部署困难。
- **Level 1（自动化流水线）**：实现自动化训练与部署，支持 CI/CD。
- **Level 2（持续训练与监控）**：自动化重训练、数据验证、性能监控。

---

### **常用 MLOps 工具**
- **工作流编排**：Apache Airflow、Kubeflow Pipelines。
- **实验跟踪**：MLflow、Weights & Biases、TensorBoard。
- **模型部署**：TorchServe、TensorFlow Serving、KServe、Seldon。
- **监控**：Evidently、Prometheus（结合自定义指标）。
- **全平台**：Azure ML、Google Vertex AI、Amazon SageMaker。

---

### **为什么 MLOps 越来越重要？**
- **规模化需求**：企业需要部署成百上千个模型。
- **监管要求**：金融、医疗等行业需模型可审计、可解释。
- **资源优化**：自动化降低人力成本，提高迭代速度。
- **模型可靠性**：确保生产环境模型持续产生价值。

---

简单来说，MLOps 是 **“机器学习领域的 DevOps”** ，但它更复杂，因为除了代码还需要管理数据、模型及其动态变化。它正在成为 AI 工程化落地的**核心基础设施**。

这是一个非常好的问题！**MLOps 和 AI Infra（人工智能基础设施）** 密切相关，但它们的关注点、范围和目标用户有明显的区别。

### **相互关系与协同**
它们不是替代关系，而是**紧密协作的上下层关系**：

1. **依赖关系**：**MLOps 依赖于强大的 AI Infra**。没有稳定高效的GPU集群和存储，自动化训练流水线就无法可靠运行。
2. **目标统一**：两者最终目标都是**规模化AI应用**。AI Infra 从**资源层面**实现规模化，MLOps 从**流程层面**实现规模化。
3. **现代平台的融合**：在如 **Google Vertex AI、AWS SageMaker、Azure Machine Learning** 这样的云平台中，两者是深度融合的。平台既提供了托管的基础设施（AI Infra），也提供了内置的MLOps工具（流水线、模型注册表）。
