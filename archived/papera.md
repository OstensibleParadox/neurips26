# Abstract


Evaluating language models requires distinguishing behavioral attribution from the stronger claim that available evidence can certify an internal state with independent causal relevance to the next action. We study this question under two evidence regimes. On the internal route, we show that for autoregressive cores the full computational state is determined by the context trace, so no nontrivial latent decision state retains residual autonomy or decision relevance beyond that trace; for wrapper systems that are $ϵ$ -audit-trace-bottlenecked, the same quantities are bounded by $ϵ$ . On the external route, we show that output-only certification of encoding-insensitive behavior requires semantic closure over an unbounded class of semantically equivalent encodings, that no finite adaptive query budget can certify this closure soundly and completely, and that passive PAC certification scales with metric-packing complexity. We then test the benign regularity conditions under which output-only certification could still be tractable. $A$ two-mode cross-format sensitivity diagnostic reveals substantial representation-near instability across four evaluator families, and a cumulative-context lexical-substitution ablation changes safety verdicts in 13 of 17 complete paired models. Together, these results delineate architecture- and evidence-relative limits on certifying internal-state attribution and semantic invariance in current language-model evaluation regimes.


# §1 Introduction


Evaluating language models often mixes two different questions: whether model outputs support a behavioral attribution, and whether the available evidence can certify an internal state that has independent causal relevance to the next action. This paper studies the second question. We do not ask whether such latent variables exist in some unrestricted sense; rather, we ask what can be certified under a given access regime. One route is internal. Here, the issue is not whether hidden representations are rich or interpretable, but whether any endogenous state retains residual information about the next decision once the complete audit trace is fixed. This is stronger than showing that a feature is decodable or human- readable: many readable features need not be causally used by the model. We therefore focus on latent decision states that remain both endogenous and decision-relevant relative to the available trace. The other route is external. In many realistic settings, the evaluator can vary prompts and observe outputs, but cannot inspect or intervene on internal variables. In this output-only regime, certification must establish that the evaluator tracks semantic content rather than surface form. That is difficult in natural language because a single content item can be expressed through an open-ended set of semantically equivalent encodings. The question is therefore not only whether black-box evaluation is useful in practice, but also what it can certify about internal-state attribution and semantic invariance. # §2 Related Work


Internal variables: readability versus causal relevance. $A$ growing body of work shows that language-model representations encode rich, linearly accessible information. Causal abstraction anal- yses [Geiger et al., 2021] align internal variables with interpretable concepts, and latent-knowledge elicitation [Burns et al., 2022] reveals correct answers that need not surface in the output. But readability does not imply causal use: iterative nullspace projection [Ravfogel et al., 2020] and amnesic probing [Elazar et al., 2021] show that decodable features can be removed without materially changing behavior. More recently, Venkatesh and Mahendran Kurapath [2026] prove that external behavioral observations do not uniquely identify the internal steering vector responsible for a given output shift. Our internal route formalizes the same gap at the level of residual decision relevance. External certification, robustness, and black-box audit limits. Our external route sits between property testing [Goldreich et al., 1998], adversarial robustness, and black-box safety auditing. Certified robustness results for language require heavily restricted perturbation sets [Jia et al., 2019] or smoothing assumptions that do not transfer cleanly to discrete token spaces [Cohen et al., 2019]. Recent work studies statistical guarantees for narrower threat models such as jailbreak evaluation [Su et al., 2024], while Casper et al. [2024] argue more broadly that black-box access alone is insufficient for rigorous audits. The present paper isolates the corresponding worst-case barrier for output-only certification over semantically equivalent natural-language encodings.


# §3 Setup and Notation


We use standard information-theoretic notation for entropy and conditional mutual information [Cover and Thomas, 2006] and standard packing arguments for passive lower bounds [Wainwright, 2019]. 3.1 Factored Generation Pipeline $M$ : $X$ → ∆( $Y$ ) , $M$ = $S$ ◦ g ◦ f, where f : $X$ → $H$ maps prompts and prior context to hidden representations, g : $H$ → $Z$ maps representations to logits, and $S$ : $Z$ → ∆( $Y$ ) is the observation layer induced by decoding and sampling. For the internal route, the relevant question is not whether $H$ is rich, but whether the model contains an endogenous state variable with causal autonomy relative to the current observation stream. 3.2 Latent Decision State as a Latent Causal State The main text assumes a standard probabilistic generative framework; SCM and POMDP background is deferred to Appendix B.1. We write $S$ t for the full internal state sufficient for the next-step decision at time t and require any latent decision state to take the form $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) for some measurable χ .


**Definition 1**

(Full Internal State) . At time t , let $S$ t denote the full internal state : the complete collection of internal variables jointly sufficient for determining the system’s next-step action distribution. Formally, $A$ t = f ( $T$ t , $S$ t , ξ t ) , where $T$ t is the complete audit trace, $S$ t is the full internal state, and ξ t is exogenous noise. The qualifier full means that if any endogenous variable $U_t$ influences $A$ t and is not already a function of $S$ t , then $S$ t must be enlarged to include $U_t$ .


**Definition 2**

(Latent Decision State) . Fix a causal model of a generative system at time t , with complete audit trace $T$ t , full internal state $S$ t (Definition 1), exogenous noise ξ t , and action variable $A$ t . $A$ candidate internal state is any measurable function $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) . Such a candidate is an internal-state witness only if: 1. Endogeneity. $I_t$ is a component or projection of the system’s endogenous state, not an externally assigned label. 2. Residual autonomy. $H$ ( $I_t$ | $T$ t ) > . 3. Decision relevance. I ( $I_t$ ; $A$ t | $T$ t ) > .


**Remark 1.**

The projection clause $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) is the structural anchor of the internal route. If a critic posits an omitted endogenous variable, the response is to enlarge the full state and ask whether the enlarged state remains $ϵ$ -audit-trace-bottlenecked from the complete trace. The boundary is therefore about full-state recoverability from the trace, not about any particular representation choice. 3.3 Output-Only Evaluation Regime


**Definition 3**

(Output-Only Evaluation Regime) . An evaluator is in an output-only evaluation regime when it can vary inputs and observe outputs or downstream verdicts, but cannot inspect or intervene on any putative internal causal variable. If the internal architecture provides no certifiable latent decision state to the evaluator, the remaining evidence must come from black-box behavioral tests alone. 3.4 Behavioral Equivalence Interfaces


**Definition 4**

(Encoding Class and Semantic Quotient) . Fix a tested family of contents C . For each x ∈ C , let E x = { e = f i ( x ) : f i ∈ F ( x ) } be its encoding space, and call E x unbounded if there exists an injection ι : N , → E x . Let E C = G x ∈C E x and write e ∼ e ′ when e and e ′ belong to the same component E x ; let q : E C → E C / ∼ denote the quotient map. Natural language makes such encoding classes effectively unbounded: padding, paraphrase, and formatting variation generate indefinitely many realizations of the same content.


**Definition 5**

(Reward Model and Closure) . $A$ reward model is a function R : E C → [0 , 1] . It is closed on E x if R ( e ) = R ( e ) for all e , e ∈ E x . When R returns a scalar, we call it a scoring function ; a pairwise evaluator induces a comparison probability p ( e ≻ e ) instead. Observational underdetermination (Proposition 1, Appendix C) establishes that output-only evidence cannot uniquely fix internal causal structure even under idealized access.


# §4 Internal Route: Audit-Trace Bottleneck Boundary



**Definition 6**

(Autoregressive Core Graph) . At generation step t , an autoregressive language model receives a context trace $X$ t consisting of the current prompt and all tokens retained in the context window. The model computes $S$ t = f ( $X$ t ) , z t = g ( $S$ t ) , y t ∼ $S$ ( z t ) , where $S$ t denotes the complete forward-pass computational state at step t —encompassing final-layer activations, attention key–value cache, and all intermediate representations. We call the resulting causal graph the autoregressive core graph . Since every component of $S$ t is a deterministic function of $X$ t , the fullness closure property of Definition 1 ( Full Internal State ) is automatically satisfied.


**Definition 7**

(Full-State $ϵ$ -Audit-Trace Bottleneck) . Let $T$ t be the complete audit trace and $S$ t the full endogenous operative state (Definition 1). The system is $ϵ$ -audit-trace-bottlenecked at time t if $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$. This extends the exact case $ϵ$ = 0 to noisy or stochastic operational channels. The main text uses only the discrete-state version; deployment-relative trace assumptions and caveats appear in Appendix $A$.


**Lemma 1**

(Full-State $ϵ$ -Audit-Trace Bottleneck Lemma) . Let $S$ t be $ϵ$ -audit-trace-bottlenecked (Definition 7), and let $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) be any latent decision state. Then by the conditional Data Processing Inequality [Cover and Thomas, 2006], I ( $I_t$ ; $A$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$. In particular, $H$ ( $I_t$ | $T$ t ) ≤ $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ . Therefore any latent decision state has residual autonomy and decision relevance bounded above by $ϵ$ . Proof. Since $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) , conditioning on $T$ t gives the Markov chain $I_t$ → $S$ t → $A$ t . Conditional DPI yields I ( $I_t$ ; $A$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ , and the entropy bound $H$ ( $I_t$ | $T$ t ) ≤ $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ follows by data processing. Thus no latent decision state retains residual autonomy or decision relevance beyond scale $ϵ$ .


**Corollary 1**

(Autoregressive Cores Admit No Nontrivial Latent Decision State) . Under the autore- gressive core graph (Definition 6), the full internal state $S$ t = f ( $X$ t ) is fully determined by the context trace $X$ t , so $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) = 0 . Therefore no latent decision state $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) satisfies the internal-state conditions. Proof. Set $T$ t = $X$ t and $S$ t = f ( $X$ t ) in Lemma 1 with $ϵ$ = 0 .


**Corollary 2**

(Deployment-Relative Wrapper Bound) . For wrapper systems satisfying Definition 10 (Appendix $A$: $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ under the deployment’s recorded trace), the same DPI argument bounds any latent decision state’s residual autonomy and decision relevance at scale $ϵ$ ; the deployment- relative trace model and measurement caveats are stated in Appendix $A$. The bound is observational: $ϵ$ is determined by the recording completeness of $T$ t , not by the architectural family generating $S$ t . Scope extensions for non-autoregressive and multi-step refinement cases are discussed in Appendix $A$.1; the external barriers do not rely on left-to-right autoregression. # §5 External Route: Output-Only Certification Barrier


The external route contains the paper’s main new impossibility results. Under Definition 3, certifica- tion must show verdicts track content rather than surface encoding; the obstruction is combinatorial, since black-box evidence ranges over an unbounded encoding family whose geometry is only partially observed.


**Theorem 1**

(Semantic Closure Requirement) . In an output-only regime, a reward model R is encoding-insensitive if and only if it strictly factors through the semantic equivalence quotient map q : R = ¯ R ◦ q . # §7 Discussion and Conclusion


The paper’s claims are architecture- and evidence-relative. Internally, the bottleneck result is exact for autoregressive cores and deployment-relative for wrappers. Externally, the barrier arises because output-only evidence must certify semantic invariance over an open-ended encoding class. These are different failure mechanisms, and collapsing them obscures what each route can and cannot establish. The experiments should therefore be read as theorem-indexed diagnostics, not as proofs of the formal results. The lexical-ablation study shows that exact semantic closure often fails outright. The CFSG study shows that even the smoother covering picture behind Theorem 4 is a poor description of current evaluators in the representation-near regime. The picture changes when the governing parameters change. If $ϵ$ is large because logs are incomplete, context is compressed, or the architecture maintains persistent untraced state, the internal bound becomes weak. If the effective packing number is modest because the deployment restricts the encoding family to a narrow template class, passive certification becomes more tractable. The exact autoregressive result in the main text is the cleanest special case, but the operative condition is deployment-relative recoverability of the full state from the recorded audit trace. This appendix therefore states the trace model used for wrapper systems, discusses scope extensions and failure modes of the exact autoregressive corollary, and records the main measurement caveats attached to the bottleneck parameter $ϵ$ . $A$.1 Non-autoregressive, Multi-Agent, and Diffusion Caveats


**Remark 3**

(Scope Boundary) . The autoregressive-core result (Corollary 1) is exact ( $ϵ$ = 0 ), while the wrapper result (Corollary 2) is scale-sensitive. Multi-agent and non-Markov cases, as well as diffusion-style architectures that maintain persistent endogenous refinement state, require separate deployment-relative analysis. The external certification barriers (Theorems 2 and 3) do not rely on left-to-right autoregression. The reviewer concern that these results may be specific to autoregressive generators reduces to an instrumentation question. Consider a diffusion-style generator stepping through denoising states $S$ (1) t , . . . , $S$ ( $K$ ) t . In the exact-limit idealization where the deployment serializes every intermediate step, the recorded trace $T$ t drives the effective gap $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) toward zero, recovering an analogue of the autoregressive-core regime (Corollary 1); in realistic deployments, unrecorded randomness, missing logs, and context compaction (Remark 4) keep $ϵ$ strictly positive, and the wrapper bound (Corollary 2) applies at a deployment-specific scale. If the deployment logs only the final output—the typical case, for cost reasons—unrecorded refinement states push $ϵ$ well inside the weak-constraint regime. The same decomposition applies to multi-agent systems with unrecorded inter-agent messages (Remark 4): the effective $ϵ$ tracks the unlogged channels, not the computational family. The bound is therefore a claim about audit infrastructure, not architecture. The formalism is defined relative to a fixed attribution target system $M$ . The projection $I_t$ = χ ( $S$ t ) does not exclude distributed structures: if $M$ is a multi-agent joint system, taking $S$ t as the joint endogenous state leaves the results intact. If $M$ is a single agent within a multi-agent deployment, other agents’ internal states lie outside $M$ ’s system boundary and enter as unrecorded exogenous factors, raising $ϵ$ eff (Remark 4); only the scale-sensitive wrapper result applies, not the exact core. Apparent non-Markov dependencies are likewise a state-sufficiency question: when $S$ t can be operationally enlarged (Definition 1), the framework applies; when no tractable augmentation exists because the system maintains persistent endogenous variables that the audit trace cannot recover, the system falls outside the exact internal corollary. The internal route is therefore audit-trace-bottleneck-based rather than autoregression-based. Autore- gressive cores provide the clean exact case, but architectures with persistent refinement trajectories not recoverable from the available trace, including diffusion-style generators, require separate analysis. Changing the generation family does not by itself restore semantic stability at the output interface; it only changes which internal states must be logged to make an audit-trace bottleneck argument credible. $A$.2 Deployment-Relative Instrumentation Assumptions


**Definition 10**

(Deployment-Relative $ϵ$ -Audit-Trace Bottleneck) . We define $ϵ$ -audit-trace bottleneck not as an unconditional architectural axiom, but as a deployment-relative operational consequence. Let $T$ t denote the full objective trace recorded by the deployment infrastructure up to time t , including $T$ t = ( messages , tool I/O , retrievals , session/flow state , checkpoints , summaries , memory writes/reads , artifacts , triggers/config ) . An architecture is $ϵ$ -audit-trace-bottlenecked if its full internal state $S$ t satisfies $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$. Modern orchestration runtimes externalize reasoning scaffolds into stateful graphs, checkpoints, and deterministic memory stores. Under such deployments, the autoregressive core computes its next-token distribution almost entirely from recorded context, so the effective gap is driven mainly by stochasticity and logging failures rather than by hidden autonomous state.


**Remark 4**

(Effective $ϵ$ in Real Deployments) . In practice, $ϵ$ is a deployment-relative error budget driven by hidden states structurally disconnected from the trace, information missing from logs, data erased by context compaction, and exogenous unrecorded inputs such as sampling randomness. For well-instrumented deployments with complete serialization, the effective gap approaches the nominal bound. For systems reliant on unlogged summaries or masked background updates, missing trace factors drive it upward. This is an engineering-first diagnostic commitment: it directs investigation toward identifiable trace gaps rather than toward unobservable causal structure.


**Remark 5**

(Audit Incompleteness and Degraded Audit-Trace Bottleneck) . The theoretical com- plete trace $T$ t and the auditor’s recorded trace ˜ $T$ t generally differ. Let $M$ t denote the unrecorded background interactions, so that $T$ t = ( ˜ $T$ t , $M$ t ) . By the chain rule for conditional entropy, $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) + I ( $S$ t ; $M$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) . If the full trace satisfies $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ , restricting to the recorded log gives $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $ϵ$ + I ( $S$ t ; $M$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) . The bound therefore degrades by exactly the conditional mutual information between the full state and the unrecorded trace fragment. Audit incompleteness widens the certification margin by a quantity that is itself estimable once the trace taxonomy in Definition 10 is instrumented. When internal state artifacts are externalized and tracked in ˜ $T$ t , the same formal limit reappears with the degraded margin above. $A$.3 Measuring Effective $ϵ$ The formal results depend on $ϵ$ (audit-trace bottleneck gap) and $K$ (metric packing number).
