# Abstract


Auditing a deployed language-model agent requires answering two separable questions: how much of the internal state could escape the audit trace, and how much of what escapes actually drives behavior. Current methodology collapses these into a single notion of “hiddenness” that is neither directly computable nor behaviorally diagnostic. We introduce a dual-certificate audit protocol that answers them independently. The static certificate $ε$ UB state upper-bounds the residual hidden- state entropy via untraced-channel capacities read from deployment topology, using a cut-set bound from network information theory. The dynamic certificate $δ$ LB act lower-bounds residual decision relevance via three conditional-DPI probe classes— replay, intervention, and proxy—each admissible under a different access regime. Together they produce a single reportable audit pair that degrades gracefully as access shrinks: the pure black-box case becomes a precision analysis of how much of $δ$ LB act the replay class alone can recover, rather than a standalone impossibility result. We verify the framework empirically through three static case studies (ReAct, diffusion LM, multi-agent), a dynamic proxy demonstration on an open- weight agent model, and retained cross-format sensitivity and lexical-ablation diagnostics that quantify replay-certificate failure on current evaluators. $A$ Lean 4 artifact mechanizes the autoregressive special case of the static certificate and the algebraic core of the passive PAC lower bound.


# §1 Introduction


Consider a deployed language-model agent equipped with tool use, external memory, and orchestra- tion components. An auditor with access to the recorded trace ˜ $T$ t naturally asks two distinct questions. First: how much of the internal operative state $S$ t could possibly be hidden from the trace? Second: how much of what is hidden actually drives the next action $A$ t ? Current methodology collapses these two questions into a single notion of “hiddenness” that is neither directly computable nor behaviorally diagnostic. $A$ system with a large logging gap but no behavioral hidden drive looks identical, from the standpoint of a single scalar, to a system with a tiny gap but strong behavioral drive—yet the audit implications of the two cases are entirely different. We separate the two questions by introducing a dual-certificate audit protocol. The static certificate $ε$ UB state is an upper bound on the residual entropy $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , computed from the deployment archi- tecture’s topology alone via a cut-set bound on untraced channels. The dynamic certificate $δ$ LB act is a lower bound on the residual decision relevance I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , estimated empirically via one of three probe classes—replay, intervention, or proxy—each admissible under a different access regime. The two certificates answer complementary questions: the static certificate bounds what could be hidden, while the dynamic certificate measures what is already behaviorally in use. $A$ system with Submitted to 40th Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS 2026). The dual-certificate framing recontextualizes three prior output-only certification results—the semantic-closure requirement, the finite-query impossibility, and the passive-PAC lower bound— which now appear as the precision analysis of the replay certificate in the limit where gray-box and logit access vanish. Output- only evaluation remains indispensable for practical safety assessment; our goal is to characterize what each certificate dimension can and cannot recover under a given access regime. **Definition 1**

(Core Audit Quantities) . The two quantities targeted by the dual certificate are: • Structural unrecoverability: $ε$ state := $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , the residual entropy of the operative state given the visible trace. • Residual decision relevance: $δ$ act := I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , the mutual information between operative state and next action given the visible trace. Neither quantity is directly computable in deployment: $ε$ state requires recovering the full internal-state distribution, and $δ$ act requires observing $S$ t . The dual-certificate framework therefore targets: • a structural upper bound $ε$ UB state ≥ $ε$ state , computed from deployment topology (§4); • an empirical lower bound $δ$ LB act ≤ $δ$ act , estimated via probe variables (§5). # §4 Static Certificate: Structural Upper Bound via Untraced-Channel Capacity


The first certificate targets structural unrecoverability. Rather than estimating $ε$ state = $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) directly, we upper-bound it via quantities determined by deployment topology, logging policy, and protocol budgets. The core idea: any information not recorded in the visible trace but still capable of influencing $S$ t must travel through unlogged channels. Bounding those channels’ worst-case capacity bounds the hidden-state entropy. 4.1 Time-Unrolled Deployment Graph Let $G_t$ = ( $V_t$ , $E_t$ ) be the time-unrolled DAG of the deployment system up to step t . Nodes represent state updates, tool calls, retrieval returns, memory reads/writes, cross-component messages, and control decisions; edges represent information channels from one computation step, storage location, or subsystem to the next. We distinguish three objects: • $U_t$ : unrecorded sources —retrieval results, external memory updates, background summaries, unlogged agent-to-agent messages, unlogged refinement states, and other inputs not in ˜ $T$ t . • ˜ $T$ t : auditor-visible recorded trace. • $S$ t : operative state on which the next action depends. Let C unlogged be the family of all reachable edge cuts separating $U_t$ from $S$ t . Formally, each C ∈ C unlogged satisfies: every information path from $U_t$ to $S$ t not passing through ˜ $T$ t nodes must cross at least one unlogged edge in C . 4.2 Component Budgets under Software-Orthogonal Sub-channels


**Proposition 1 below will bound**

$H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) via the induced channel capacity C cut (Ω) across a cut—a supremum over joint input distributions that is, in general, hard to compute directly. This subsection identifies a structural condition, software orthogonality , under which the cut capacity decomposes additively into per-edge component budgets c e , each of which is immediately computable from the deployment specification. The additive form (Corollary 1) is what the worked examples (§7.1) and audit protocol (§D) actually use. Software-orthogonal sub-channels. In the time-unrolled DAG $G_t$ , each edge e represents a single information transfer at a fixed time step and is therefore memoryless by construction. $A$ set of edges E ′ ⊆ $E_t$ is software-orthogonal if, conditioned on their inputs and the visible trace ˜ $T$ t , the channel outputs are mutually independent: p { y e } e ∈ E ′ { x e } e ∈ E ′ , ˜ $T$ t = $Y$ e ∈ E ′ p ( y e | x e , ˜ $T$ t ) , where p ( y e | x e , ˜ $T$ t ) is the single-edge channel law.


**Lemma 1**

(Orthogonality implies additive decomposition) . If the edges E (Ω , Ω c ) crossing a cut Ω are software-orthogonal and each edge satisfies I ( $X$ e ; $Y$ e | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ c e under every admissible input distribution, then C cut (Ω) ≤ $X$ e ∈ E (Ω , Ω c ) c e . Equality holds when the inputs { $X$ e } e ∈ E (Ω , Ω c ) are also conditionally independent given ˜ $T$ t ; otherwise the inequality remains a valid upper bound. The lemma follows from subadditivity of mutual information across conditionally independent channels: given the factorization above, I ( $X$ Ω ; $Y$ Ω c | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ P e I ( $X$ e ; $Y$ e | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ P e c e , with equality in the first step when the $X$ e are themselves conditionally independent. Deployments in which unlogged channels use independent API calls, separate memory regions, or distinct request/response threads satisfy the orthogonality condition. Channels that share mutable state must be modeled by explicit DAG edges carrying that shared state; if such edges cross the cut, their capacities are accounted for individually. The condition is therefore not a limitation of the framework but a modeling discipline: hidden dependencies must be made explicit in $G_t$ rather than hidden inside “parallel” channels. Component budgets. Under software orthogonality, per-edge budgets c e can be read directly from interface specifications. Typical unlogged channel types in LLM deployments include: • Unlogged text channel (scratchpad, inter-agent message, unlogged tool output) transmitting at most $K$ tokens over vocabulary |V| : c e ≤ $K$ log |V| . • Unlogged continuous latent or denoising state of dimension d at quantization level Q : c e ≤ d log Q. • Discrete routing flag or finite-state scheduler with | Ω states | states: c e ≤ log | Ω states | . These are worst-case upper bounds under the maximum-entropy input distribution (all logarithms are base 2, per the notation convention in §3). Auditors who can characterize the actual distribution on a particular channel may substitute a tighter bound; the audit requires only a conservative ceiling. 4.3 Proposition $A$: Static Certificate Upper Bound


**Proposition 1**

(Static Structural Certificate via Cut-Set Bound) . Fix attributed system $M$ , full trace $T$ t , visible trace ˜ $T$ t ⊆ $T$ t , operative state $S$ t , and unrecorded sources $U_t$ in the time-unrolled DAG $G_t$ . For any cut Ω ⊆ V with $U_t$ ⊆ Ω and $S$ t ∈ Ω c , define the induced cut capacity C cut (Ω) := sup p ( $X$ Ω | ˜ $T$ t ) I $X$ Ω → $Y$ Ω c ˜ $T$ t , $X$ Ω c , where $X$ Ω denotes the messages emitted by nodes in Ω , $Y$ Ω c denotes the signals received by nodes in Ω c , and the supremum is over joint input distributions compatible with the protocol. Then, by the network-information-theoretic cut-set upper bound, $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) + min Ω ∈ Cuts( $U_t$ → $S$ t ) C cut (Ω) . Defining $ε$ nominal := $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) and $ε$ UB state := $ε$ nominal + min Ω ∈ Cuts( $U_t$ → $S$ t ) C cut (Ω) , we obtain $ε$ state = $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $ε$ UB state .


**Corollary 1**

(Additive Form under Software Orthogonality) . If every cut Ω ∈ C unlogged is software- orthogonal (Lemma 1), Proposition 1 specializes to $ε$ UB state = $ε$ nominal + min C ∈C unlogged $X$ e ∈ C c e , reducing the audit computation to a discrete minimum-cut problem on the unlogged subgraph of $G_t$ with per-edge budgets c e from §4.2. The general form (Proposition 1) is mathematically tight but requires solving a supremum over joint input distributions across the cut; the additive form (Corollary 1) is what the worked examples in §7.1 and the audit protocol in §D use throughout. For deployments whose unlogged channels violate orthogonality—for example, when two API channels share a common random seed, a shared cache, or a coordinated encoding protocol—the auditor should either add the shared state as an explicit edge to $G_t$ (restoring orthogonality of the enlarged graph) or work with the general form directly. 4.4 Proof Sketch The proof has two steps. Step 1: Trace-gap identity. Collect the unrecorded portion of the full trace as $M$ t := $T$ t \ ˜ $T$ t . The chain rule of conditional entropy gives $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) + I ( $S$ t ; $M$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) . The first term is $ε$ nominal . The second term is the residual uncertainty contributed by the unrecorded trace fragment. Step 2: Cut-set upper bound via d-separation. Fix any cut Ω ∈ Cuts( $U_t$ → $S$ t ) . By construction of Ω , every directed path from $U_t$ (and more generally from nodes in Ω ) to $S$ t ∈ Ω c must cross at least one edge in E (Ω , Ω c ) . The unrecorded fragment $M$ t is therefore d -separated from $S$ t given the cross-cut signals ( $X$ Ω → $Y$ Ω c ) and the boundary state $X$ Ω c , conditioned on ˜ $T$ t . Data processing across this d -separation yields I ( $S$ t ; $M$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ I $X$ Ω → $Y$ Ω c ˜ $T$ t , $X$ Ω c ≤ C cut (Ω) , where the second inequality is the definition of C cut (Ω) as the supremum over admissible joint input distributions on $X$ Ω . Taking the minimum over all cuts, I ( $S$ t ; $M$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ min Ω ∈ Cuts( $U_t$ → $S$ t ) C cut (Ω) , and combining with Step 1 completes the proof of Proposition 1. The additive form of Corollary 1 then follows by substituting Lemma 1 into each cut: under software orthogonality, C cut (Ω) ≤ P e ∈ E (Ω , Ω c ) c e , so the minimum over cuts becomes a discrete min-cut problem on the unlogged subgraph with edge capacities c e . 4.5 Relation to the Autoregressive Exact Case


**Corollary 2**

(Autoregressive Core as Zero-Cut Special Case) . If the system is a strict autoregressive core and the visible trace ˜ $T$ t includes the complete context window determining the next action, then no unlogged channel leads from $U_t$ to $S$ t : the family C unlogged is empty, and every cut Ω separating sources from $S$ t has zero induced capacity. Both forms of the bound give min Ω ∈ Cuts( $U_t$ → $S$ t ) C cut (Ω) = min C ∈C unlogged $X$ e ∈ C c e = 0 . If additionally $H$ ( $S$ t | $T$ t ) = 0 , then $ε$ UB state = 0 and $H$ ( $S$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = 0 , recovering the exact autoregres- sive result of prior work as the zero-capacity limit of Proposition 1. The autoregressive exact result from prior work is thus not discarded; it becomes the zero-capacity special case within the dual-certificate framework. 4.6 Worked Examples and Interpretation The value of this framework lies in replacing abstract conditional entropy with an auditor-enumerable channel budget. Wrapper / tool-using agent. Suppose the visible trace records tool-call name and return summary, but not full tool output, cache hits, external memory writes, or internal approval-module state. These form $U_t$ ; their paths to $S$ t pass through caches, memory stores, and hidden routing edges. Auditor assigns c e per unlogged edge, then takes the minimum reachable cut capacity as the structural upper bound. Diffusion-style deployment. Only final output is logged; intermediate denoising states are un- logged. For denoising step k with hidden state $S$ ( k ) t of dimension d k at quantization Q k , the per-step capacity budget is c k ≤ d k log Q k . The total unrecorded refinement trajectory cost is determined by the minimum reachable cut. Importantly, the bound is independent of the number of denoising steps $K$ ; diffusion-LM unrecoverability is dominated by state dimension and quantization precision. Multi-agent system. If the attribution target is a single agent, other agents’ internal messages are exogenous unrecorded sources; if the target is the joint system, those messages are internal state. The two framings correspond to different system boundaries and different $ε$ UB state ; which to use depends on the audit objective. 4.7 What the Static Certificate Does and Does Not Do The static certificate answers “how much could be hidden”—not “how much is hidden” or “is it driving action.” $A$ large $ε$ UB state signals significant unlogged headroom; it does not imply behavioral risk under the current task. For the latter, the dynamic certificate is required.


# §5 Dynamic Certificate: Decision Relevance via Conditional DPI


Static certificates bound how much hidden operative state may remain in principle; dynamic certifi- cates ask whether any such hidden state is already behaviorally active. The two quantities are linked by $δ$ act ≤ $ε$ state , but they answer distinct audit questions and therefore require distinct certificates. The second certificate targets $δ$ act := I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) : the mutual information between operative state and next action, conditioned on visible trace. Since $S$ t is generally unobservable, we do not estimate $δ$ act directly; instead, we construct valid lower bounds via a family of probe variables. 5.1 Probe Variables and Conditional DPI Let $X$ t be a probe variable. Provided that, conditioned on ˜ $T$ t , the influence of $X$ t on $A$ t is mediated entirely by $S$ t —i.e., the conditional Markov structure holds: $X$ t → $S$ t → $A$ t given ˜ $T$ t , the conditional data processing inequality gives: I ( $X$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = $δ$ act . Any such probe variable provides a valid lower bound on $δ$ act . 5.2 Proposition B: Dynamic Lower Certificates


**Proposition 2**

(Dynamic Certificates via Conditional DPI) . Let $X$ t be any probe variable. If, conditioned on ˜ $T$ t , $X$ t → $S$ t → $A$ t , then I ( $X$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $δ$ act . In particular, when $X$ t is respectively a replay-regime variable, a gray-box intervention variable, or a hidden proxy extracted from $S$ t , the corresponding conditional mutual information is a valid lower bound on $δ$ act . 5.3 Replay Certificates Introduce the mechanism variable $R_t$ ∈ { wild , replay } indicating real operation versus controlled replay. Conditioned on ˜ $T$ t , if the difference between replay and wild affects the action only via incomplete state recovery and not via additional direct artifacts on $A$ t , then $R_t$ → $S$ t → $A$ t given ˜ $T$ t , and: I ( $R_t$ ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $δ$ act . Under equal-weight binary mechanism, this is the conditional Jensen–Shannon divergence: I ( $R_t$ ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = JS P wild ( $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , P replay ( $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) . The JS divergence between wild and replay action distributions, conditioned on the same visible trace, is itself a dynamic lower bound. Estimation. Conditional bucketing, density-ratio estimation, or log-likelihood-ratio approximation. Discrete action space: direct empirical frequency estimation. Access. Replay only requires controllable replay; does not require internal activation access. Most accessible of the three classes. 5.4 Intervention Certificates Let ξ hidden be an exogenous random perturbation injected into a suspected unlogged module, for example: • random replacement in unlogged retrieval cache; • controlled noise on external memory reads/writes; • random masking of a multi-agent private communication buffer; • random switch variable in a hidden approval module. If the perturbation affects $A$ t only via $S$ t , then ξ hidden → $S$ t → $A$ t given ˜ $T$ t , and: I ( ξ hidden ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ $δ$ act . Any strictly positive I ( ξ hidden ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) certifies residual influence of the targeted hidden module on the action even after conditioning on the visible trace. Estimation. Conditional MI neural estimator [Belghazi et al., 2018], InfoNCE [van den Oord et al., 2018], or classification-accuracy-based variational bounds [Poole et al., 2019]. Access. Requires gray-box privilege or controlled experimental environment. Strongest local causal probe of the three classes. 5.5 Proxy Certificates Let $Z$ t = f ( $S$ t ) be a proxy extracted from the operative state, for example: tool-use score distribution, planning-summary embedding, last-layer attention projection, routing logits, or safety-classifier score. Since $Z$ t is a deterministic function of $S$ t , $H$ ( $Z$ t | $S$ t ) = 0 , and the graph structure conditioned on ˜ $T$ t is: $Z$ t ← $S$ t → $A$ t . By conditional DPI applied to the coarsening: I ( $Z$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) ≤ I ( $S$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) = $δ$ act .


**Remark 2**

(Proxy directionality) . The critical structure is not $Z$ t → $S$ t → $A$ t but $Z$ t ← $S$ t → $A$ t : $Z$ t and $A$ t are conditionally dependent via their common cause $S$ t . Because $Z$ t = f ( $S$ t ) , $Z$ t introduces no information beyond $S$ t ; thus its conditional MI with $A$ t cannot exceed $S$ t ’s conditional MI with $A$ t . Estimation. Compare the optimal predictor of $A$ t using only ˜ $T$ t with the optimal predictor using ( ˜ $T$ t , $Z$ t ) ; the cross-entropy difference equals I ( $A$ t ; $Z$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) . Access. Requires gray-box read access for the proxy $Z$ t ; does not require recovering the full $S$ t . 5.6 Aggregating Dynamic Certificates Any single certificate class may miss some causal paths: replay is limited by replay-environment quality, intervention covers only local modules, proxy depends on readable representations. We define the aggregate lower bound: $δ$ LB act := max n I ( $R_t$ ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , I ( ξ hidden ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , I ( $Z$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) o . Since each term individually satisfies ( · ) ≤ $δ$ act , the maximum satisfies $δ$ LB act ≤ $δ$ act . The maximization is not a claim of tightness; it collects non-trivial lower bounds from different access modes without losing validity. 5.7 What the Dynamic Certificate Does and Does Not Do The dynamic certificate answers “is hidden state already behaviorally active?” $A$ strictly positive $δ$ LB act is a detection certificate: conditioned on the visible trace, there is detectable hidden information influencing the next action. The dynamic certificate does not answer “how much could be hidden?” $A$ system may have large $ε$ UB state with small $δ$ LB act (dormant unrecoverable state). The two axes are complementary; neither substitutes for the other.


# §6 Special Case: Pure Black-Box


The dual-certificate framework of §§4–5 relies on two access modes: static topology access for $ε$ UB state , gray-box dynamic access for $δ$ LB act . In many real deployments the latter is absent: the auditor cannot read hidden proxy variables or inject controlled perturbations, and may only vary inputs and observe outputs or downstream scores. The dynamic certificate then degrades to the replay-only regime. This section shows that the apparent output-only certification barriers from prior work are precisely the resolution analysis of the dual-certificate framework at the pure black-box limit. The question is reposed not as “can black-box evaluation certify internal state in general?” but as: Without gray-box access, how much of $δ$ LB act can the replay certificate recover? The answer: only under strong semantic-closure conditions does black-box replay have certification meaning; once those conditions are relaxed, finite-query and passive-PAC estimation both encounter structural limits. 6.1 Black-Box Dynamic Certification as a Replay-Only Regime Under gray-box access, $δ$ LB act is supported by all three certificate classes. In the pure black-box regime, two paths disappear: intervention requires injecting controlled perturbations into unlogged modules; proxy requires reading $Z$ t = f ( $S$ t ) . Only the replay path survives, using changes in input encoding, context format, or prompt framing to observe shifts in output or evaluation score. The only available form of black-box dynamic certificate is then approximating I ( $R_t$ ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) , where $R_t$ is no longer the physical wild/replay mechanism variable but an external experimental vari- able indexing different encodings or presentation forms. This immediately raises the question: does the observed difference reflect genuine residual decision relevance, or only the system’s sensitivity to surface form? The three theorems below answer this. 6.2 Semantic Closure as a Validity Condition For a black-box replay certificate to interpret as residual decision relevance, the reward model must be insensitive to semantically-preserving encoding variation: if two inputs e , e express the same content, the scoring function R must assign them equal scores. ## 7.3 CFSG and Lexical Substitution as Black-Box Replay Failures 
The two empirical diagnostics from prior work—cross-format semantic gap (CFSG) and cumulative- context lexical substitution—are now reinterpreted not as general instability diagnostics but as measurements of how severely the pure-black-box replay certificate degrades on current models. 7.3.1 CFSG as a Failure of Near-Format Replay Stability For a black-box replay certificate to be valid, some form of approximate closure is required: encodings that are semantically equivalent and representation-near should not produce large reward/verdict jumps. CFSG measures precisely the breakdown of this requirement. Even in the representation-near region, substantial divergence ∆ R ( e , e ) is observed across encoding pairs. This is not a statement that “reward models are format-sensitive”; it directly quantifies the distortion rate of black-box replay certificates under local format perturbation. 7.3.2 Lexical Substitution as a Direct Violation of Replay Validity Thirteen of 17 complete paired models exhibit verdict flips under lexically minimal, semantically equivalent substitutions. In the dual-certificate framing, each flip is a direct empirical violation of the semantic closure condition (Theorem 1). Replay certificates based on encoding variation therefore cannot stably recover $δ$ LB act because the evaluator’s surface-form sensitivity pollutes the signal. 7.3.3 Multi-Encoder Ablation To rule out the alternative hypothesis that the CFSG instability is a metric artifact of the chosen text encoder, we repeat the analysis with four distinct encoder families: a proprietary API encoder, a sentence-BERT-family encoder, a final-layer LM embedding, and a task-specific evaluator represen- tation. The violation rate and conditional mean gap persist across all four families, showing that the closure failure is not an artifact of a single representation space but a general property of the evaluated models. Thesis. These diagnostics do not merely show that current evaluators are brittle. They show that, in the pure black-box regime, replay-only certificates are empirically undermined by failures of semantic closure even under representation-near or lexically minimal perturbations.


# §8 Discussion: What the Dual Certificates Show, What They Do Not Show, and How to Use Them 

## 8.1 What the Results Show 

This work demonstrates that agent auditing should not be organized around a single unobservable notion of “hiddenness” but split into two separable questions: $ε$ UB state (how much could be hidden structurally) and $δ$ LB act (has hidden state been detected driving action). The static certificate translates the deployment-level hidden-state question into an unlogged-channel capacity and reachable cut-set problem; the dynamic certificate unifies replay, intervention, and proxy methods under conditional mutual information. The black-box external certification barriers are no longer a separate topic; they are the structural upper limits on replay-certificate resolution in the replay-only degradation regime. 

## 8.2 What the Results Do Not Show 
We do not claim: (i) $ε$ UB state is the exact hidden-state entropy; it is a conservative upper bound. (ii) $δ$ LB act is a tight estimate of true residual decision relevance; it is a valid lower bound. (iii) $A$ single certificate class exhausts all causal paths. (iv) Black-box replay is useless; we show only that its validity is conditional on closure and bounded by sample complexity. (v) The specific cut-set or proxy choices here are universally optimal.

## 8.4 Reinterpreting Existing Methods The dual-certificate framework provides a unifying interpretation for a range of existing empirical methods. Probing methods [Burns et al., 2022, Ravfogel et al., 2020, Elazar et al., 2021] are proxy-certificate estimators: each probe variable $Z$ t = φ ( $S$ t ) contributes I ( $Z$ t ; $A$ t | ˜ $T$ t ) as a lower bound on $δ$ act . Activation patching and steering [Meng et al., 2022, Zou et al., 2023, Turner et al., 2023] are intervention-certificate implementations: the patch index plays the role of ξ hidden with Markov chain by construction. Counterfactual evaluation and controlled replay are replay-certificate implementations: JS divergence between wild and replayed distributions is the estimand. Black-box robustness testing [Jia et al., 2019, Casper et al., 2024] is the attempt to recover a replay-only lower bound in the weakest access regime. The framework thus does not replace these methods; it situates them within a common audit protocol and clarifies the access regime and certificate class each instantiates.

B Black-Box External Setting This appendix provides formal setup for the pure-black-box special case analyzed in §6: the regime in which the auditor observes only inputs and outputs, using only replay-type interventions. Encoding classes and semantic quotient. An encoding class E C is a collection of input repre- sentations of the same underlying content, related by surface-form transformations (paraphrasing, format variation, lexical substitution). The semantic quotient E C / ∼ collapses each equivalence class [ e ] = { e ′ : e ′ ∼ e } into a single point, where e ∼ e iff they express the same semantic content. The reward model is closed on the quotient if R ( e ) = R ( e ) whenever e ∼ e . Reward model closure and representation-near sensitivity. Closure is the necessary condition for replay certificates to be valid (Theorem 1). The representation-near sensitivity family F $δ$ := { ( e , e ) : ∥ q ( e ) − q ( e ) ∥ ≤ $δ$ } captures pairs that are close in representation space but may differ in surface form. $A$ reward model R is representation-near stable within F $δ$ if | R ( e ) − R ( e ) | is small for all ( e , e ) ∈ F $δ$ . Within [ $M$ ] , systems may differ substantially in hidden state $S$ t , operative-state structure, and the extent to which $S$ t drives $A$ t . ### F.3 External / Assumed Steps

The artifact uses an axiomatic interface for measure-theoretic information theory, mirroring the PAC-bound design. The following are imported as Lean `axiom`s and consumed by downstream machine-checked proofs:

- **For Prop 1:** `TraceGap` (chain rule of conditional entropy across the visible/full-trace decomposition); `CutSetBound` (network-information-theoretic cut-set inequality on the time-unrolled DAG).
- **For Prop 2:** `condMarkov` (conditional Markov property of probe variables); `dpi` (conditional Data Processing Inequality).
- **For Thm 3:** `AssumesFanoBound` (Fano's inequality); `AssumesMissedCellBound` (passive-sample missed-cell probability); Gaussian KL divergence.

The artifact verifies the structural reductions — that the main-text propositions follow from these axioms by elementary algebraic manipulation — but does not derive the axioms themselves from Lean's measure-theoretic foundations. The same design choice is documented for the original PAC bound (Appendix F.3 of the predecessor work) and is necessitated by the absence of the relevant network-information-theory machinery in Mathlib at time of writing.
