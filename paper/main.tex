\begin{abstract}
Two agent realizations can produce identical output-trace distributions yet differ maximally in residual decision relevance: one realization can have $\delta_\text{act}=0$ (hidden state is dormant) while another has $\delta_\text{act}=H(A_t\mid\tilde T_t)$ (hidden state drives every action), with no output-only observable distinguishing them. We prove this Output-Trace Identifiability Gap as Theorem~1 and then close it with a dual-certificate protocol: a structural-access closer $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ (min-cut upper bound on untraced channels) and a gray-box-access closer $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$ (conditional-DPI lower bound via admissible probes). The two certificates are independent axes. ReAct controlled replay shows near-zero $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}=0.0163$ bits (95\% CI $[0.0124,0.0208]$) while logging ablation drives $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ from $16{,}464$ to $0$ bits. Multi-agent counterfactual report intervention approaches near-saturation at $0.901$ bits (CI $[0.873,0.928]$). LLaDA denoising occupies the mid-spectrum at $0.110$ bits (CI $[0.052,0.234]$), with decision relevance concentrated at the final binding step. A Lean~4 artifact mechanizes Theorem~1, Propositions~1 (with the cut-set capacity bound as a structural premise)--2, and Corollary~2 axiom-free from finite-PMF first principles, with all information-theoretic facts proved as theorems over finite discrete probability mass functions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider a ReAct-style agent shipped with an unlogged scratchpad of capacity $16{,}384$ bits per turn. A regression test on output traces shows no behavioral change, yet the same trace under controlled replay with the scratchpad disabled reveals a $0.0163$-bit shift in the tool-token probability distribution on planning tasks. The disagreement is not a probing artifact. It is a structural feature of the audit: the recorded trace fails to distinguish two qualitatively different systems, one whose hidden capacity is dormant on the test distribution and one whose hidden capacity is active. Identifying which holds is the audit task.

This failure is not a limitation of a particular probe or estimator. It is an identifiability gap. Given any observable joint distribution over traces and actions, there exist two agent realizations---one with zero residual decision relevance, one with maximal---that produce exactly the same observational data.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Theorem~1---Output-Trace Identifiability Gap.} Behavioral equivalence does not imply audit equivalence: two realizations can saturate the $[0, H(A_t\mid\tilde T_t)]$ range of $\delta_\text{act}$ while producing identical observational marginals.
  \item \textbf{Dual-certificate gap-closers.} Proposition~1 (structural-access closer) and Proposition~2 (gray-box-access closer) close the gap via deployment-topology min-cut and admissible probes under conditional DPI. The two certificates are independent axes.
  \item \textbf{Three empirical realizations spanning the gap.} ReAct controlled replay provides a near-zero witness ($\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}=0.0163$ bits); LLaDA denoising occupies the mid-spectrum ($0.110$ bits); multi-agent counterfactual report intervention approaches the binary-action ceiling ($0.901$ bits). Each instance indexes $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$ over a different hidden-state coordinate.
  \item \textbf{Lean~4 mechanization.} All main-text information-theoretic claims are mechanized axiom-free from finite-discrete definitions over \texttt{FinitePMF} (Appendix~\ref{app:lean-boundary}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Probing, patching, and causal abstraction.}
Causal abstraction~\citep{geiger2021causal}, latent-knowledge elicitation~\citep{burns2022discovering}, nullspace projection~\citep{ravfogel2020null}, and amnesic probing~\citep{elazar2021amnesic} are natural sources of proxy-style evidence: when they expose a probe variable $Z_t = \varphi(S_t)$ and the required conditional assumptions hold, its MI with action can be interpreted through the proxy certificate. Causal tracing/ROME~\citep{meng2022locating}, representation engineering~\citep{zou2023representation}, and activation addition~\citep{turner2023activation} provide intervention-style evidence when the patch affects action only through $S_t$.

\paragraph{Black-box audit and network information theory.}
Casper et al.~\citep{casper2024blackbox} argue that black-box access is insufficient for auditing internal agent mechanisms. Our work makes this insufficiency quantitative for the audit functional $\delta_\text{act}$: output-only access cannot distinguish $\delta_\text{act}=0$ from $\delta_\text{act}=H(A_t\mid\tilde T_t)$. Pearl's partial-identification framework~\citep{pearl2009causality} applies here: latent-variable models are generally not identifiable from observational data alone. We apply this principle to the audit functional $\delta_\text{act}$ and pair it with explicit gap-closers. Property testing~\citep{goldreich1998property} and black-box safety auditing~\citep{su2024mission} study what can be inferred from outputs alone. Output-only access can support behavioral tests, but it does not by itself give a lower bound on $\delta_\text{act}$; $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ remains computable from topology when structural access is available. The static certificate proof applies a cut-set upper bound~\citep{elgamal2011network} to the time-unrolled DAG (full derivation in Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo}).

\paragraph{Diffusion language-model agents.}
LLaDA is a large-scale masked diffusion language model with bidirectional denoising and instruction-following ability~\citep{nie2025llada}. Recent work further studies diffusion LMs in multi-step decision and tool-use workflows, including matched comparisons to autoregressive agents~\citep{zhen2026dllmagent}. These systems make intermediate denoising latents a natural non-ReAct hidden channel for the dynamic certificate.

\section{Setup and Realization Formalism}
\label{sec:setup}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Standard information-theoretic notation follows \citet{cover2006elements}. All logarithms are base 2; entropy and mutual information are reported in bits.

At step $t$: \emph{full trace} $T_t$ (all intermediate activations), \emph{recorded trace} $\tilde T_t \subseteq T_t$ (auditor-visible), \emph{operative state} $S_t$ (internal computation), \emph{action} $A_t$ (next token), \emph{unrecorded sources} $U_t$ (exogenous inputs not in $\tilde T_t$).

\begin{definition}[Agent Realization]
\label{def:realization}
An \emph{agent realization} $\mathcal{R} = (P_S, f_T, f_A, \pi_U)$ consists of an operative-state distribution $P_S$ over $S_t$, a trace-generation map $f_T: S_t \to \Delta(\tilde T_t)$, an action policy $f_A: (S_t, \tilde T_t) \to \Delta(A_t)$, and a source policy $\pi_U$ governing $U_t$. The realization induces an observational marginal $P_{\mathcal{R}}(\tilde T_t, A_t)$ on the support of a fixed deployment prompt distribution $P_\text{prompt}$.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Behavioral Equivalence]
\label{def:behavioral-equiv}
Two realizations $\mathcal{R}_0, \mathcal{R}_1$ are \emph{behaviorally equivalent}, written $\mathcal{R}_0 \sim_B \mathcal{R}_1$, iff they produce identical joint distributions over observable variables on the deployment prompt distribution:
\[
  P_{\mathcal{R}_0}(\tilde T_t, A_t) = P_{\mathcal{R}_1}(\tilde T_t, A_t) \quad \forall (\tilde T_t, A_t) \in \operatorname{supp}(P_\text{prompt}).
\]
Behavioral equivalence concerns the fixed deployment marginal only. Paraphrase variation and red-teaming are generalization probes that explore the policy under distribution shift; they are not audits on the deployment marginal and do not by themselves refute behavioral equivalence.
\end{definition}

\subsection{Two Core Audit Quantities}

\begin{definition}[Core Audit Quantities]
\label{def:certs}
The two quantities targeted by the dual certificate are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Structural unrecoverability:} $\varepsilon_\text{state} := H(S_t \mid \tilde T_t)$, the residual entropy of the operative state given the visible trace.
  \item \textbf{Residual decision relevance:} $\delta_\text{act} := I(S_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$, the mutual information between operative state and next action given the visible trace.
\end{itemize}
The dual-certificate framework in \S\ref{sec:gap-closers} targets upper and lower bounds on these quantities respectively.
\end{definition}

\section{Output-Trace Identifiability Gap}
\label{sec:identifiability-gap}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Output-Trace Identifiability Gap]
\label{thm:identifiability-gap}
Let $S, T, A$ be finite. For any finite PMF $Q$ on $T \times A$ with $\sum_a Q(t,a) > 0$ for all $t \in \operatorname{supp}(Q_T)$ and $|S| \geq |A|$, there exist two finite joint PMFs $P_0, P_1$ on $S \times T \times A$ such that
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] $P_0$ and $P_1$ project to the same observational marginal $Q$: $\sum_s P_0(s,t,a) = \sum_s P_1(s,t,a) = Q(t,a)$;
  \item[(ii)] under the audit functional $\delta_\text{act}(P) := I_P(S; A \mid T)$, we have $\delta_\text{act}(P_0) = 0$ and $\delta_\text{act}(P_1) = H_Q(A \mid T)$.
\end{enumerate}
Hence no functional of the observational marginal $Q$ alone can distinguish a realization with $\delta_\text{act}=0$ from one with maximal $\delta_\text{act}$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Construction]
Define two PMFs on $S \times T \times A$:
\begin{align*}
  P_0(s,t,a) &= \mathbf{1}[s = s_0] \cdot Q(t,a) \quad \text{(Dirac state: $S$ constant, no residual decision relevance)}, \\
  P_1(s,t,a) &= \mathbf{1}[s = \phi(a)] \cdot Q(t,a) \quad \text{(state copies action via injection $\phi : A \hookrightarrow S$)}.
\end{align*}
For (i): $\sum_s P_0(s,t,a) = Q(t,a)$ and $\sum_s P_1(s,t,a) = Q(t,a)$ by construction---both project to the same $Q$. For (ii): In $P_0$, $S = s_0$ deterministically, so $I_{P_0}(S; A \mid T) = 0$. In $P_1$, $S = \phi(A)$, so $A$ is determined by $S$ given any $T$, giving $I_{P_1}(S; A \mid T) = H_Q(A \mid T)$. The cardinality condition $|S| \geq |A|$ guarantees the injection $\phi$ exists.
\end{proof}

\section{Gap-Closing Certificates}
\label{sec:gap-closers}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

By the data processing inequality, $\delta_\text{act} \leq \varepsilon_\text{state}$. The two certificates inherit this ordering: if $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB} = 0$ then $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB} = 0$; the converse does not hold---$\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB} = 0$ is consistent with large $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ (dormant unrecoverable state). Each certificate requires a specific access mode: structural (deployment topology, sufficient for $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ via \S\ref{sec:static-cert}), controlled replay (re-execution under wild/replay conditions), or proxy/intervention (read $Z_t=f(S_t)$ or perturb a hidden module, supporting conditional-DPI lower bounds via \S\ref{sec:dynamic-cert}). The empirical core uses these access modes in a controlled ReAct agent.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Structural-Access Closer: Static Upper Bound via Untraced-Channel Capacity}
\label{sec:static-cert}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The structural-access closer upper-bounds $\varepsilon_\text{state} = H(S_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ from deployment topology. Its value depends on \emph{which components the auditor trusts}.

\paragraph{Macro-Graph and Trust Partition.}

The deployment is represented at two levels. At the coarse level, a \emph{macro-graph} partitions components into Trusted ($\mathcal{T}$, protocol-budgeted via interface specs), Untrusted ($\mathcal{U}$, adversarial-budgeted), and Suspect ($\mathcal{S}$, the audit target itself).
Each macro-edge expands into a time-unrolled micro-DAG $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$ as below. The trust classification of the source component determines which budget regime applies to every micro-edge in the expansion.
This partition is an auditor-side modeling input, not an intrinsic system label. The certificate therefore records the partition together with the topology and budgets: changing only $(\mathcal{T},\mathcal{U},\mathcal{S})$ requires recomputing $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$, and disputed classifications can be reported as a sensitivity range. This is the point of the trust axis: black-box evaluation leaves trust assumptions implicit, whereas the static certificate forces them into the audit artifact where they can be challenged.

\paragraph{Time-Unrolled Micro-Graph and Dual Budgets.}

Let $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$ be the time-unrolled DAG of the deployment up to step $t$. As before, $U_t$ are unrecorded sources, $\tilde T_t$ the visible trace, $S_t$ the operative state, and $\mathcal{C}_\text{unlogged}$ all reachable cuts separating $U_t$ from $S_t$.

Each edge $e \in E_t$ carries a per-edge budget that depends on the trust status of its source component:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Protocol budget} $c_e^{\text{proto}}$: the maximum information flow under correct protocol execution. Read from interface specifications (e.g., $K \log |\mathcal{V}|$ for a $K$-token text channel; Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo} gives the full taxonomy).
  \item \textbf{Adversarial budget} $c_e^{\text{adv}} := \sup_{\mathcal{A} \in \mathfrak{A}(e)} I(X_e^{\mathcal{A}} \to Y_e \mid \tilde T_t)$, where $\mathfrak{A}(e)$ is the set of all strategies compatible with the channel's deployment format. By definition $c_e^{\text{adv}} \geq c_e^{\text{proto}}$, with equality only when the channel format constrains the adversary to protocol behavior.
\end{itemize}
Edges originating in $\mathcal{T}$ use $c_e^{\text{proto}}$; edges crossing into or originating in $\mathcal{U}$ use $c_e^{\text{adv}}$. The adversarial budget is defined over the set of strategies $\mathfrak{A}(e)$ compatible with the channel format; restricting $\mathfrak{A}$ yields a ladder: protocol-compliant (available when the component is organizationally trustable), format-compatible (default for untrusted components), and arbitrary (requiring deployment redesign). Cryptographic commitments can move a channel down the ladder even without organizational trust.

A set of edges is \emph{software-orthogonal} if, conditioned on $\tilde T_t$, the channel outputs factor: $p(\{y_e\}_{e \in E'} \mid \{x_e\}, \tilde T_t) = \prod_{e \in E'} p(y_e \mid x_e, \tilde T_t)$.

\begin{lemma}[Additive decomposition]
\label{lem:ortho-decomp}
Under software orthogonality, the induced cut capacity decomposes additively:
$C_\text{cut}(\Omega) \leq \sum_{e \in E(\Omega, \Omega^c)} c_e^{\text{budget}(e)}$,
where $\text{budget}(e) = \text{proto}$ for Trusted edges and $\text{adv}$ for Untrusted edges.
\end{lemma}

When software orthogonality fails, superadditivity of non-orthogonal Untrusted channels is possible (Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo} discusses remedies).

\begin{proposition}[Structural-Access Closer: Static Certificate via Cut-Set Bound]
\label{prop:static-ub}
For any cut $\Omega$ separating $U_t$ from $S_t$ with induced capacity $C_\text{cut}(\Omega) = \sup_{p(X_\Omega \mid \tilde T_t)} I(X_\Omega \to Y_{\Omega^c} \mid \tilde T_t, X_{\Omega^c})$,
\[
  H(S_t \mid \tilde T_t) \;\leq\; \underbrace{H(S_t \mid T_t)}_{\varepsilon_\text{nominal}} \;+\; \min_{\Omega} C_\text{cut}(\Omega).
\]
Setting $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB} := \varepsilon_\text{nominal} + \min_\Omega C_\text{cut}(\Omega)$ gives $\varepsilon_\text{state} \leq \varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$.
\end{proposition}

Here $X_\Omega$ denotes the input signals entering edges crossing the cut, $Y_{\Omega^c}$ the corresponding output signals, and $X_{\Omega^c}$ the boundary state on the sink side (formal definitions in Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo}).

\begin{corollary}[Additive form]
\label{cor:additive-ub}
Under software orthogonality, $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB} = \varepsilon_\text{nominal} + \min_{C \in \mathcal{C}_\text{unlogged}} \sum_{e \in C} c_e$, a discrete min-cut on the unlogged subgraph.
\end{corollary}

\emph{Proof sketch:} Chain-rule gives $H(S_t \mid \tilde T_t) = H(S_t \mid T_t) + I(S_t; M_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ where $M_t = T_t \setminus \tilde T_t$. Every path from $U_t$ to $S_t$ crosses a cut $\Omega$, so $M_t$ is $d$-separated from $S_t$ by the cross-cut signals; DPI yields $I(S_t; M_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq C_\text{cut}(\Omega)$. Minimizing over cuts and substituting Lemma~\ref{lem:ortho-decomp} completes the proof (full derivation in Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo}).

\begin{corollary}[Autoregressive zero-cut]
\label{cor:auto-exact}
If the system is a strict autoregressive core and $\tilde T_t$ includes the full context window, then $\mathcal{C}_\text{unlogged} = \varnothing$, every cut has zero induced capacity, and $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB} = 0$.
\end{corollary}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Gray-Box-Access Closer: Decision Relevance via Conditional DPI}
\label{sec:dynamic-cert}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

The gray-box-access closer targets $\delta_\text{act} := I(S_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$. Since $S_t$ is unobservable, we specify admissible probes whose measured variables satisfy $X_t \to S_t \to A_t$ given $\tilde T_t$. Conditional DPI supplies the correctness argument: $I(X_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq \delta_\text{act}$.

\begin{proposition}[Gray-Box-Access Closer: Probe Certificates from Conditional DPI]
\label{prop:dynamic-lb}
For any admissible probe variable $X_t$ with $X_t \to S_t \to A_t$ given $\tilde T_t$, the probe measurement $I(X_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ is a valid lower-bound certificate for $\delta_\text{act}$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Admissible probe: Replay.}
\label{sec:replay-cert}
Let $R_t \in \{\text{wild}, \text{replay}\}$ indicate whether a missing state fragment is reconstructed under the same visible trace $\tilde T_t$ (controlled re-execution, not paraphrase variation). Then $I(R_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) = \mathrm{JS}(P_\text{wild}, P_\text{replay} \mid \tilde T_t) \leq \delta_\text{act}$.  Discrete action spaces admit direct empirical JS estimation over either realized actions or model-reported action probabilities; the experiments below state which is used.

\paragraph{Admissible probe: Intervention.}
Let $\xi_\text{hidden}$ be an exogenous perturbation to a suspected unlogged module (cache, memory buffer, scratchpad). If $\xi_\text{hidden} \to S_t \to A_t$ given $\tilde T_t$, then $I(\xi_\text{hidden}; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq \delta_\text{act}$. Discrete action spaces admit direct conditional action-distribution shift estimates, such as JS divergence. For continuous action spaces, the same certificate targets $I(\xi_\text{hidden}; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ through variational lower-bound estimators such as InfoNCE~\citep{oord2018cpc} or MINE~\citep{belghazi2018mine}. Requires gray-box perturbation access; the most direct causal probe of the three.

\paragraph{Admissible probe: Proxy.}
Let $Z_t = f(S_t)$ be a readable coarsening of the operative state. Since $Z_t \leftarrow S_t \to A_t$ given $\tilde T_t$, conditional DPI yields $I(Z_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq \delta_\text{act}$, estimated via the cross-entropy gap of predictors with and without $Z_t$.

\paragraph{Continuous actions and estimator error.}
When $A_t$ is continuous, the proof obligation does not change---only the estimator does. The audit should report a conservative lower confidence bound on the variational estimate, e.g., $\max\{0,\hat I_\text{InfoNCE/MINE}-\mathrm{err}_{N,\mathcal{F}}\}$, where $\mathrm{err}_{N,\mathcal{F}}$ covers finite-sample uncertainty, critic-class approximation error, and optimization instability. Sample splitting, held-out critic evaluation, and negative controls play the same role here that bootstrap CIs and random-proxy controls play in the discrete experiments: they prevent estimator artifacts from being interpreted as certified decision relevance. The Lean artifact remains finite-discrete; continuous-action certificates are an empirical extension of Proposition~\ref{prop:dynamic-lb}, not a claim that the continuous MI estimators are mechanized.

\paragraph{Probe aggregation.}
A single certificate class can miss some causal paths.  Define $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB} := \max\{ I(R_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t), I(\xi_\text{hidden}; A_t \mid \tilde T_t), I(Z_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) \}$.  By monotonicity of the maximum, the aggregate remains a valid lower bound on $\delta_\text{act}$.

\paragraph{Indexed activation profiles.}
The scalar certificate is the maximum over an indexed family of admissible probes. Keeping the index gives an \emph{activation profile}
\[
  \Delta(j) := I(X_t^{(j)}; A_t \mid \tilde T_t),
\]
where $j$ may denote a scratchpad field, a denoising step, an activation layer, or a communication edge.  The static certificate identifies where residual capacity can reside; the activation profile identifies where that capacity is behaviorally used.  The experiments below keep this algebra fixed while changing the index: module in ReAct, time in the diffusion LM, and communication edge in the multi-agent setting.

\section{Empirical Instances Across the Gap}
\label{sec:empirical}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

Theorem~1 predicts a spectrum: two behaviorally equivalent realizations can occupy the endpoints $0$ and $H(A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ while producing identical observational marginals. We apply the framework to three deployments traversing the $\delta$-spectrum: ReAct under controlled replay (low $\delta$, dormant capacity), LLaDA denoising (mid $\delta$, late-binding capacity), and multi-agent private-report intervention (high $\delta$, near-saturated capacity). These experiments test the certificate framework's ability to distinguish behavioral regimes rather than directly constructing two realizations with identical $Q$ as in Theorem~1 (which would require controlled variation of hidden-state structure under fixed observable marginals); the gap theorem provides the theoretical guarantee that such a distinction is necessary, and the certificates operationalize it. The static min-cut (\S\ref{sec:exp-static}) provides a shared topology coordinate; \S\S\ref{sec:exp-intervention}--\ref{sec:multi-agent-private-report} traverse the spectrum. Read-only proxy and synthetic ground-truth calibrations are reported in Appendices~\ref{app:proxy-bias} and~\ref{app:synthetic}. All pipelines ran on a single M4 Max workstation.

\label{sec:exp-static}

\subsection{Dynamic Causal Certificates: Intervention and Controlled Replay}
\label{sec:exp-intervention}

This tests the gap directly: $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$ unchanged, $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$ varies with task---confirming the two axes remain orthogonal. We separate the two axes with a dormant/active task split under one topology. The unlogged scratchpad is present in both cases: irrelevant for calculator tasks, necessary for planning tasks. Intervention perturbs the scratchpad (masking or replacement); controlled replay removes it entirely while holding $\tilde T_t$ fixed. Table~\ref{tab:dynamic-certs} reports JS divergence; for the headline replay contrast, the argmax tool counts are unchanged, so the evidence is a policy-probability shift rather than a realized-tool flip.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\textbf{The two-evidence-types frame.} Existing audit frameworks collapse hiddenness into a scalar; our results require two structurally independent kinds of evidence (topology, behavior) and a third axis (trust) for action. This decomposition makes the gap closable rather than merely diagnosable.

\textbf{Limitations.} The fixed-deployment-marginal scope of Definition~\ref{def:behavioral-equiv} applies; distribution shift under red-teaming or deployment drift is not covered. The structural-access closer (Proposition~1) requires software orthogonality for tightness of the per-edge additive decomposition; shared hidden state across unlogged channels weakens the min-cut interpretation without invalidating the upper bound (Appendix~\ref{app:netinfo}). The gray-box-access closer (Proposition~2) requires the probe to satisfy the conditional Markov chain $X_t \to S_t \to A_t$ given $\tilde T_t$; probe misspecification can underestimate $\delta_\text{act}$.

\textbf{Trust boundaries in practice.} The trust partition (\S\ref{sec:static-cert}) is an auditor-side input: varying only the trust assumption---without changing topology or dynamic data---can shift the audit strategy from detection to mandatory redesign. This does not remove judgment from the audit; it makes the judgment inspectable. A certificate should therefore state the trust partition, the anchor for each Trusted classification, and the fallback Untrusted recomputation when the anchor is disputed. This affects AI supply-chain auditing: when a deployment integrates a third-party model, API, or retrieval module, the auditor must decide whether to treat that component as Trusted (protocol-budgeted, audited via $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$) or Untrusted (adversarial-budgeted, requiring topology-level mitigation). The default-deny principle applies: absent cryptographic or organizational trust anchors, components should be classified as Untrusted to avoid systematic underestimation of $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB}$. Thus the framework does not pretend trust classification is objective; it improves on pure black-box auditing by forcing the assumption into a named, recomputable input.

\textbf{Future Directions.} Future work should lift the finite-support assumption to continuous state spaces, develop sharper error analyses for InfoNCE/MINE-style continuous-action certificates, automate dynamic-certificate probe synthesis via active probe selection rather than hand design, and connect the Lean artifact to a verified audit toolchain that emits machine-checked certificates per deployment. Natural Language Autoencoders (NLAs)~\citep{frasertaliente2026nla} could supply readable proxy probes for Proposition~\ref{prop:dynamic-lb}, but only when their generated descriptions satisfy the same conditional-Markov requirement; failures would instantiate the probe-misspecification limitation above.

\section{Network Information Theory Background for Proposition~\ref{prop:static-ub}}
\label{app:netinfo}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{Conditional channel capacity.}
For each unlogged edge $e$ in the time-unrolled DAG $G_t$, the conditional capacity $c_e$ is the maximum information that can flow through $e$ under the worst-case distribution consistent with the system protocol. It is a structural upper bound read from interface specifications: token window length, hidden-dimension size, quantization level, or discrete-state cardinality. A text channel with budget $K$ tokens over vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$ has $c_e \leq K \log |\mathcal{V}|$; a vector state of dimension $d$ at quantization $Q$ has $c_e \leq d \log Q$; a discrete scheduler with $|\Omega|$ states has $c_e \leq \log |\Omega|$. The certificate remains valid under any tighter $c_e^\star \geq I(X_e; Y_e \mid \tilde T_t)$, so auditors who can measure the empirical input distribution to a particular channel may substitute a tighter bound.

\paragraph{Cut-set upper bound and additive decomposition.}
Let $G_t = (V_t, E_t)$ be the time-unrolled DAG. For a cut $\Omega \subseteq V_t$ separating source nodes from a target, the \emph{induced cut capacity} $C_\text{cut}(\Omega)$ is the supremum over joint input distributions $p(X_\Omega \mid \tilde T_t)$ of the directed conditional mutual information $I(X_\Omega \to Y_{\Omega^c} \mid \tilde T_t, X_{\Omega^c})$. The cut-set upper bound~\citep{elgamal2011network} states that any information flowing from sources in $\Omega$ to a target in $\Omega^c$ is bounded above by $C_\text{cut}(\Omega)$; minimizing over cuts gives the tightest such bound. Proposition~\ref{prop:static-ub} applies this bound to the unrecorded trace $M_t$. We use only the upper-bound direction; the network-coding achievability results~\citep{ahlswede2000network} are not invoked. Under software orthogonality, Lemma~\ref{lem:ortho-decomp} shows $C_\text{cut}(\Omega) \leq \sum_{e \in E(\Omega, \Omega^c)} c_e$, reducing the certificate to a discrete min-cut problem on $G_t$ (the form used in experiments).

\paragraph{Proof sketch of Proposition~\ref{prop:static-ub}.}
The argument has two steps. (1)~Trace-gap identity: let $M_t := T_t \setminus \tilde T_t$; then $H(S_t \mid \tilde T_t) = H(S_t \mid T_t) + I(S_t; M_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ by the chain rule, where $H(S_t \mid T_t) = \varepsilon_\text{nominal}$. (2)~Cut-set bound: $M_t$ is $d$-separated from $S_t$ by the cross-cut signals $(X_\Omega \to Y_{\Omega^c},\, X_{\Omega^c})$ conditional on $\tilde T_t$, so $I(S_t; M_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq C_\text{cut}(\Omega)$ for every cut $\Omega \in \mathrm{Cuts}(U_t \to S_t)$. Minimizing over cuts and combining with step (1) yields the proposition. Corollary~\ref{cor:additive-ub} follows by substituting Lemma~\ref{lem:ortho-decomp}. The full derivation is mechanized axiom-free in the Lean~4 artifact (Appendix~\ref{app:lean-boundary}).

\section{Experimental Protocols}
\label{app:methodology}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Static Certificate Computation Protocol}

Inputs: deployment architecture specification, logging manifest, and protocol budgets (token window sizes, hidden dimensions, quantization levels). Procedure: (1)~construct the time-unrolled DAG $G_t$; (2)~partition edges into logged and unlogged; (3)~verify software orthogonality (Lemma~\ref{lem:ortho-decomp}) or add shared-state edges explicitly until orthogonality holds; (4)~assign capacity $c_e$ per unlogged edge (\S\ref{sec:static-cert}); (5)~enumerate reachable cut sets $\mathcal{C}_\text{unlogged}$ via min-cut after collapsing logged edges to zero residual capacity; (6)~compute $\varepsilon_\text{state}^\text{UB} = \varepsilon_\text{nominal} + \min_C \sum_{e \in C} c_e$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:additive-ub}). For the logging ablation, repeat (2)--(6) for the ordered manifests in \S\ref{sec:exp-static}. See \texttt{experiments/7.1\_static\_certificate/README.md} for implementation.

\subsection{Dynamic Certificate Estimation Protocols}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Proxy certificate.} Run $N$ trajectories recording $(\tilde T_t, Z_t^{(k)}, A_t)$; train cross-fitted predictors $p_\theta(A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ and $p_\phi(A_t \mid \tilde T_t, Z_t^{(k)})$; compute $\hat I_k = L_\text{trace} - L_{\text{trace}+Z^{(k)}}$; estimate trajectory-block bootstrap CIs. Repeat for random, label-permuted, and PCA-projected probes.
  \item \textbf{Controlled replay certificate.} For each $\tilde T_t$, run the same system under wild and controlled replayed missing-state conditions; estimate $\hat\Delta = \hat{\mathrm{JS}}(P_\text{wild}, P_\text{replay} \mid \tilde T_t)$; aggregate over trace slices. Valid only under the replay condition in \S\ref{sec:replay-cert}.
  \item \textbf{Intervention certificate.} Inject $\xi_\text{hidden}$ at perturbation levels $\{0, \sigma_1, \ldots\}$ while holding $\tilde T_t$ fixed; estimate $\hat I(\xi_\text{hidden}; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ via conditional action-distribution shift (discrete actions) or InfoNCE/MINE (continuous). Repeat on dormant and active tasks under the same topology.
  \item \textbf{Multi-agent private-report certificate.} Sample $N/2$ arithmetic and $N/2$ factual-retrieval tasks; generate private evidence reports under instructions forbidding direct API names; sample the controller action distribution $k$ times under wild, neutral replay, opposite-class report, same-class shuffled report, and oracle-tag conditions; parse actions into $\{\texttt{calculator}, \texttt{search}, \texttt{invalid}\}$; compute per-task JS divergence with task-block bootstrap CIs. The main contrast is the opposite-class worker-report intervention.
\end{enumerate}

See the README files for experiments 7.2, 7.3, and 7.6 for implementation details.

The dormant/active split is an estimator-noise diagnostic: on calculator tasks (dormant), $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB} = 0$ trivially; on planning tasks (active), the proxy point estimate is positive but its CI straddles zero. The CE-diff estimator $\hat I = L(\tilde T_t) - L(\tilde T_t, Z_t)$ lower-bounds $I(Z_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$. Three bias sources and two variance sources matter in practice.

\paragraph{Bias sources.}
(1)~\emph{Proxy coarsening} — $Z_t = f(S_t)$ discards information in $S_t$ not captured by $f$, so $I(Z_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t) \leq I(S_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$. (2)~\emph{Restricted predictor class} — logistic regression may not reach the Bayes-optimal predictor. (3)~\emph{Finite-sample bias} — cross-entropy on held-out folds is upward-biased for the population CE; cross-fitting mitigates but does not eliminate this. Positive bias (underestimation) is safe for the audit since it never overstates $\delta_\text{act}$.

\paragraph{Variance sources.}
(1)~\emph{Trajectory sampling} — finite $N$ from the deployment distribution; block bootstrap estimates the resulting CI. (2)~\emph{Proxy dimension} — higher-dimensional proxies carry more information but increase predictor variance; Table~\ref{tab:proxy-results} shows $d{=}5$ outperforming both $d{=}3$ and the full 10-dim representation.

\paragraph{Sampling noise and temperature.}
Increasing decoding temperature can raise $H(A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$, but independent sampler noise is not a certificate amplifier — it changes the audited policy and can reduce $I(Z_t; A_t \mid \tilde T_t)$ when the added variation is unrelated to the hidden state. We do not use temperature injection for the main certificate; the synthetic experiment (\S\ref{app:synthetic}) instead injects hidden-state stochasticity with known ground truth.

\textbf{Foundational objects.} A \texttt{FinitePMF} is a Lean structure with exactly three fields: a mass function \texttt{pmf} of type $\alpha \to \mathbb{R}$, a proof \texttt{pmf\_nonneg} of $\forall x, 0 \leq \mathrm{pmf}(x)$, and a proof \texttt{sum\_one} of $\sum_x \mathrm{pmf}(x) = 1$. Every theorem quantifies over finite index types through the Lean typeclass assumptions \texttt{Fintype} and \texttt{DecidableEq}, so marginalization, entropy, KL, and conditional mutual information are all explicit \texttt{Finset.univ} sums. No measure-space, sigma-algebra, countable-additivity, or continuity axiom is imported for probability.

\textbf{Real logarithms and zero mass.} The artifact uses Mathlib's \texttt{Real.log} on $\mathbb{R}$ rather than postulating a separate logarithm. Base-2 quantities are defined by division by \texttt{Real.log 2}, and the positivity/nonzero facts for \texttt{Real.log 2} are proved locally from Mathlib theorems. Mathlib makes \texttt{Real.log 0 = 0}; the entropy summand \texttt{negMulLog2 p} is therefore a total real-valued function with the zero-mass term evaluating to zero. Proofs that require logarithm algebra case-split on zero mass and establish strict positivity before using identities such as \texttt{Real.log\_div}. Thus divisions by zero and support conditions are handled inside the Lean statements, not hidden as informal side assumptions.

\textbf{Entropy.} \texttt{negMulLog2} and \texttt{entropyOf}/\texttt{entropy} define Shannon entropy via $-p\log_2 p$ over finite types. \texttt{negMulLog2\_nonneg} and \texttt{entropy\_nonneg} establish $-p\log_2 p \ge 0$ for $p\in[0,1]$; \texttt{entropy\_le\_log\_card} gives $H(P) \le \log_2|\alpha|$ via KL nonnegativity.

\textbf{KL divergence and DPI.} \texttt{kl\_nonneg} proves $\sum p_x \log(p_x/q_x) \ge 0$ via $\log x \le x-1$ with case-splitting on zero mass. \texttt{condMarkov} defines $X \to Y \to Z \mid W$ via PMF factorization, and \texttt{cond\_dpi} proves the conditional DPI $I(X;Z\!\mid\!W) \le I(Y;Z\!\mid\!W)$ via KL nonnegativity, chain-rule identities, and tuple reindexing.

\textbf{Type equivalence.} \texttt{FinitePMF.comapEquiv}, \texttt{entropyOf\_equiv\_eq}, and \texttt{entropyOf\_mul\_log2} establish entropy invariance under type isomorphism and the $\cdot \ln 2$ conversion.

Supporting modules: \texttt{IdentifiabilityGap} (Theorem~1), \texttt{DualCertificate} (Propositions~1--2), \texttt{QuantizedBound} (discrete-to-analog bridge $H(P) \le d\log_2 Q$), \texttt{PACBounds} (Gaussian-KL and Fano wrapped as explicit assumption structures), \texttt{Screenability} (Corollary~2), and \texttt{ScreenabilityBridge}/\texttt{Tools} (compatibility and utilities).
