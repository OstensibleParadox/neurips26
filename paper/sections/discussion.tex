  \section{Discussion}
  \label{sec:discussion}
  % ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

  \subsection{One Plot: Capacity, Then Activation}

  The three real-system experiments follow a single logic. Capacity maps locate
  where hidden capacity can reside---scratchpad modules, denoising interfaces,
  peer-state edges. Dynamic certificates identify which of those sites affect
  actions. By Corollary~\ref{cor:action-mediated-unrec}, every
  positive dynamic certificate is also a lower bound on transcript
  non-recoverability, but only through the action channel. The same scratchpad
  is dormant on calculator tasks and active on planning tasks in ReAct; in
  LLaDA, action relevance concentrates at the final binding step; in the
  multi-agent deployment, delegation makes the private worker report the active
  edge.

  Effect sizes index deployment topology. Under the same ReAct topology,
  calculator tasks leave the scratchpad dormant while planning tasks produce
  nonzero realized-action shifts under intervention
  ($\ReactInterventionMaskBits$ bits, CI $\ReactInterventionMaskCI$) and a
  softer policy-readout shift under controlled replay ($\ReactReplayBits$ bits,
  CI $\ReactReplayCI$). The LLaDA final binding step rises to
  $\LladaFinalBits$ bits; the multi-agent
  counterfactual report edge rises to $\MultiAgentCounterfactualBits$ bits. A
  hidden channel that supplements an already-determined visible trace has low
  $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$; one that carries the decision has high
  $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$. Under this protocol, a claim that an unobserved
  variable causally affects behavior must identify a structural channel in the
  capacity map and a lower-bound probe in
  $\delta_\text{act}^\text{LB}$. A stronger claim of state-only
  non-recoverability requires an independent witness
  $\varepsilon_\text{unrec}^\text{LB}$, which we demonstrate in the closed-form
  laboratories and synthetic ground-truth calibration rather than in the
  black-box real-system diagnostics.
